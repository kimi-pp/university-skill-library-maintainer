# Chapter 10: Theories of Fixed and Circulating Capital — The Physiocrats and Adam Smith

## Core Idea
Quesnay, the leading Physiocrat, was the first to systematically distinguish between fixed and circulating capital, calling them avances primitives (original advances) and avances annuelles (annual advances). He correctly identified this as a distinction within productive capital engaged in production, particularly for agricultural capital. Adam Smith generalized these categories but created confusion by mixing distinctions within productive capital with distinctions between productive capital and capital in circulation (commodity-capital and money-capital).

## Frameworks Introduced

### Physiocratic Contribution (Quesnay)

#### Avances Primitives vs. Avances Annuelles
- **Quesnay's distinction**: avances primitives (fixed capital) and avances annuelles (circulating capital)
- **Scope**: Distinction exists within productive capital, capital directly engaged in production process
- **Focus**: Primarily applied to agricultural capital (farmer's capital) as only truly productive capital
- **Turnover periods**: Annual turnover for avances annuelles; more than annual (decennial) for avances primitives
- **Generalization**: Later applied to other capitals and industrial capital in general

#### Correct Analysis
- **Location**: Difference arises only after advanced money transformed into productive capital elements
- **Within productive capital**: Distinction exists solely within productive capital
- **Exclusion of money**: Never occurs to Quesnay to classify money among original or annual advances
- **Contrast**: Both advances stand opposed to money and market commodities as productive capital
- **Value circulation basis**: Difference reduced to different manner of entering finished product value
- **Replacement difference**: Annual advances wholly replaced annually; primitives partly and at longer intervals
- **Circulation manner**: Different manner of value circulation and replacement/reproduction

#### Adam Smith's Contribution and Confusion

##### Generalisation
- **Progress**: Smith generalized physiocratic categories beyond agriculture to all productive capital
- **Result**: avances annuelles → circulating capital; avances primitives → fixed capital
- **Limitation**: Progress confined to generalization; implementation far inferior to Quesnay

##### Empirical Approach Problems
- **Crude empiricism**: Broaches investigation in crudely empirical manner, creating lack of clarity from outset
- **Capital functions**: Ways value may be invested to perform capital functions and yield surplus-value are as varied as investment spheres
- **Investment spheres**: Different branches of production where capital may be invested
- **Non-productive capital**: Includes ways value functions as capital without being invested as productive capital (interest-bearing capital, merchant's capital)
- **Subject abandonment**: Moves away from real subject: how division of productive capital into different elements affects turnover

##### Smith's Definition
- **Two ways capital yields profit**: Smith's starting point — ways capital may be employed to yield revenue or profit
- **First way**: "Employed in raising, manufacturing, or purchasing goods, and selling them again with a profit" — agriculture, manufacture, commerce
- **Problem**: Speaks of different investment spheres, not distinctions within productive capital
- **Abandonment**: Abandons physiocratic foundation based on distinctions within productive capital affecting turnover

##### Merchants' Capital Confusion
- **Circulation capital**: Capital must be sold after transformation into goods; must pass from seller to buyer, change from commodity to money form
- **Useless in possession**: Capital of no use while in seller's possession or same shape
- **Smith's statement**: "The capital employed in this manner yields no revenue or profit to its employer, while it either remains in his possession or continues in the same shape"
- **Contradiction**: But Smith later says capital employed in agriculture/industry divides into fixed and circulating
- **Interpretation**: Capital employed to produce goods must be sold; transformation into goods and sale changes from productive capital form to commodity-capital/money-capital forms

##### Circulating Capital as Capital of Circulation
- **Smith's definition**: Goods yield no revenue until sold for money; money yields nothing until exchanged for goods; capital continually going from owner in one shape and returning in another
- **Adam Smith's term**: "Such capitals therefore may very properly be called circulating capitals"
- **Marx's correction**: This is not circulating capital but capital of circulation — capital in form pertinent to circulation process (commodity-capital and money-capital)
- **Form distinction**: Not different kinds of industrial capital but different forms assumed by same capital-value during its life

##### Smith's Fundamental Confusion
- **Mixing categories**: Confuses productive capital distinctions with circulation sphere form distinctions
- **Value circulation**: Capital-value advanced in fixed capital circulates via product just like circulating capital; both converted into money-capital by commodity-capital circulation
- **Replacement difference**: Fixed capital value circulates piece-meal and must be replaced piece-meal; circulating capital value circulates all at once
- **Body form reproduction**: Fixed capital must be reproduced in bodily form at intervals

##### Illustration Problem
- **Merchant's capital example**: Smith uses merchant's capital (not in production process at all) to illustrate circulating capital
- **Absurdity**: Starting with capital that doesn't function as productive capital at all
- **Contradiction**: Merchant's capital is "altogether a circulating capital" but later claims circulating/fixed distinction evolves from productive capital differences
- **Confusion source**: Has physiocratic distinction in mind but mixes with capital-value forms in circuit

##### Fixed Capital Definition
- **Smith's definition**: Capital employed in land improvement, useful machines, instruments of trade — things yielding revenue without changing masters or circulating further
- **Proportions vary**: Different occupations require very different fixed/circulating capital proportions
- **Artisan example**: Some part of every master artificer's capital fixed in trade instruments; very small in some (tailors, shoemakers, weavers), very great in others
- **Majority circulating**: For most artificers, greater part circulates in wages or material prices, repaid with profit by work price

##### Machine Manufacturer Problem
- **Contradiction exposure**: To machine manufacturer, machine is product circulating as commodity-capital; according to Smith's definition, not fixed capital
- **Confusion cause**: Smith mixes distinction from productive capital element circulation with differences in capital forms (productive vs. circulation capital)
- **Functional ambiguity**: With Smith, things function as fixed capital (instruments of labour) or circulating capital (commodity-capital) depending on position in capital's life-process

## Key Concepts
- **Physiocrats**: French economic school emphasizing agriculture as source of wealth
- **Avances Primitives**: Quesnay's term for fixed capital (original/advance investments)
- **Avances Annuelles**: Quesnay's term for circulating capital (annual advances)
- **Adam Smith's Generalisation**: Application of categories to all productive capital
- **Productive Capital**: Capital directly engaged in production process
- **Capital of Circulation**: Capital in commodity-capital and money-capital forms
- **Merchant's Capital**: Capital functioning only in circulation sphere
- **Circulation vs. Production**: Distinction between capital in circulation and in production
- **Value Circulation**: Movement of value through different capital forms
- **Replacement Cycles**: How different capital elements are replaced over time

## Key Takeaways
1. Quesnay was first to systematically distinguish fixed from circulating capital as avances primitives vs. avances annuelles
2. Quesnay correctly located distinction within productive capital engaged in production
3. Adam Smith generalized categories from agriculture to all productive capital
4. Smith created confusion by mixing productive capital distinctions with circulation form distinctions
5. Smith incorrectly used merchant's capital (pure circulation capital) to illustrate circulating capital concept
6. Fixed capital value circulates piece-meal through product sales; circulating capital value circulates all at once
7. Both fixed and circulating capital values ultimately convert to money-capital through commodity circulation
8. Smith's empirical approach lacked clarity and mixed different levels of analysis
9. The key distinction is within productive capital, not between productive capital and capital in circulation
10. Marx's correction: Smith's "circulating capital" often refers to capital of circulation (commodity/money capital forms)
