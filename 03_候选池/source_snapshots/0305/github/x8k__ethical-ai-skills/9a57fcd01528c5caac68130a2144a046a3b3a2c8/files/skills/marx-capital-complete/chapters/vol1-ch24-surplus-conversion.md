# Chapter 24: Conversion of Surplus-Value into Capital

## Core Idea
Accumulation of capital is the employment of surplus-value as capital, reconverting it into capital to expand production. This chapter explains how surplus-value (which appears as money) is transformed back into capital, enabling production on an increasing scale and reproducing capitalist relations.

## Frameworks Introduced

### The Accumulation Process
- **Definition**: "Employing surplus-value as capital, reconverting it into capital, is called accumulation of capital"
- **Individual capitalist**: Surplus-value is used to purchase additional means of production and labour-power
- **Expansion**: Accumulation enables production on a progressively increasing scale
- **Continuity**: The surplus-value of one period becomes capital in the next

### The Capital Circuit and Accumulation
- **Original form**: Capital advanced in money form (M)
- **Surplus-value form**: Originally appears as value of portion of gross product
- **Realization**: Surplus-value realized through sale, converted into money
- **Reconversion**: Both capital value and surplus-value (now both money) are laid out to purchase commodities
- **Result**: Production begins afresh on an extended scale

### The Annual Product and Circulation
- **Total annual product**: Sum of all individual capitals converted into commodities during the year
- **Market transactions**: Individual capitalists bring their products to market
- **Interchange**: Market transactions only transfer individual components of annual product
- **Limitation**: Circulation cannot augment total annual production or alter nature of objects
- **Composition matters**: Use of total annual product depends on its composition, not circulation

### Surplus-Value as Capital
- **Transformation**: Surplus-value must be converted into capital to serve accumulation
- **Purpose**: Enables capitalist to expand production scale
- **Mechanism**: Surplus-value used to purchase additional means of production (4/5) and labour-power (1/5)
- **Effect**: New capital brings in additional surplus-value in its turn

### The Invisibility of Surplus-Value
- **Money form**: Once surplus-value is converted to money, its origin is invisible
- **Appropriation**: "We can neither see nor smell in this sum of money a trace of surplus-value"
- **Implication**: The capitalist appears as the owner of capital, not as the appropriator of surplus-value
- **Property law**: Capitalist property in surplus-value appears as natural right, not as product of exploitation

### Accumulation and Capitalist Identity
- **Capitalist function**: "It is only because his money constantly functions as capital that the economic guise of a capitalist attaches to a man"
- **Continuous process**: Accumulation maintains and reproduces the capitalist as capitalist
- **Class reproduction**: Accumulation reproduces capitalist class relations
- **Historical development**: Accumulation drives the development of capitalist production

## Key Concepts
- **Accumulation of Capital**: Employment of surplus-value as capital
- **Surplus-Value Conversion**: Transformation of surplus-value from commodity form to money to capital
- **Capital Circuit**: M → C → ... → P → ... → C' → M' → M'' (expanded reproduction)
- **Annual Product**: Total commodities produced by society's capital in a year
- **Circulation**: Transfer of commodities between capitalists, does not create value
- **Capital Composition**: Division between constant and variable capital in accumulation
- **Property Laws**: Legal forms that legitimize capitalist appropriation of surplus-value

## Key Takeaways
1. Accumulation is the conversion of surplus-value into capital for expanded production
2. Surplus-value must be reconverted into capital to serve accumulation
3. The capitalist appears as owner of capital, but is actually the appropriator of surplus-value
4. Accumulation maintains and reproduces capitalist class relations
5. Market circulation transfers but does not create value; composition of annual product determines its use
6. Accumulation enables production on a progressively increasing scale
