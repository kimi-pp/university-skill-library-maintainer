# Chapter 30: Reaction of the Agricultural Revolution on Industry. Creation of the Home Market for Industrial Capital

## Core Idea
The expropriation and expulsion of the agricultural population created not only the proletariat but also the home market for industrial capital. The destruction of rural domestic industry and the separation of manufacture from agriculture transformed raw materials and means of subsistence into commodities, creating a vast internal market that industrial capital could exploit. Modern industry, through machinery, finally completed this separation and conquered the entire home market for industrial capital.

## Frameworks Introduced

### The Home Market Creation Mechanism
- **Dual process**: Expropriation created both wage labourers and the market for their consumption
- **Supply for industry**: "The expropriation and eviction of a part of the agricultural population not only set free for industrial capital, the labourers, their means of subsistence, and material for labour"
- **Market transformation**: Means of subsistence and raw materials transformed from use-values into commodities
- **Concentration effect**: Scattered customers of artisans concentrated into "one great market provided for by industrial capital"
- **Historical observation**: A. Anderson saw this as "direct intervention of Providence" in his History of Commerce

### The Transformation of Rural Domestic Industry
- **Before**: Peasant family produced means of subsistence and raw materials for their own consumption
- **Yarn, linen, woollen stuffs**: Things whose raw materials were within reach of every peasant family were spun and woven by it for its own use
- **After**: These became articles of manufacture; country districts served as markets
- **Separation**: Process of separation between manufacture and agriculture
- **Requirements**: "Only the destruction of rural domestic industry can give the internal market of a country that extension and consistence which the capitalist mode of production requires"

### Manufacturing Period Limitations
- **Partial conquest**: Manufacture properly so-called conquers only partially the domain of national production
- **Foundation**: Always rests on handicrafts of town and domestic industry of rural districts as ultimate basis
- **Contradictory process**: If it destroys domestic industry in one form, it calls them up elsewhere because it needs them for raw material preparation
- **New class**: Creates small villagers who cultivate soil as accessory calling while chiefly occupied in industrial labour
- **Direct sales**: Products sold directly to manufacturers or through merchants

### The Fluctuating Peasantry (15th-18th Century)
- **Historical puzzle**: From last third of 15th century, continual complaints about capitalist farming encroachment and peasantry destruction
- **Persistent appearance**: Peasantry keeps "turning up again, although in diminished number, and always under worse conditions"
- **Explanation**: England alternately cultivated corn or bred cattle; peasant cultivation fluctuated with these cycles
- **Modern industry's role**: Only modern industry supplies the lasting basis through machinery
- **Radical expropriation**: "Expropriates radically the enormous majority of the agricultural population, and completes the separation between agriculture and rural domestic industry"

### The Social Transformation of Raw Materials
- **Flax example**: Westphalian peasants under Frederick II all span flax
- **Forcible expropriation**: Peasants hunted from soil; others turned into day labourers
- **Manufacture emergence**: Large establishments for flax-spinning and weaving arise
- **Same material, new form**: "The flax looks exactly as before. Not a fibre of it is changed, but a new social soul has popped into its body"
- **Capital transformation**: Now forms part of constant capital of master manufacturer

### The Concentration of Production
- **Before**: Raw materials divided among numerous small producers who cultivated and spun them in retail fashion
- **After**: Concentrated in hand of one capitalist who sets others to spin and weave for him
- **Labour concentration**: Spindles and looms formerly scattered now crowded in few great labour-barracks
- **Means of production**: Transformed from means of independent existence into means for commanding workers and extracting unpaid labour
- **Hidden origins**: Large manufactories and farms originated from "throwing into one of many small centres of production, built up by expropriation of many small independent producers"
- **Popular intuition**: Mirabeau called them "manufactures réunies" (workshops thrown into one), like "fields thrown into one"

### Mirabeau's Analysis of Manufacturing Forms
- **Manufactures réunies**: Large manufactories with hundreds of men under a director
- **Neglect**: These concentrated forms received most attention but were not the most important
- **Manufactures séparées**: Discrete workshops where labourers work separately and on their own account
- **Economic comparison**: Large workshops enrich one or two entrepreneurs; labourers are only journeymen with no share in success
- **Discrete workshop advantages**: No one becomes rich, but many labourers are comfortable; saving and industrious can amass capital
- **Social benefit**: Workers see good conduct and activity as means of bettering their condition, not just obtaining wage increases
- **Mirabeau's conclusion**: Discrete workshops "make a really important object of national prosperity"

### Urquhart's Critique of Commercial System
- **Wool example**: Twenty pounds of wool unobtrusively converted into yearly clothing by peasant family industry
- **Hidden value**: This makes no show but supports family needs directly
- **Commercial transformation**: Bring to market, send to factory, then to broker, then to dealer
- **Capital multiplication**: "Great commercial operations, and nominal capital engaged to the amount of twenty times its value"
- **Exploitation**: Working class "emersed to support a wretched factory population, a parastical shop-keeping class, and a fictitious commercial, monetary, and financial system"
- **Agricultural wisdom**: "The plough, the yoke, were 'the invention of gods, and the occupation of heroes'; are the loom, the spindle, the distaff, of less noble parentage"
- **Warning**: "You sever the distaff and the plough, the spindle and the yoke, and you get factories and poor-houses, credit and panics, two hostile nations, agriculture and commercial"

### Carey's Critique of England's Colonial Policy
- **Accusation**: England tried to turn every other country into mere agricultural nation whose manufacturer is England
- **Turkey example**: Carey claims Turkey ruined because landowners never permitted to strengthen themselves by forming natural alliance between plough and loom
- **Irony**: Carey was a great Russophile who wanted to prevent separation through protectionist system — the very system that accelerates it
- **Historical note**: Urquhart himself was agent in Turkey's ruin through Free-trade propaganda in English interest

### The Liberal Hypocrisy
- **Philanthropic economists**: Mill, Rogers, Goldwin Smith, Fawcett ask English landed proprietors "Where are our thousands of freeholders gone?"
- **Biblical reference**: "As God asked Cain after Abel"
- **Counter-question**: "But where do you come from, then? From the destruction of those freeholders"
- **Extended critique**: "Why don't you ask further, where are the independent weavers, spinners, and artisans gone?"

## Key Concepts
- **Home Market**: Internal market created by separation of producers from means of production
- **Rural Domestic Industry**: Peasant production of goods for own consumption
- **Manufactures Réunies**: Concentrated large manufactories (Mirabeau's term)
- **Manufactures Séparées**: Discrete workshops with independent labourers
- **Separation of Agriculture and Manufacture**: Historical process dividing production into separate spheres
- **Concentration of Capital**: Gathering of scattered means of production into capitalist hands
- **Social Soul**: Marx's metaphor for how commodity form transforms the social character of products
- **Primitive Accumulation**: Historical process creating preconditions for capitalism

## Key Takeaways
1. Expropriation created both the proletariat and the home market for industrial capital
2. The destruction of rural domestic industry was essential for creating the internal market
3. Raw materials and means of subsistence were transformed from use-values into commodities
4. Manufacturing period only partially achieved the separation of manufacture from agriculture
5. The peasantry persisted in diminished form until modern industry completed their radical expropriation
6. Machinery provided the lasting basis for capitalist agriculture and completed the separation
7. Modern industry finally conquered the entire home market for industrial capital
8. The concentration of production hid its origins in the expropriation of small independent producers
9. Mirabeau distinguished between concentrated manufactures (manufactures réunies) and discrete workshops (manufactures séparées)
10. Critics like Urquhart and Carey exposed the exploitative nature of the emerging capitalist commercial system
