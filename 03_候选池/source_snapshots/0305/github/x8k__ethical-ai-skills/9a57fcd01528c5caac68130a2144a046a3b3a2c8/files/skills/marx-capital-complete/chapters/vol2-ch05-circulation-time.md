# Chapter 5: The Time of Circulation

## Core Idea
The movement of capital through the sphere of production and the two phases of the sphere of circulation takes place in a series of time periods. The time in production is the production time; the time in circulation is the circulation time. The total circuit time equals the sum of production time and circulation time. However, production time itself can exceed labour-time due to pauses, interruptions, and natural processes, creating latent capital that doesn't expand value.

## Frameworks Introduced

### Capital Circuit Time Components
- **Production time**: Time capital spends in sphere of production
- **Circulation time**: Time capital spends in sphere of circulation
- **Total circuit time**: Production time + Circulation time
- **Mutual exclusion**: Time of circulation and time of production exclude each other; capital cannot be in both simultaneously

### Production Time Composition
- **Labour-process time**: Period during which labour-process occurs
- **Instrument idle time**: Periods when labour-process interrupted (e.g., night) but instruments remain at production site
- **Three components of means of production time**:
  1. Time during which they function as means of production (serve in productive process)
  2. Stops during which production process and means of production functioning are interrupted
  3. Time during which they are held in readiness as prerequisites, representing productive capital but not yet entered production
- **Difference**: Production time vs. time in process of production

### Functioning vs. Latent Capital
- **Functioning time**: When means of production actively serve in productive process
- **Latent capital**: Capital held in readiness as prerequisites (cotton, coal, etc. in spinning-mill)
- **Fallow capital**: Latent capital that acts as neither product nor value creator but is essential for uninterrupted production flow
- **Storage capital**: Buildings, apparatus for storing productive supply; conditions of productive process, component parts of advanced productive capital

### Labour-Process Interruptions
- **Natural processes**: Grain after sowing, wine fermenting in cellar, materials in tanneries exposed to chemical processes
- **Production time > Labour-time**: Time of production exceeds labour-time when subject of labour exposed to physical processes without human labour intervention
- **Excess production time**: Arises from latent existence of productive capital in production sphere without functioning in production process, or functioning in process without participating in labour-process

### Value Creation and Interruptions
- **No labour absorption**: Means of production that don't function in labour-process absorb neither labour nor surplus-labour
- **No value expansion**: No expansion of productive capital value during production time exceeding labour-time, regardless of inseparability from production process
- **Maximising overlap**: The more production time and labour-time cover each other, the greater productivity and self-expansion of capital in given space of time
- **Capitalist tendency**: Reduce excess of production time over labour-time as much as possible
- **Production time definition**: Time in which capital produces use-values and expands, functions as productive capital, including time when latent or produces without expanding value

### Normal Interruptions
- **Process pauses**: Normal interruptions of entire production process during which productive capital doesn't function
- **No value/surplus-value creation**: Normal interruptions create neither value nor surplus-value
- **Night work desire**: Hence desire for work to continue at night (Book I, Chapter VIII, 4)

### Natural Process Time
- **Subject of labour transformations**: Intervals in labour-time that subject of labour must endure in production process
- **Product advancement**: Create neither value nor surplus-value but advance product, form part of its life, process through which it must pass
- **Value transfer**: Value of apparatus transferred to product in proportion to entire time during which they perform function
- **Labour's role**: Labour places means of production in conditions where they pass through natural processes producing useful effects or use-value changes
- **Continuous value transfer**: Labour always transfers value of means of production to product when it consumes them suitably as means of production

### Circulation Time Characteristics
- **Capital forms in circulation**: Capital abides as commodity-capital and money-capital in circulation sphere
- **Circulation processes**: Transformation from commodity-form to money-form and vice versa
- **Surplus-value realisation**: Transformation of commodities into money realises surplus-value embodied in commodities
- **Capital reconversion**: Transformation of money into commodities reconverts capital-value into elements of production
- **Simple metamorphosis**: As processes of circulation, these remain simple metamorphosis of commodities despite value realisation/reconversion

### Circulation Time Effects on Production
- **Production interruption**: During circulation time, capital doesn't function as productive capital; produces neither commodities nor surplus-value
- **Bulk movement**: If entire capital-value passes in one bulk from one phase to another, production process and self-expansion interrupted during circulation time
- **Renewal speed**: Renewal of production proceeds faster or slower depending on circulation time length
- **Aliquot movement**: If capital parts pass through circuit successively, longer aliquot parts in circulation means smaller part functioning in production sphere
- **Negative limits**: Expansion and contraction of circulation time operate as negative limits to contraction or expansion of production time or extent of capital functioning as productive capital

### Ideal Circulation and Productivity
- **Ideal metamorphoses**: The more circulation metamorphoses are ideal (time approaches zero), the more capital functions and the more its productivity and self-expansion increase
- **Payment on delivery**: If capitalist receives payment on product delivery in own means of production, circulation time approaches zero
- **Maximum functioning**: Capital functions maximally when circulation time minimised

### Circulation Time as Limiting Factor
- **General limitation**: Capital's circulation time limits its production time and surplus-value generation process
- **Proportional limitation**: Limitation proportional to circulation time duration
- **Variable duration**: Circulation time may increase/decrease considerably, restricting production time in varying degrees
- **Political economy error**: Political economy sees only apparent effect (negative) but takes it for positive because consequences are positive
- **Mystic source illusion**: Appearance seems to prove capital has mystic source of self-expansion independent of production process and labour exploitation

### Causes of Deception
- **Profit calculation**: Capitalist method of calculating profit where negative cause figures as positive one
- **Different spheres**: With capitals in different investment spheres where only circulation times differ, longer circulation time tends to increase prices
- **Profit equalisation**: Longer circulation time serves as cause of equalising profits
- **Turnover time**: Circulation time is phase of turnover time, which also includes production time

## Key Concepts
- **Production Time**: Time capital spends in production sphere
- **Labour-Time**: Time during which labour is actually expended
- **Circulation Time**: Time capital spends in circulation sphere
- **Total Circuit Time**: Sum of production and circulation times
- **Latent Capital**: Capital held in readiness but not actively functioning
- **Fallow Capital**: Latent capital essential for uninterrupted production
- **Natural Process Time**: Time for natural transformations without labour
- **Functioning Time**: Time means of production actively serve in production
- **Turnover Time**: Production time + Circulation time
- **Metamorphosis**: Transformation of capital from one form to another

## Key Takeaways
1. Total capital circuit time = production time + circulation time
2. Production time can exceed labour-time due to pauses, latent capital, and natural processes
3. Latent capital (held in readiness) is fallow capital essential for production continuity
4. Natural process time (grain growing, wine fermenting) advances product but creates no value
5. Capital expands value only during labour-time, not during excess production time
6. Capitalist production tends to minimise excess of production time over labour-time
7. Circulation time and production time mutually exclude each other
8. During circulation time, capital produces neither commodities nor surplus-value
9. The more circulation time approaches zero, the more productive and self-expanding capital becomes
10. Political economy mistakenly sees circulation time effects as positive when they are actually negative limits
