# Chapter 1: The Circuit of Money Capital

## Core Idea
The circular movement of capital takes place in three stages: M-C (money transforms into commodities), P (productive consumption), and C'-M' (commodities with surplus-value transform back into money). The formula for money-capital circuit is M-C...P...C'-M', where dots indicate interruption and C', M' are increased by surplus-value. This chapter analyzes the first stage (M-C) in detail, revealing how money-capital transforms into productive capital.

## Frameworks Introduced

### The Three Stages of Capital Circuit
- **Stage 1: M-C**: Capitalist appears as buyer on commodity and labour markets; money transforms into commodities
- **Stage 2: P**: Productive consumption of purchased commodities; capitalist produces commodities of greater value
- **Stage 3: C'-M'**: Capitalist returns as seller; commodities transformed into money
- **Formula**: M-C...P...C'-M' (money-capital circuit)
- **Previous focus**: Volume I discussed stages 1 and 3 only as necessary for understanding stage 2 (production)

### Current Object of Study
- **Forms of capital**: Various forms capital assumes and strips off in repetition of its circuit
- **Pure conception**: Must discard factors unrelated to changing/building of forms
- **Assumptions**: Commodities sold at values; same conditions throughout; no value changes during circuit movement

### Stage I: M-C (First Stage)
- **Act**: Conversion of sum of money into sum of commodities; purchaser transforms money into commodities, sellers transform commodities into money
- **Functional definition**: Not form but material content makes this a functionally definite section in independent circuit of individual capital
- **Specific use-character**: Commodities are means of production (MP) and labour-power (L) — material and personal factors in commodity production
- **Notation**: C = L + MP; M-C = M-L + M-MP

### Market Separation
- **Two markets**: The two series of purchases belong to entirely different markets
- **Commodity market**: Purchase of means of production (M-MP)
- **Labour market**: Purchase of labour-power (M-L)
- **Qualitative division**: Sum of commodities into which M is transformed has qualitative division

### Quantitative Relations in M-C
- **Wage form**: Value/price of labour-power paid as wages — price of sum of labour containing surplus-labour
- **Example**: Daily value of labour-power = 5 hours labour valued at 3 shillings; appears as price/wages for 10 hours labour
- **Contract scale**: Contract with 50 labourers = 500 hours/day; 250 hours = 25 days of surplus labour
- **Means of production proportion**: Quantity and volume must be sufficient for utilisation of mass of labour

### M-C as Quantitative Relation
- **Not just qualitative**: M-C expresses quantitative relation between L (money spent for labour-power) and MP (money spent for means of production)
- **Determining factor**: Relation determined by quantity of excess labour/surplus-labour to be expended
- **Spinning-mill example**: Weekly wage of 50 labourers = £50; £372 for means of production if value of means transformed by 3,000 weekly hours (1,500 surplus) into yarn
- **Absolute sufficiency**: Part spent for means of production must absolutely suffice; must be calculated and procured in corresponding proportion

### The Balance Problem
- **Insufficient means**: If means of production at hand insufficient, excess labour cannot be utilised; right to dispose of it is futile
- **Excess means**: If more means of production than available labour, not saturated with labour, not transformed into products
- **Saturation requirement**: Means of production must be sufficient to absorb amount of labour and be transformed into products

### Transformation to Productive Capital
- **Result of M-C**: Purchaser disposes of greater capacity to render labour-power fluent — greater quantity of labour than necessary for replacement of labour-power value
- **Realisation**: Has means of production requisite for realisation/materialisation of this quantity of labour
- **Surplus-value production**: Disposes of factors for production of articles of greater value than elements of production
- **Value form**: Advanced value in money-form now assumes bodily form where it can be incarnated as value generating surplus-value (commodities)
- **Productive capital**: Value exists in condition/form of productive capital P — factor of creating value and surplus-value

### Capital Value States
- **P value**: Value of P = L + MP = M exchanged for L and MP
- **M vs P**: M is same capital-value as P, only different mode of existence
- **Money-capital**: M is capital-value in state/form of money
- **Transformation**: M-C (sum of purchases) = transformation of capital-value from money-form into productive form
- **Summary**: Money-capital into productive capital transformation

### Money as Capital Depository
- **First depository**: In circuit diagram, money appears as first depository of capital-value
- **Money-capital**: Represents form in which capital is advanced

## Key Concepts
- **Money-Capital Circuit**: M-C...P...C'-M' circuit formula
- **Productive Capital (P)**: Capital in form of means of production and labour-power
- **Money-Capital (M)**: Capital in money form
- **M-C Stage**: Transformation of money into commodities (means of production + labour-power)
- **Labour-Power (L)**: Variable capital component
- **Means of Production (MP)**: Constant capital component
- **Surplus Labour**: Labour beyond that necessary for reproducing labour-power value
- **Capital Advance**: Value advanced in money form
- **Capital Value**: Value of capital regardless of form

## Key Takeaways
1. Capital circuit has three stages: M-C (purchase), P (production), C'-M' (sale)
2. Money-capital circuit formula: M-C...P...C'-M'
3. First stage M-C involves purchasing means of production and labour-power in separate markets
4. Quantitative relation between L and MP determined by surplus-labour quantity
5. Means of production must be proportioned to absorb available labour
6. M-C transforms money-capital into productive capital
7. Productive capital P = L + MP with capacity to generate surplus-value
8. Money is first depository of capital-value; money-capital is form of advance
