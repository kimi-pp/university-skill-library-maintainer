# Chapter 15: Effect of the Time of Turnover on the Magnitude of Advanced Capital

## Core Idea
The time of turnover affects how much capital must be advanced to maintain continuous production. If circulation time is long relative to the working period, capital must either be advanced in larger amounts to maintain production scale, or production scale must be reduced. The additional capital required is proportional to the ratio of circulation time to total turnover period, not to the absolute circulation time.

## Frameworks Introduced

### Basic Scenario
- **Assumptions**: Commodity-capital from 9-week working period; ignore fixed capital wear/tear and surplus-value for now
- **Value**: £900 product value = circulating capital (wages + raw/auxiliary materials), £100/week outlay
- **Working period = production period**: 9 weeks
- **Circulation time**: 3 weeks
- **Total turnover period**: 12 weeks (9 + 3)

### Production Interruption Problem
- **End of week 9**: Productive capital converted to commodity-capital
- **Weeks 10-12**: Capital stays in circulation; new production period cannot start
- **Standstill**: Production at standstill for 3 weeks (25% of turnover period)
- **Annual impact**: 3 months/year of production standstill if pattern continues

### Two Solutions for Continuous Production

#### Solution 1: Reduce Production Scale
- **Scale reduction**: Reduce production to maintain continuity with existing capital
- **Calculation**: £900 distributed over 12 weeks = £75/week (instead of £100)
- **Impact**: Reduces scale of business, requires curtailment of fixed capital, overall business reduction
- **Minimum capital**: Each business has normal minimum capital investment essential to maintain capacity
- **Flexibility**: Reductions possible within limits of current normal minimum to ever-increasing normal maximum
- **Crisis response**: During crises, circulating capital reduced by cutting working time
- **Prosperity response**: During prosperity, circulating capital expanded through extended/intensified working time
- **Reserve capacity**: Some businesses use reserve fixed capital (reserve locomotives) and additional labourers

#### Solution 2: Additional Circulating Capital
- **Alternative**: Maintain production scale by adding circulating capital
- **Calculation**: £300 additional capital for 3 weeks (one-quarter of £900)
- **Total investment**: £1,200 successively invested over 12-week period; £300 = one-quarter of £1,200
- **Ratio**: Additional capital proportion = circulation time proportion to turnover period (3/12 = 1/4)
- **Condition**: Only applies to branches with uniform weekly capital investment, not varying amounts like agriculture

### Additional Capital Function
- **Week 1-9**: Original £900 completes working period, becomes commodity-capital
- **Weeks 10-12**: Cannot reopen with same capital; additional £300 maintains continuity
- **First turnover**: Additional capital opens second working period without interruption

### Capital Movement Pattern
- **First period of turnover (12 weeks)**:
  - First working period: 9 weeks
  - First turnover completed at beginning of 13th week
  - Last 3 weeks: Additional £300 functions, opening second working period
- **Second period of turnover**:
  - Beginning of 13th week: £900 returned, but second working period already opened in 10th week by additional £300
  - 1/3 of working period already completed; £300 converted from productive to product
  - Only 6 weeks needed to complete second working period
  - Only 2/3 of returned £900 (£600) can enter second working period
  - £300 of original capital set free to play role of additional capital in first working period
  - End of 6th week of second period: Second working period complete
  - £900 advanced in second working period returns after 3 weeks (end of 9th week of second period)
  - During 3 weeks of circulation, freed £300 comes into action, beginning third working period
- **Third period of turnover**:
  - Close of 9th week of second period: New reflux of £900
  - Third working period already commenced in 7th week of previous period; 6 weeks elapsed
  - Third working period lasts only another 3 weeks
  - Only £300 of returned £900 enter productive process
  - Fourth working period fills remaining 9 weeks

### Simplified Example (5+5 weeks)
- **Working period**: 5 weeks, circulation time: 5 weeks, turnover period: 10 weeks
- **Year**: 50 weeks
- **Weekly outlay**: £100
- **Working period capital**: £500
- **Circulation capital**: £500 additional
- **Working periods**: 1st (1-5), 2nd (6-10), 3rd (11-15), 4th (16-20), 5th (21-25), etc.
- **Returns**: End of 10th, 15th, 20th, 25th, 30th weeks
- **Zero circulation time case**: If circulation time = 0, turnover = working period = 5 weeks
- **Turnovers/year**: 50/5 = 10 turnovers, capital turned over = £5,000
- **With 5-week circulation**: Also produces £5,000/year but £500 always in commodity-capital form
- **Year-end**: 10th working period product (weeks 46-50) completes turnover only by half; circulation time falls into next year

### Third Example (6+3 weeks)
- **Working period**: 6 weeks, circulation time: 3 weeks, weekly advance: £100
- **First working period**: Weeks 1-6, £600 commodity-capital, returned at end of 9th week
- **Second working period**: Weeks 7-12; during weeks 7-9, £300 additional capital advanced
- **End of 9th week**: Return of £600; £300 advanced during weeks 10-12
- **End of 12th week**: £300 free, £600 in commodity-capital returnable at end of 15th week
- **Third working period**: Weeks 13-18; during weeks 13-15, advance above £300, then reflux of £600
- **End of 18th week**: £300 free in money, £600 on hand as commodity-capital returning at end of 21st week
- **Result**: During 9 working periods (54 weeks), £5,400 commodities produced; at end of 9th working period, £300 in money + £600 in commodities not yet circulated

### General Principles

#### Capital Separation vs. Crossing
- **Equal halves case**: Only when working period = circulation time do capital I and II move separately (Exceptional assumption)
- **Normal case**: Movements cross each other in all other cases (Illustrations I and III)
- **Capital II function**: Additional capital with portion of capital I forms capital functioning in second turnover period
- **Capital I remainder**: Remainder of capital I set free to perform original function of capital II
- **Value equivalence**: Capital operating during circulation time = capital II originally advanced in value, same aliquot part of total capital

#### Idle Capital Analysis
- **Second illustration**: Capital functions during 5 weeks working period, lies idle during 5 weeks circulation period
- **Annual idle time**: Entire idle time for capital I = half year
- **Additional capital**: Also lies idle half year
- **Additional capital determination**: Determined by ratio of circulation time to turnover period, not aggregated circulation times during year
- **Assumption**: All turnovers under same conditions

## Key Concepts
- **Turnover Magnitude Effect**: How turnover time affects capital requirements
- **Continuous Production**: Maintaining uninterrupted production flow
- **Additional Circulating Capital**: Extra capital needed for circulation gaps
- **Capital Ratio**: Proportion of additional capital to total capital (circulation time / turnover period)
- **Production Scale**: Level of output and capital investment
- **Normal Minimum**: Minimum capital essential for business capacity
- **Capital Movement**: Pattern of capital flow through circuit
- **Idle Capital**: Capital not actively functioning in production
- **Capital Separation**: Distinct movement of different capital portions
- **Capital Crossing**: Overlapping movement of capital portions

## Key Takeaways
1. Time of turnover affects capital advance magnitude needed for continuous production
2. Long circulation time creates production gaps unless capital is advanced in larger amounts
3. Two solutions: reduce production scale or add circulating capital
4. Additional capital required = (circulation time / turnover period) × total capital
5. This ratio applies regardless of absolute circulation time
6. Capital movements normally cross each other except when working period = circulation time
7. Idle capital time determined by circulation time ratio, not absolute time
8. Maintaining production scale requires additional capital proportional to circulation time ratio
9. Examples show different patterns with varying working/circulation period ratios
10. Principle applies to branches with uniform capital investment, not varying amounts like agriculture
