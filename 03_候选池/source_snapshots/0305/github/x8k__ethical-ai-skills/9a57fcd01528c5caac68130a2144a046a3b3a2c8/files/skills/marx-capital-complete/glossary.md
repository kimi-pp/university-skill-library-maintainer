# Glossary — Marx's Capital (Volumes I-IV)

**A**

- **Abstract Labor** — Labor considered apart from its concrete form, as the expenditure of human labor power in the physiological sense; the substance of value (Ch1 vol1)
- **Absolute Surplus-Value** — Surplus-value produced by extending the working day beyond the point where the worker reproduces the value of their own labor-power (Ch10 vol1)
- **Accumulation of Capital** — The process by which surplus-value is converted into additional capital; the expansion of capital on an increasing scale (Ch23-25 vol1)
- **Alienation** — The process by which workers are separated from the products of their labor, the labor process, their species-being, and other workers under capitalism

**B**

- **Bourgeoisie** — The capitalist class; owners of the means of production who employ wage labor

**C**

- **Capital** — Value that expands itself through the exploitation of labor-power; self-valorizing value (M-C-M')
- **Capital Composition** — The proportion between constant capital and variable capital (c:v); organic composition includes the technical aspect
- **Capitalist Mode of Production** — The specific historical form of production in which capital employs wage labor to produce surplus-value
- **Circulating Capital** — Capital that completes its circuit within one turnover period (raw materials, wages); contrasted with fixed capital
- **Commodity** — A use-value that has exchange-value; the elementary form of bourgeois wealth; a unity of use-value and value (Ch1 vol1)
- **Commodity Fetishism** — The mystification by which the social character of labor appears as an objective property of the products themselves (Ch1 vol1)
- **Concrete Labor** — Labor in its particular useful form (tailoring, weaving, etc.); creates use-values; distinguished from abstract labor
- **Constant Capital (c)** — Capital invested in means of production (raw materials, machinery, buildings) that transfers its value to the product; does not create new value
- **Contradiction** — In dialectical materialism, the internal opposition within a unity that drives development (e.g., use-value vs. value, private vs. social labor)

**D**

- **Dialectical Materialism** — Marx's method: reality is understood through its contradictions; social being determines consciousness; history is the history of class struggle

**E**

- **Exchange-Value** — The quantitative proportion in which use-values of one kind exchange for use-values of another; the phenomenal form in which value appears (Ch1 vol1)
- **Exploitation** — The extraction of surplus labor from the worker by the capitalist; the foundation of capitalist profit
- **Expanded Reproduction** — The reproduction of capital on an increasing scale through the reinvestment of surplus-value (Ch23-24 vol1, Ch21 vol2)

**F**

- **Fixed Capital** — Capital invested in means of production that serve for multiple production cycles (machinery, buildings); transfers value gradually through wear and tear (Ch8 vol1, Ch8 vol2)
- **Fictitious Capital** — Capital that exists in the form of titles to revenue (stocks, bonds) rather than real productive capital (Ch25 vol3)

**I**

- **Industrial Reserve Army** — The relative surplus population created by capital accumulation; serves as a leverage for capital to discipline labor and depress wages (Ch25 vol1)

**L**

- **Labor-Power** — The capacity to work; a special commodity whose use-value is the creation of value greater than its own value; the worker's ability to labor (Ch6 vol1)
- **Labor Theory of Value** — The theory that the value of commodities is determined by the socially necessary labor time required for their production

**M**

- **Means of Production** — The objective conditions of labor: raw materials, instruments of labor (tools, machinery), and labor objects
- **Merchant's Capital** — Capital engaged in the buying and selling of commodities; produces no surplus-value but appropriates a share of it (Ch16-20 vol3)
- **Money** — The universal equivalent; the form in which all commodities express their value; the independent form of value (Ch3 vol1)

**O**

- **Organic Composition of Capital** — The ratio of constant capital to variable capital (c:v) determined by the technical conditions of production; tends to rise with capitalist development

**P**

- **Primitive Accumulation** — The historical process of separating workers from the means of production; creates the preconditions for capitalist production (Ch26-32 vol1)
- **Production Price** — The price at which commodities are sold under capitalism, equal to cost-price plus average profit; differs from value due to the equalization of profit rates across sectors (Ch9-10 vol3)
- **Productive Capital** — Capital in its productive form (P) in the circuit of capital; the form that actually produces surplus-value
- **Productive Labor** — Labor that produces surplus-value directly; under capitalism, wage labor that produces capital
- **Proletariat** — The working class; those who own only their labor-power and must sell it to capitalists to survive

**R**

- **Rate of Profit** — The ratio of surplus-value to total capital advanced (s/(c+v)); tends to fall due to the rising organic composition of capital (Ch2-3 vol3)
- **Rate of Surplus-Value** — The ratio of surplus-value to variable capital (s/v); measures the degree of exploitation of labor-power (Ch9 vol1)
- **Relative Surplus-Value** — Surplus-value produced by reducing the necessary labor time through increased productivity, allowing more surplus labor in the same working day (Ch12 vol1)
- **Reproduction** — The continuous renewal of the production process; simple reproduction maintains the same scale, expanded reproduction increases it

**S**

- **Simple Reproduction** — The reproduction of capital on the same scale, where all surplus-value is consumed rather than reinvested (Ch23 vol1, Ch20 vol2)
- **Socially Necessary Labor Time** — The labor time required to produce any use-value under the conditions of production normal for a given society and with the average degree of skill and intensity of labor
- **Surplus Labor** — Labor performed beyond the point where the worker reproduces the value of their own labor-power; the source of surplus-value
- **Surplus-Value** — The value created by surplus labor; the difference between the value produced by the worker and the value of their labor-power (wages); the foundation of capitalist profit

**T**

- **Theories of Surplus-Value** — The fourth volume of Capital, containing Marx's critical history of economic thought on surplus-value, profit, rent, etc.
- **Turnover of Capital** — The time required for capital to complete its circuit and return to its money form; affects the annual rate of profit (Ch7-9 vol2)

**U**

- **Unproductive Labor** — Labor that does not produce surplus-value directly; includes services, much state employment; contrasted with productive labor
- **Use-Value** — The utility of a thing, its ability to satisfy human wants through its properties; the material content of wealth; the bearer of exchange-value (Ch1 vol1)

**V**

- **Variable Capital (v)** — Capital invested in labor-power; creates new value (including surplus-value) through the labor process; its value is reproduced plus surplus-value is added
- **Value** — The social substance embodied in commodities; crystallized human labor; appears as exchange-value (Ch1 vol1)

**W**

- **Wages** — The price of labor-power; appears as the payment for labor but is actually payment for the capacity to work; takes forms including time-wages and piece-wages (Ch19-22 vol1)
- **Working Day** — The period during which the worker works for the capitalist; divided into necessary labor (reproducing labor-power value) and surplus labor (creating surplus-value) (Ch10 vol1)
