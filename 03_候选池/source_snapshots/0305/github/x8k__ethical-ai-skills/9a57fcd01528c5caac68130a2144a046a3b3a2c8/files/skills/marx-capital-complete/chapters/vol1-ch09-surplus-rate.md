# Chapter 9: The Rate of Surplus-Value

## Core Idea
Surplus-value is created exclusively by variable capital (labour-power), and the rate of surplus-value (s/v) measures the degree of exploitation of labour by capital. This chapter demonstrates that the magnitude of surplus-value depends solely on the variable capital, not the total capital or its constant portion.

## Frameworks Introduced

### The Rate of Surplus-Value (s')
- **Formula**: s' = s / v (surplus-value divided by variable capital)
- **What it measures**: Degree of exploitation of labour-power by capital
- **Alternative expression**: s' = surplus labour / necessary labour
- **Significance**: Shows the proportion in which the capitalist divides the working day into paid and unpaid labour

### Value-Product vs. Product-Value
- **Value-Product**: The new value actually created in production = v + s
- **Product-Value**: The total value of the product = c + v + s (includes transferred constant capital)
- **Key insight**: Only variable capital creates new value; constant capital merely re-appears
- **Example**: With c=£410, v=£90, s=£90: value-product=£180, product-value=£590

### The Two Key Formulas
- **C = c + v** → **C' = (c + v) + s**
  - C: Original capital advanced
  - C': Expanded capital (original + surplus-value)
  - The difference C' - C = s (surplus-value)

- **C' = c + (v + s)** = c + v' (where v' = v + s)
  - Shows that surplus-value arises exclusively from the variable capital component
  - v' represents the expanded variable capital after surplus-value creation

### Proving Surplus-Value Comes Only from Variable Capital
**Method**: Successive substitutions that isolate the source
- **Step 1**: C' = (c + v) + s → surplus-value appears as excess over total capital
- **Step 2**: But c is only transferred, not created anew → surplus-value must come from v
- **Step 3**: v + s = v' (expanded variable capital) → surplus-value is purely a change in v
- **Conclusion**: "Surplus-value is purely the result of a variation in the value of v"

### The Constant Capital Illusion
- **Appearance**: Total capital grows from C to C', suggesting constant capital contributes
- **Reality**: Only v changes magnitude; c remains constant and is merely preserved
- **Why the confusion**: The increase in total capital (C→C') hides that only v expanded
- **Clarification**: If c=0 (hypothetical case), surplus-value would still be s; if s=0, no expansion occurs

### Rate of Surplus-Value as Exploitation Measure
- **Definition**: "The relative quantity of surplus-value, or the ratio of the surplus-value to the variable capital, or the ratio of the surplus part of the value to the part of its value that forms the equivalent for labour-power"
- **Alternative names**: Degree of exploitation, rate of exploitation
- **Worker's perspective**: Shows what proportion of the working day is unpaid (surplus labour)
- **Capitalist's perspective**: Shows what proportion of labour-power cost is recovered vs. exceeded

## Key Concepts
- **Rate of Surplus-Value (s')**: s/v; measures exploitation degree
- **Value-Product**: v + s; new value created by labour
- **Product-Value**: c + v + s; total commodity value
- **Expanded Variable Capital (v')**: v + s; variable capital after value creation
- **Degree of Exploitation**: Alternative term for rate of surplus-value
- **Surplus Labour**: Unpaid labour time; source of surplus-value
- **Necessary Labour**: Paid labour time; reproduces labour-power value

## Mental Models

### Use the substitution method
When analyzing value creation:
1. Start with C' = (c + v) + s
2. Recognize c is only transferred, not created
3. Isolate v + s as the new value
4. Express as v' = v + s
5. Conclude: surplus-value comes only from v

### Calculate exploitation
When examining a capitalist enterprise:
- **Given**: Working day = 10 hours, necessary labour = 4 hours
- **Surplus labour** = 10 - 4 = 6 hours
- **Rate of surplus-value** = 6/4 = 150%
- **Interpretation**: Worker works 150% more than needed to reproduce their wage

### Distinguish value flows
When tracing capital:
- **Constant capital**: Flows through production unchanged (transferred)
- **Variable capital**: Flows through production expanded (v → v+s)
- **Total capital**: Appears to grow, but only v actually changes

## Key Takeaways
1. Surplus-value arises exclusively from variable capital (labour-power)
2. The rate of surplus-value (s/v) measures the degree of exploitation
3. Value-product (v+s) is different from product-value (c+v+s)
4. The formula C' = c + v' reveals that only variable capital expands
5. Constant capital's value is preserved, not created; its magnitude doesn't affect surplus-value
6. The rate of surplus-value can be expressed as the ratio of surplus labour to necessary labour
