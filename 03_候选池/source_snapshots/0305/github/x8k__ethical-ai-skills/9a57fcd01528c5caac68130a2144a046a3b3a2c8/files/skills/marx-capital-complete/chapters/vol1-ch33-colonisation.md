# Chapter 33: The Modern Theory of Colonisation

## Core Idea
Political economy confuses two fundamentally different kinds of private property: one based on producers' own labour, the other on exploitation of others' labour. In colonies, where capitalist relations collide with independent producers, political economists are forced to reveal the truth about capitalist production. E.G. Wakefield's colonisation theory exposes that capital is not a thing but a social relation, and that wage labour must be artificially created in colonies where independent production dominates.

## Frameworks Introduced

### The Two Types of Private Property
- **Labour-based property**: Property resting on producers' own labour — small peasant or artisan ownership
- **Capital-based property**: Property resting on employment of others' labour — capitalist ownership
- **Antithesis**: Capitalist property is direct antithesis of labour-based property and grows only on its tomb
- **European context**: In Western Europe (home of political economy), primitive accumulation is largely accomplished; capitalist regime dominates national production
- **Ideological function**: Political economists apply pre-capitalist legal notions to capitalist world to justify exploitation

### The Colonial Contradiction
- **Collision**: In colonies, capitalist regime collides with producer resistance — independent labourers employ labour to enrich themselves, not capitalists
- **Practical manifestation**: Struggle between two diametrically opposed economic systems
- **Mother-country support**: Where capitalist has state power behind him, he uses force to clear away independent production modes
- **Apologetic exposure**: Same interest that compels political economist to proclaim theoretical identity of capitalism with its contrary in mother-country compels him to proclaim antagonism in colonies
- **Truth revelation**: Must prove that capitalist development requires expropriation of labourers and transformation of means of production into capital

### Wakefield's Discovery
- **Capital as social relation**: Discovered that property in money, means of subsistence, machines, etc., does not stamp a man as capitalist without the correlative — the wage-worker compelled to sell himself
- **Not a thing**: "Capital is not a thing, but a social relation between persons, established by the instrumentality of things"
- **Peel's dilemma**: Mr. Peel took £50,000 in means of subsistence and production to Swan River, West Australia, plus 300 working-class people; once arrived, "Mr. Peel was left without a servant to make his bed or fetch him water from the river"
- **Missing element**: Forgot to export English modes of production (wage labour system) to Swan River

### Wakefield's Conceptual Confusions
- **Means of production vs capital**: Knows that means of production are not capital while property of immediate producer; become capital only when they exploit and subject labourer
- **Capitalist soul**: But political economist so intimately wed capital's social soul to material substance that he calls them capital under all circumstances, even when opposite
- **Equal division of capital**: Calls splitting of means of production into individual property of many independent labourers "equal division of capital"
- **Feudal jurist parallel**: Like feudal jurist who stuck feudal law labels on pure monetary relations

### The Social Contract Myth
- **Wakefield's explanation**: Capitalist accumulation requires that labourer cannot accumulate for himself — requires separation from means of production
- **Problem**: In colonies, labourers can accumulate; capitalist accumulation impossible; wage labourer class wanting
- **Wakefield's solution**: Social contract — mankind divided itself into owners of capital and owners of labour by concert and combination
- **Self-expropriation**: "Mass of mankind expropriated itself in honour of the 'accumulation of capital'"
- **Colonial reality**: This instinct of self-denying fanaticism does not exist in colonies where conditions for social contract actually exist
- **Slavery as basis**: According to Wakefield, slavery is sole natural basis of colonial wealth; systematic colonisation is pis aller for dealing with free men

### The Spanish Example
- **Saint Domingo**: First Spanish settlers did not obtain labourers from Spain; without labourers, capital would perish or diminish to what each individual could employ with own hands
- **Swan River**: Actually occurred in England's last colony — Swan River Settlement — where great mass of capital, seeds, implements, cattle perished for want of labourers
- **Result**: No settler preserved much more capital than he could employ with his own hands

### The Free Colony Problem
- **Essence**: Bulk of soil is public property; every settler can turn part into private property and individual means of production without hindering later settlers
- **Secret of colonial prosperity**: This is why colonies prosper — independent production possible
- **Anti-capitalist cancer**: But this prevents establishment of capital; wakefield calls it "barbarising tendency of dispersion"
- **Consequence**: Parcelling of means of production among innumerable owners annihilates centralisation of capital and all foundations of combined labour
- **Long-term projects**: Every undertaking extending over several years and demanding fixed capital outlay prevented from being carried out

### The Labour Market Problem in Colonies
- **European advantage**: In Europe, capital invests without hesitating because working class constitutes its living appurtenance, always in excess, always at disposal
- **Colonial disadvantage**: Regular reproduction of wage labourer as wage labourer collides with impediments; wage labourer of today is independent producer tomorrow
- **constant transformation**: Wage labourers vanish from labour market into independent production for themselves instead of capital
- **Perverse reaction**: This reacts perversely on labour market conditions — degree of exploitation remains low; wage labourer loses sentiment of dependence
- **Wakefield's complaint**: Supply of wage labour is neither constant, nor regular, nor sufficient; "always not only small but uncertain"

### The Wage Problem
- **High wages**: Labourer takes so great a share of produce that he soon becomes a capitalist himself
- **Few accumulations**: Few, even with long lives, can accumulate great masses of wealth
- **Labourer resistance**: Labourers decline to allow capitalist to abstain from payment of greater part of their labour
- **Imported labour**: Even if capitalist imports European wage-workers with his own capital, they soon cease to be labourers for hire and become independent landowners or competitors
- **Capitalist horror**: "The excellent capitalist has imported bodily from Europe, with his own good money, his own competitors!"

### Merivale's Contribution
- **Urgent desire**: On account of high wages, there is "urgent desire for cheaper and more subservient labourers — for a class to whom the capitalist might dictate terms, instead of being dictated to by them"
- **Natural dependence**: In ancient civilised countries, labourer (though free) is by law of Nature dependent on capitalists
- **Artificial creation**: In colonies, this dependence must be created by artificial means
- **Colonial solution**: Need to create wage labour class artificially

### Systematic Colonisation Theory
- **Purpose**: Wakefield's theory (tried by England through Acts of Parliament) attempted to manufacture wage-workers in colonies
- **Artificial creation**: Since spontaneous colonisation produces independent producers, need systematic colonisation to create wage labour
- **Method**: Not discovered by Wakefield but discovered in colonies the truth about mother-country conditions
- **Protection parallel**: Just as protection system at origin attempted to manufacture capitalists artificially in mother-country

### The Internal Market Problem
- **No exclusive agriculture**: No part of American population exclusively agricultural except slaves and their employers
- **Diverse occupations**: Free Americans who cultivate soil follow many other occupations
- **Self-production**: They make furniture, tools, build houses, carry produce to market
- **Household industry**: They are spinners, weavers, make soap, candles, shoes, clothes for own use
- **Secondary pursuit**: Cultivation often secondary pursuit of blacksmith, miller, shopkeeper
- **No field of abstinence**: With such people, where is the field for capitalist abstinence and exploitation?

### The Beauty of Capitalist Production
- **Self-reproduction**: Capitalist production constantly reproduces wage-worker as wage-worker
- **Relative surplus population**: Produces, in proportion to capital accumulation, relative surplus-population of wage-workers
- **Supply-demand control**: Keeps law of supply and demand in right rut; wages oscillate within limits satisfactory to capitalist exploitation
- **Dependence creation**: Secures social dependence of labourer on capitalist — indispensable requisite
- **Ideological transmutation**: Political economist transforms this dependence into free contract between equally independent commodity owners
- **Colonial exposure**: In colonies, this pretty fancy is torn asunder — absolute population increases quickly but labour market always understocked

### The Population Paradox
- **Rapid growth**: Absolute population increases much more quickly than mother-country (many labourers enter as ready-made adults)
- **Labour market failure**: Yet labour market always understocked; law of supply and demand of labour falls to pieces
- **Capital inflow**: Old world constantly throws in capital thirsting for exploitation and abstinence
- **Reproduction collision**: Regular reproduction of wage labourer collides with impertinent and partly invincible impediments
- **Surplus labour problem**: What becomes of production of supernumerary wage labourers? They become independent producers working for themselves instead of capital

### The Wakefield Solution: Artificial Means
- **Poverty creation**: In interest of so-called national wealth, seek artificial means to ensure poverty of people
- **Apologetic crumbling**: Wakefield's apologetic armour crumbles off like rotten touchwood as he exposes colonial realities
- **Merit**: Wakefield's great merit is discovering in colonies the truth about mother-country conditions

### The Colonial Reality vs. Theory
- **American prosperity**: Mass of American people well-to-do, independent, enterprising, comparatively cultured
- **English contrast**: English agricultural labourer is miserable wretch, a pauper
- **Agricultural wages**: In no country (except North America and some new colonies) do wages of free agricultural labour exceed bare subsistence
- **Animal comparison**: "Undoubtedly, farm-horses in England, being a valuable property, are better fed than English peasants"
- **National wealth paradox**: Yet national wealth is identical with misery of people

### The Land Appropriation Solution
- **Artificial scarcity**: How to heal anti-capitalistic cancer of colonies? Create artificial scarcity of land
- **Price mechanism**: Fix sufficiently high price on land to prevent labourers from becoming independent too quickly
- **Wakefield's formula**: "In order that a part of the soil should be withdrawn from competition, and a fund for the emigration of labourers be created"
- **Capitalist interest**: State should receive a price for land sufficient to maintain constant stream of wage labourers to capital
- **Practical implementation**: Wakefield's colonisation theory attempted by Acts of Parliament

### The Class Perspective
- **Capitalist view**: Capitalist wants cheap, subservient labourers — class to whom he can dictate terms
- **Labourer view**: In colonies, labourers dictate to capitalists; in Europe, capitalists dictate to labourers
- **Molinari's dilemma**: Free trade economist Molinari acknowledges that in colonies where slavery abolished, workers exploit industrial entrepreneurs, demanding wages out of proportion
- **Planters' ruin**: Planters unable to obtain sufficient sugar prices to cover wage increases; obliged to furnish extra from profits then capital; many ruined
- **Molinari's question**: "Would it not be better if both survived?" — but this questions foundations of political economy

### The Theoretical Contradiction
- **Commandments violation**: What becomes of ten commandments, Moses and prophets, law of supply and demand if in Europe entrepreneur can cut down labourer's share and in West Indies labourer can cut down entrepreneur's?
- **Legitimate part**: What is this legitimate part that capitalist daily neglects to pay?
- **Police solution**: Molinari wants to use police to set law of supply and demand on right road in colonies

### Collins' Critique
- **Individual appropriation**: It is thanks to individual appropriation of soil that men exist who possess only strength of their arms
- **Vacuum analogy**: "When you put a man in a vacuum, you rob him of the air. You do the same, when you take away the soil from him... for you are putting him in a space void of wealth, so as to leave him no way of living except according to your wishes"
- **Wakefield's position**: Wakefield quotes this approval but still argues for artificial land scarcity

### The Australian Example
- **Self-government**: As soon as Australia became her own law-giver, passed laws favourable to settlers
- **Land squandering**: English Government had already squandered land, standing in the way
- **1862 Land Act**: First and main object was to give increased facilities for settlement of people

## Key Concepts
- **Colonisation Theory**: Systematic approach to colonial settlement and development
- **Artificial Land Scarcity**: Creating land price barriers to prevent independent production
- **Wage Labour Creation**: Artificial creation of proletariat in colonial contexts
- **Capital as Social Relation**: Capital not as thing but as relation between capitalists and wage labourers
- **Systematic Colonisation**: Wakefield's theory of controlled colonial development
- **Primitive Accumulation**: Historical process of creating preconditions for capitalism
- **Independent Production**: Production by labourers owning their means of production
- **Free Contract Ideology**: Bourgeois myth of equal exchange between capital and labour
- **Class Struggle**: Conflict between capitalists and labourers over terms of production
- **National Wealth**: Ideological concept equating wealth accumulation with national prosperity

## Key Takeaways
1. Political economy confuses labour-based property with capital-based property, which are fundamentally opposed
2. In colonies, capitalist relations collide with independent producers, forcing political economists to reveal capitalist truths
3. Wakefield discovered that capital is a social relation, not a thing — it requires both capitalists and wage labourers
4. In colonies, labourers can become independent producers, preventing capitalist accumulation
5. Wakefield's colonisation theory proposes artificial creation of wage labour through land pricing
6. The beauty of capitalist production is its constant reproduction of wage labourers and relative surplus population
7. In colonies, the law of supply and demand of labour breaks down because labourers escape wage labour
8. Capitalists desire cheap, subservient labour; in colonies, they must create this dependence artificially
9. The colonial reality exposes the myth of free contract and reveals the coercive nature of capitalist relations
10. Wakefield's theory reveals that capitalist production requires the artificial maintenance of poverty
