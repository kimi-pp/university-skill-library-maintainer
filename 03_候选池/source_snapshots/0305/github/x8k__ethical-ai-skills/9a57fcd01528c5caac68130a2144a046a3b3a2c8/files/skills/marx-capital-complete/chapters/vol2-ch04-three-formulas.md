# Chapter 4: The Three Formulas of the Circuit

## Core Idea
The three circuits of capital — money-capital (M-C...P...C'-M'), productive capital (P...C'-M'-C...P), and commodity-capital (C'-M'-C...P) — are not separate processes but different expressions of the same total process. When combined, all premises appear as results produced by the process itself. The total process is the unity of production and circulation, where each circuit form is continuously present alongside the others, and the continuity of capitalist production depends on the successive passage of capital parts through all three forms.

## Frameworks Introduced

### The Three Circuit Formulas
- **Formula I (Money-Capital)**: M — C ... P ... C' — M'
- **Formula II (Productive Capital)**: P ... Tc ... P (where Tc = total circulation process)
- **Formula III (Commodity-Capital)**: Tc ... P (C')
- **Unified view**: All three combined show every element as point of departure, transit, and return

### Common Purpose
- **Self-expansion of value**: Determining purpose and compelling motive in all three circuits
- **Form I expression**: Explicit in money-capital formula
- **Form II beginning**: Starts with P, the process of creating surplus-value
- **Form III beginning**: Starts with self-expanded value (C'), even on same scale repetition

### Circulation and Metamorphosis
- **Ordinary metamorphosis**: Capital circulation presents only ordinary metamorphosis of commodities (C-M and M-C)
- **Laws of circulation**: Laws from Book I, Chapter III, Section 2 apply to mass of money in circulation
- **Connection problem**: Formal change of money and commodities cannot explain actual connection between individual capital circuits
- **Social capital**: Must consider connection between circuits as partial movements of total social capital reproduction

### Circular Movement Dynamics
- **Every point**: In constantly revolving circle, every point is simultaneously departure and return
- **Interruption effect**: If rotation interrupted, not every departure point is return point
- **Mutual presupposition**: Every individual circuit presupposes others; repetition in one form comprises performance in others
- **Formal difference**: Difference appears merely formal or subjective, existing only for observer

### Individual vs. Social Capital
- **Individual appearance**: Difference exists only as individual one for various industrial capitals
- **Reality**: Every individual industrial capital is present simultaneously in all three circuits
- **Continuous co-existence**: Three circuits (reproduction forms of three capital forms) made continuously side by side
- **Example**: One part of capital-value as commodity-capital transforms into money-capital; another part leaves production as new commodity-capital; C'...C' circuit continuously described
- **Unity**: Reproduction of capital in each form and stage is as continuous as metamorphosis and passage through three stages

### Capital as Whole and Parts
- **Assumption in analysis**: Assumed capital-value in entire magnitude acts as money-capital, productive-capital, or commodity-capital
- **Example**: £422 first entirely as money-capital, then wholly as productive capital, finally as commodity-capital (£500 yarn with £78 surplus-value)
- **Stage character**: Various stages are interruptions; capital cannot perform multiple functions simultaneously
- **Functional exclusion**: As long as £422 retain money-form (until M-C purchases), entire capital exists and functions only as money-capital

### Production Continuity Problem
- **Interruption concern**: Circuit P...P would represent periodical renewal of productive capital AND interruption of function (production process) until circulation completed
- **Jerky production**: Without continuity, production would proceed in jerks, renewed only after circulation stages completed
- **Artisan example**: Like Chinese artisan working only for private customers; production ceases until new order received

### Continuity Mechanism
- **Simultaneous existence**: All parts of capital in motion; all parts go through motion in succession
- **Yarn example**: 10,000 lbs. weekly yarn product leaves production, enters circulation; capital-value must be converted into money-capital
- **Reconversion requirement**: Money-capital cannot re-enter production until reconverted into L + MP
- **Process character**: Circuit-describing process = constant interruption, leaving one stage, entering next, discarding one form, assuming another
- **Mutual exclusion**: Each stage not only presupposes next but also excludes it
- **Capitalist necessity**: Continuity is characteristic mark of capitalist production, necessitated by technical basis

### Reality of Continuous Production
- **Simultaneous stages**: While 10,000 lbs. yarn appear as commodity-capital and transform into money, new cotton/coal take yarn's place in production
- **Parallel conversion**: New materials already reconverted from money/Commodity form into productive capital form and begin functioning
- **Successive circulation**: At same time, preceding 10,000 lbs. yarn go through second circulation stage, reconverted from money into productive capital elements
- **Simultaneous circuits**: All parts of capital successively describe circuits, simultaneously at different stages

### Industrial Capital in Orbit
- **Continuous progression**: Industrial capital continuously progresses along orbit
- **Simultaneous existence**: Exists simultaneously at all stages and in diverse functional forms corresponding to stages
- **Circuit initiation**: Part converted from commodity-capital into money begins circuit C'...C'
- **Capital whole**: Industrial capital as moving whole has already passed through circuit
- **Money movement**: One hand advances money, another receives it; M...M' inauguration at one place coincides with money return at another
- **Unity condition**: Actual circuit of industrial capital in continuity is unity of circulation and production processes AND unity of all three circuits

### Division of Capital
- **Successive vs. co-existence**: Succession of parts governed by co-existence; co-existence is division of capital
- **Factory system**: In ramified factory, product constantly in various formation stages, constantly passes from one production phase to another
- **Definite size**: Individual industrial capital has definite size (capitalist's means), minimum magnitude for each industry branch
- **Proportional division**: Division must proceed according to definite proportions
- **Magnitude determination**: Available capital magnitude determines process of production dimensions, which determines commodity-capital and money-capital dimensions

### Co-existence as Succession Result
- **Continuity determination**: Co-existence by which production continuity determined is merely result of succession
- **Stagnation effect**: If C'-M' interrupted for one part (commodity cannot be sold), circuit of this part interrupted, no replacement by reproduction means
- **Blockage**: Succeeding parts emerging as C' find function change blocked by predecessors
- **Production restriction**: If stagnation lasts, production restricted, entire process brought to halt
- **Systemic impact**: Every stagnation in succession carries disorder into co-existence; stagnation in one stage causes stagnation in entire capital circuit

### Process as Phase Succession
- **Necessary transition**: Process presents as succession of phases; capital transition to new phase necessitated by departure from another
- **Functional cycles**: Each separate circuit has one functional form as departure and return point
- **Aggregate continuity**: Aggregate process is unity of three circuits (different forms expressing continuity)
- **Mutual dependency**: Cycle of each functional form dependent on others
- **Reproduction prerequisite**: For social capital, aggregate production process must be simultaneously reproduction process and circuit of each element

### Fluid Forms and Simultaneity
- **Successive movement**: Various fractional parts pass successively through stages and functional forms
- **Functional expression**: Thanks to this, every functional form passes simultaneously with others through own circuit, always different part of capital expressed in it
- **Spatial co-existence**: Capital as whole exists simultaneously, spatially side by side, in different phases
- **Constant succession**: Every part constantly and successively passes from one phase/form to next, functions in all in turn
- **Fluid forms**: Forms are fluid; simultaneity brought about by succession
- **Reciprocal movement**: Every form follows and precedes another; return of one capital part to form necessitated by return of other part to other form
- **Continuous cycles**: Every part continuously describes own cycle; always another part exists in this form; special cycles form simultaneous and successive elements of aggregate process

### Continuity vs. Interruption
- **Aggregate continuity**: Instead of interruption, aggregate process achieves continuity
- **Technical basis**: Continuity necessitated by technical basis of capitalist production
- **Capital dependence**: Individual capital continuity depends on capital magnitude

## Key Concepts
- **Three Circuit Forms**: Money-capital, productive capital, commodity-capital circuits
- **Total Circuit**: Unity of all three circuit forms
- **Capital Division**: Distribution of capital across different stages and forms
- **Simultaneous Existence**: Capital existing in all forms simultaneously
- **Successive Movement**: Capital parts passing through stages in sequence
- **Co-existence**: Different stages existing side by side
- **Succession**: Sequential passage through stages
- **Continuity**: Characteristic feature of capitalist production
- **Stagnation**: Interruption in one stage affecting entire circuit
- **Social Capital**: Aggregate capital of society

## Key Takeaways
1. Three circuits (M...M', P...P, C'...C') are different expressions of same total process
2. All premises appear as results produced by the process itself
3. Self-expansion of value is determining purpose in all circuits
4. Every individual capital is simultaneously present in all three circuits
5. Continuity of capitalist production is characteristic mark, necessitated by technical basis
6. Capital exists simultaneously in all stages through division of its parts
7. Co-existence of stages is result of succession of parts through stages
8. Stagnation in one stage causes stagnation throughout entire circuit
9. Aggregate process is unity of circulation and production AND unity of all three circuits
10. Individual capital continuity depends on magnitude allowing division across stages
