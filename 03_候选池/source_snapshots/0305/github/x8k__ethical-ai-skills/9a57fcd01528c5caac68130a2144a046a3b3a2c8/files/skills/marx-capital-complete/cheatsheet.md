# Cheatsheet — Marx's Capital

## Core Formulas

### Value and Surplus Value
```
Commodity Value (W) = Constant Capital (c) + Variable Capital (v) + Surplus Value (s)
                    = c + v + s

Rate of Surplus Value (s') = s / v  (degree of exploitation)

Rate of Profit (p') = s / (c + v)  (tends to fall over time)

Mass of Surplus Value = s' × v × number of workers
```

### Capital Composition
```
Organic Composition = c : v  (technical + value composition)

Turnover Time = Circulation Time + Production Time

Annual Rate of Profit = (s / (c + v)) × (number of turnovers per year)
```

## Key Distinctions

| Concept | Definition | Example | Source |
|---------|------------|---------|--------|
| **Use-Value** | Utility of a thing | A coat keeps you warm | Ch1 vol1 |
| **Exchange-Value** | Quantitative proportion in exchange | 1 coat = 20 yards of linen | Ch1 vol1 |
| **Value** | Socially necessary labor time | 10 hours of average labor | Ch1 vol1 |
| **Concrete Labor** | Specific useful work | Tailoring, weaving | Ch1 vol1 |
| **Abstract Labor** | Human labor in general | "Labor" as value-creating substance | Ch1 vol1 |
| **Constant Capital (c)** | Value transferred from means of production | Raw materials, machinery | Ch8 vol1 |
| **Variable Capital (v)** | Value advanced for labor-power | Wages | Ch8 vol1 |
| **Surplus Value (s)** | Value created beyond v | Profit source | Ch7 vol1 |
| **Fixed Capital** | Serves multiple production cycles | Machinery, buildings | Ch8 vol1 |
| **Circulating Capital** | Consumed in one cycle | Raw materials, wages | Ch8 vol1 |

## Capital Circuit

```
Money Capital (M) → Commodities (C) [Means of Production + Labor-Power]
                          ↓
                   Production Process (P) [Labor creates new value]
                          ↓
                   Commodity Capital (C') [C + s]
                          ↓
                   Money Capital (M') [M + s]
```

**Total Circuit**: M - C ... P ... C' - M'

**Purpose**: Not use-values, but the expansion of value (M → M')

---

## Three Forms of Capital

| Form | Symbol | Function | Surplus-Value Creation |
|------|--------|----------|-------------------------|
| **Money Capital** | M | Purchases means of production and labor-power | No, enables |
| **Productive Capital** | P | Organizes production, exploits labor | **Yes, directly** |
| **Commodity Capital** | C' | Realizes value through sale | No, realizes |

---

## Capital Turnover

| Turnover Type | Definition | Formula | Effect on Profit |
|---------------|------------|---------|------------------|
| **Annual Turnover** | Capital completes circuit once per year | n = 1 | Normal profit |
| **Multiple Turnover** | Capital completes circuit >1 time/year | n > 1 | Higher annual profit |
| **Partial Turnover** | Fixed capital turns over across multiple years | n < 1 | Lower annual profit |

**Annual Surplus Value** = s × n (where n = number of turnovers)

## Reproduction Schemas (Volume II)

### Simple Reproduction
```
Department I (Means of Production):
  4000c + 1000v + 1000s = 6000

Department II (Consumption Goods):
  2000c + 500v + 500s = 3000

Exchange:
- I(v + s) = II(c) → 2000 = 2000 ✓
- I(c) = I(c) → 4000 exchanged within Department I
- II(v + s) = II(v + s) → 1000 consumed by workers/capitalists
```

### Expanded Reproduction
```
Department I:
  4000c + 1000v + 1000s = 6000

Department II:
  1500c + 750v + 750s = 3000

Exchange for expansion:
- I(v + s) = 2000 must cover II(c) = 1500 + 500 for new I(c)
- Capitalists consume only part of surplus-value
```

## Tendency of Rate of Profit to Fall

### Causes
1. **Rising Organic Composition**: c/v ratio increases with technological development
2. **Fixed Capital Growth**: More machinery, less labor-power employed
3. **Raw Material Costs**: May rise or fall, affecting composition

### Counteracting Factors
1. **More Intensive Exploitation**: Increase rate of surplus-value (s')
2. **Depression of Wages Below Value**: Pay workers less than labor-power value
3. **Cheapening of Constant Capital**: Technical progress reduces c value
4. **Relative Surplus Population**: Reserve army depresses wages
5. **Foreign Trade**: Cheaper inputs from abroad
6. **Increase in Share Capital**: Stock companies reduce individual capital needs

### Formula
```
Rate of Profit (p') = s / (c + v) = (s/v) / ((c/v) + 1)

As c/v ↑, p' ↓ (all else equal)
```

## Working Day Structure

```
Total Working Day = Necessary Labor Time + Surplus Labor Time
                   ↑               ↑
              Reproduces v      Creates s (surplus-value)

Example: 10 hour day
- Necessary labor: 4 hours (reproduces wage value)
- Surplus labor: 6 hours (creates surplus-value)
- Rate of surplus-value: 6/4 = 150%
```

## Primitive Accumulation Methods

1. **Enclosure Movements**: Forcible expropriation of peasantry from land
2. **Colonialism**: Looting of colonies (gold, silver, slaves, raw materials)
3. **Slave Trade**: Massive accumulation of capital from slave labor
4. **State Debt**: Public debt finances wars and accumulation
5. **Taxation**: Forced extraction from population
6. **Protectionism**: Mercantilist policies benefit early capitalists

## Crisis Types

| Type | Cause | Manifestation | Resolution |
|------|-------|---------------|------------|
| **Overproduction** | Anarchy of production | Glut, falling prices | Devaluation |
| **Financial** | Credit bubble burst | Bank failures, panic | Credit contraction |
| **Disproportionality** | Sectoral imbalance | Shortages in some, glut in others | Reallocation |
| **Profit Squeeze** | Rising wages, falling profits | Stagflation | Restore profit rates |

## Class Structure

```
Capitalist Class (Bourgeoisie)
├── Industrial Capitalists (factories, production)
├── Commercial Capitalists (trading, merchants)
├── Money Capitalists (bankers, financiers)
└── Landowners (rentiers)

Working Class (Proletariat)
├── Industrial Workers (factories)
├── Agricultural Workers (farm labor)
├── Commercial Workers (clerks, sales)
├── Reserve Army (unemployed)
└── Lumpemproletariat (marginalized)

├── Petty Bourgeoisie (small owners)
├── Managers, Supervisors
└── Professionals, Technicians
```

## Quick Reference: Marx's Method

1. **Start from the concrete** → Observe real economic phenomena
2. **Rise to the abstract** → Identify fundamental categories (commodity, value, capital)
3. **Analyze contradictions** → Find internal oppositions that drive development
4. **Return to the concrete** → Explain real phenomena through abstract categories
5. **Historical context** → Always locate analysis in specific historical conditions
6. **Class perspective** → Analyze from the viewpoint of proletarian liberation

## Common Misconceptions

| Misconception | Correction | Source |
|--------------|------------|--------|
| Marx said labor creates ALL value | Labor creates value under capitalism; use-values come from nature + labor | Ch1 vol1 |
| Labor theory is simple | Value = socially necessary labor time; complex social determination | Ch1 vol1 |
| Marx predicted immiseration | Real wages can rise, but exploitation continues; relative immiseration | Various |
| Marx ignored supply/demand | Supply/demand affect market prices, but value is determined by labor time | Ch10-12 vol3 |
| Capital is just money | Capital is a social relation; money is just one form | Ch4-5 vol1 |
| Communism = equal poverty | "From each according to ability, to each according to needs" | Critique of Gotha Program |
