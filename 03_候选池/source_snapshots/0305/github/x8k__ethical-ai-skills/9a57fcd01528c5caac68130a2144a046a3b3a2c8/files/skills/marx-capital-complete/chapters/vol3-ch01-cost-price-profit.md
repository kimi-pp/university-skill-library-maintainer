# Chapter 1: Cost-Price and Profit

## Core Idea

This opening chapter of Volume III introduces the concept of cost-price as a capitalist category that obscures the true source of value. Marx demonstrates that while the commodity's value is C = c + v + s, the capitalist perceives only k + s (cost-price + surplus-value), where cost-price k = c + v represents what the commodity costs the capitalist. The critical distinction is that cost-price measures capitalist expenditure of capital, while actual value measures expenditure of labour. This category, while real in capitalist practice, creates the false appearance that surplus-value arises from capital itself rather than from unpaid labour, setting the stage for the fetishized forms of profit, interest, and rent analyzed throughout Volume III.

### II. The Cost-Price Category

#### Commodity Value Composition
- **Formula**: C = c + v + s (constant capital + variable capital + surplus-value)
- **Cost-Price Definition**: k = c + v (capital-value expended in production elements)
- **Example**: £500 capital (£20 wear/tear + £380 materials + £100 labour-power), rate of surplus-value = 100%
  - Product value = 400c + 100v + 100s = £600
  - Cost-price = £500 (replaces expended capital)

#### The Dual Meaning of Cost-Price
- **For Capitalist**: Represents actual cost of commodity (what it costs him)
- **Reality**: Two different magnitudes:
  - **Capitalist Cost**: Measured by capital expenditure
  - **Actual Cost**: Measured by labour expenditure
- **Surplus-Value Portion**: Costs capitalist nothing because it costs labourer unpaid labour
- **Capitalist Perception**: After labourer enters production, labourer becomes ingredient of operating productive capital belonging to capitalist; thus capitalist appears as actual producer

#### The Cost-Price Formula
- **Transformation**: C = c + v + s → C = k + s (commodity-value = cost-price + surplus-value)
- **Grouping**: Value portions replacing capital expended grouped under cost-price
- **Specific Character**: Expresses capitalist production's specific character

#### Cost-Price vs. Value
- **Quantitative Difference**: k = C - s (cost-price smaller than commodity value)
- **Practical Manifestation**: Individualisation of this value portion continually manifest in commodity production
- **Reproduction Requirement**: Must be reconverted from commodity-form via circulation into productive capital form

#### Cost-Price and Value Formation
- **No Relation**: Category of cost-price has nothing to do with commodity-value formation or capital self-expansion process
- **False Appearance**: In capitalist economics, cost-price assumes false appearance of value production category

### III. The Components of Cost-Price: Different Origins

#### Numerical Illustration
- **Assumptions**: One labourer's average social working-day = 6s. = 6M
- **Capital**: £500 = 400c + 100v
  - 400c = 1,333⅓ working-days (materials value)
  - 100v = 333⅓ working-days (labour-power value)
- **Production**: Rate of surplus-value = 100%, labour expenditure = 100v + 100s = 666⅔ working-days
- **Product Value**: £600 = reappearing 400c + newly produced £200

#### Cost-Price Composition Analysis
- **Cost-Price £500**: Comprises reappearing 400c + one-half of newly produced £200 (100v)
- **Two Different Origins**:
  1. **Constant Capital Portion (400c)**: Previously existing value; transferred from means of production to product
     - Does not get created in commodity production process
     - Exists as commodity value component only because existed previously as capital element
     - Expended constant capital replaced by portion of commodity-value it adds to product
     - Double meaning: Goes into cost-price as value-replacing capital; forms commodity-value element because value of expended capital
  
  2. **Variable Capital Portion (100v)**: Newly produced value portion
     - Created by 666⅔ working-days
     - One part (100v) replaces advanced variable capital (labour-power price)
     - Advanced capital-value does NOT go into creation of new value
     - In production process, labour-power acts as creator of value
     - Value of labour-power in advanced capital replaced by living value-creating labour-power

#### The Reversed Appearance
- **Capitalist Standpoint**: Advanced capital of £100 represents money expression of value produced in 333⅓ working-days
- **But**: This value (purchased labour-power) does NOT form part of actually functioning productive capital
- **Instead**: Living labour-power takes its place in production process
- **At 100% Exploitation**: Labour-power expended during 666⅔ working-days adds £200 new value to product
- **Capitalist Perception**: £100 figures as capital invested in wages, price of labour performed during 666⅔ days
- **Calculation**: £100 ÷ 666⅔ = 3s. as price of ten-hour working-day = value of 5 hours' labour product

#### The False Equivalence
- **Capital Advanced Comparison**:
  - I. £500 = £400 capital in means of production (price) + £100 capital in labour (price of 666⅔ working-days/wages)
  - II. £600 = £500 cost-price (£400 price of means + £100 price of 666⅔ working-days) + £100 surplus-value
- **Capitalist Perspective**: Variable capital invested in labour-power differs from constant capital only by serving as payment for different material element of production
- **Functional Reality**: Does NOT serve different purpose in creating commodity-value or capital self-expansion

#### The Disappearing Distinction
- **Cost-Price View**: Both constant and variable capital portions appear as prices of consumed elements (means of production and labour)
- **Reappearance**: Both prices reappearing in cost-price just as figured in capital advanced
- **Visible Values**: Only finished, existing values (portions of advanced capital going into commodity value) visible
- **Hidden Element**: Element creating new values not visible
- **Result**: Distinction between constant and variable capital disappears in cost-price

#### Cost-Price's Double Meaning
1. **First Meaning**: Portion of commodity-value (£500 of £600) replacing advanced capital (£500)
2. **Second Meaning**: This commodity-value component exists only because existed previously as cost-price of employed production elements (means of production and labour), i.e., as advanced capital
- **Conclusion**: Capital-value reappears as commodity cost-price because and insofar as expended as capital-value

### IV. Fixed vs. Circulating Capital in Cost-Price

#### The Material Distinction
- **Pre-Production**: Instruments of labour value = £1,200
- **Post-Production**: £20 as part of commodity value (depreciation) + £1,180 as remaining value of instruments
- **Implication**: £1,180 remains as element of productive capital, not commodity-capital
- **Complete Consumption**: Materials of production and wages entirely consumed; full value goes into commodity value

#### Turnover Relation
- **Various Components**: Assume different forms of fixed and circulating capital in relation to turnover
- **Cost-Price Formation**: Only distinction appreciable = fixed vs. circulating capital
- **Replacement Requirement**: Cost-price must buy back these materially different production elements

## Key Concepts

- **Cost-Price (k)**: Capitalist's cost of production; c + v; what commodity costs the capitalist
- **Surplus-Value (s)**: Portion of value not costing capitalist anything; unpaid labour
- **Cost-Price Formula**: C = k + s (commodity-value = cost-price + surplus-value)
- **Capital Expenditure vs. Labour Expenditure**: Different measurement bases for capitalist vs. actual cost
- **Advanced Capital**: Capital invested before production; appears as wage payment
- **Functioning Capital**: Living labour-power creating value in production process
- **Fixed Capital**: Portion of capital transferring value gradually through wear and tear
- **Circulating Capital**: Portion of capital entirely consumed and transferring full value to product
- **Value Transfer vs. Value Creation**: Constant capital transfers value; variable capital enables value creation
- **Fetishized Appearance**: Cost-price creates illusion that capital itself produces surplus-value

---

## Key Takeaways

1. **Cost-Price as Capitalist Category**: Represents capitalist's expenditure, not actual production cost measured by labour

2. **The Great Obscuration**: Cost-price grouping (c + v) hides the distinction between constant capital (value transfer) and variable capital (value creation source)

3. **Dual Origin of Cost-Price**: Contains both previously existing value (constant capital) and newly created value portion (replacing variable capital)

4. **The Variable Capital Illusion**: Wages appear as price of labour itself rather than price of labour-power, obscuring exploitation

5. **Functional vs. Material Distinction**: From value creation perspective, constant and variable capital serve same function in cost-price; only materially different production elements

6. **Fixed Capital Complexity**: Only partially consumed fixed capital appears in commodity value; remainder stays in productive capital form

7. **Circulating Capital Completeness**: Fully consumed circulating capital (materials, wages) entirely transfers value to product

8. **The Reversal**: Under capitalist production, the true relationship (capital expends labour to create surplus-value) appears reversed (capital itself creates value)

9. **Practical Reality**: Cost-price category not mere accounting; continually manifest in reproduction process where capital must repurchase production elements

10. **Theoretical Foundation**: This chapter establishes basis for Volume III's analysis of profit, interest, rent as transformed forms of surplus-value
