# Chapter 23: Simple Reproduction

## Core Idea
Reproduction is the continuous renewal of the production process, which every society must perform. Under capitalism, reproduction takes a capitalist form: it reproduces not just commodities but capital itself — self-expanding value. Simple reproduction maintains production at the same scale, consuming all surplus-value.

## Frameworks Introduced

### The Necessity of Continuous Reproduction
- **Universal principle**: "A society can no more cease to produce than it can cease to consume"
- **Periodic process**: Production must go through the same phases repeatedly
- **Connected whole**: Production must be viewed as a flowing, connected process
- **Social requirement**: Every society must reproduce its conditions of production

### Conditions of Reproduction
- **Material requirement**: Society must constantly reconvert part of products into means of production
- **Replacement principle**: Must replace consumed instruments, raw materials, auxiliary substances
- **Productive consumption**: A portion of yearly product is destined for production, not individual consumption
- **Form specificity**: If production is capitalist, reproduction is capitalist

### Capitalist Reproduction
- **Purpose**: "To reproduce as capital – i.e., as self-expanding value – the value advanced"
- **Capitalist identity**: "It is only because his money constantly functions as capital that the economic guise of a capitalist attaches to a man"
- **Revenue form**: Surplus-value acquires the form of revenue flowing from capital
- **Continuity**: Simple reproduction is repetition of production on the old scale

### The Capitalist Consumption Fund
- **Simple reproduction condition**: Surplus-value serves only as capitalist's consumption fund
- **Periodic spending**: Revenue spent as periodically as it is gained
- **Character change**: Continuity gives new character to the production process
- **Worker's role**: Labourer produces both surplus-value (for capitalist consumption) and variable capital (for their own wages)

### The Wage Fund Myth
- **Economists' formula**: Wages as a share in the product itself
- **Reality**: "What flows back to the labourer in the shape of wages is a portion of the product that is continuously reproduced by him"
- **Critique**: The wage fund is not a pre-existing fund but is reproduced by the worker's own labour
- **Implication**: Workers reproduce the capital that pays their wages before receiving it

### The Continuous Process
- **Prelude repetition**: Purchase of labour-power is constantly repeated
- **Payment timing**: Worker is paid after expending labour-power and producing surplus-value
- **Employment condition**: Worker's employment lasts only as long as they reproduce the variable capital fund
- **Capital circulation**: The capitalist consumes surplus-value while reproducing capital

## Key Concepts
- **Simple Reproduction**: Reproduction on the same scale, consuming all surplus-value
- **Reproduction**: Continuous renewal of the production process
- **Means of Production**: Instruments, raw materials, auxiliary substances for production
- **Productive Consumption**: Use of products as inputs for further production
- **Revenue**: Periodic flow of surplus-value from capital
- **Variable Capital Fund**: The portion of capital advanced for wages, reproduced by workers
- **Wage Fund Myth**: False idea that wages come from a pre-existing capital fund

## Key Takeaways
1. Reproduction is the continuous renewal of production, necessary for all societies
2. Under capitalism, reproduction reproduces capital (self-expanding value) not just commodities
3. Simple reproduction maintains production at the same scale by consuming all surplus-value
4. The capitalist consumes surplus-value while the worker reproduces the capital that pays wages
5. The wage fund is reproduced by workers' labour, not a pre-existing capital pool
6. Continuous reproduction gives production its capitalist character
