# Chapter 5: Economy in the Employment of Constant Capital

## Core Idea

This chapter analyzes how capitalists increase the rate of profit through economy in the employment of constant capital. Marx demonstrates that while surplus-value originates solely from variable capital, the rate of profit (s/C) can be increased by reducing the value of constant capital (c) relative to total capital, even with a given mass and rate of surplus-value. The chapter systematically examines five major sources of such economy: (1) general savings from large-scale production, (2) savings achieved at labourers' expense, (3) economy in constant capital use, (4) utilization of production excretions (waste), and (5) economy through inventions. Each source reveals how capitalist production both develops social productive forces and squanders human life, illustrating the contradictory nature of capital accumulation.

## Frameworks Introduced

### I. General Principles of Constant Capital Economy

#### Absolute Surplus-Value and Constant Capital Relation
- **Working-Day Prolongation**: Extending surplus-labour while variable capital unchanged (same labourers, same nominal wages)
- **Relative Value Effect**: Reduces relative value of constant capital compared to total and variable capital
- **Fixed Capital Independence**: Volume of fixed capital (buildings, machinery) unchanged whether serving 16 or 12 hour labour-process
- **Fixed Capital Reproduction**: Value reproduced in fewer turnover periods; time for required advance to make certain profit abbreviated
- **Overtime Pay**: Prolongation increases profit even if overtime paid or better paid than normal hours
- **Historical Motivation**: Mounting need to increase fixed capital in modern industry = main reason capitalists lengthen working-day

#### Constant Working-Day Conditions
- **Labourer Increase**: Requires more labourers and, to extent, more fixed capital (buildings, machinery) to exploit greater labour quantity
- **Relative Surplus-Value**: If intensity/productivity increase, more relative surplus-value produced
- **Circulating Capital Increase**: In raw material-using branches, more raw material processed in given time → circulating constant capital portion increases
- **Machinery Increase**: More machinery set in motion by same labourers → fixed capital portion increases
- **Contradictory Effect**: Surplus-value increase accompanied by constant capital increase; greater exploitation requires greater capital outlay → profit rate reduced on one hand, increased on other

#### Overhead Costs and Working-Day
- **Fixed Overhead**: Supervision costs less for 500 men working 18 hours than 750 working 12 hours
- **Factory Example**: "Expense of working a factory 10 hours almost equals that of working it 12" (Factory Inspector Report, October 1848)
- **Taxes, Insurance, Depreciation**: State/municipal taxes, fire insurance, permanent employee wages, machinery depreciation remain unchanged regardless of working-time length
- **Production Decrease Effect**: As production decreases, these expenses rise relative to profit

#### Fixed Capital Reproduction Time
- **Practical Determination**: Not by mere lifetime, but by labour-process duration during which they serve and wear out
- **Overtime Impact**: If labourers work 18 instead of 12 hours = 3 extra days/week → 1 week stretched into 1½ weeks, 2 years into 3
- **Unpaid Overtime**: If overtime unpaid, labourers give away gratis 1 week out of every 3 and 1 year out of every 3
- **Reproduction Speed**: Value reproduction speeded up 50%, accomplished in ⅔ usual time

#### Analytical Assumptions
- **Given Mass and Rate**: Analysis proceeds from assumption that mass and rate of surplus-value are given
- **Focus**: Economy in production conditions (co-operation, division of labour, machinery) from large-scale production

### II. Large-Scale Production and Social Labour

#### Economy from Concentration
- **Social Labour Conditions**: Production conditions serve as social, socially combined labour conditions
- **Aggregate Labourer**: Conditions consumed in process by aggregate labourer instead of small fractions by disconnected labourers
- **Examples**:
  - Large factory with 1-2 central motors: Cost doesn't increase proportionally to horse-power/sphere of activity
  - Transmission equipment: Cost doesn't grow proportionally to number of working machines
  - Machine frames: Don't become dearer proportionally to number of tools employed
- **Additional Savings**: Fuel, lighting, storage buildings, etc.

#### Waste Utilization
- **Excretions of Production**: So-called waste from industry/agriculture returned to production cycle
- **Excretions of Consumption**: Natural waste from human body, clothing remains (rags), etc.
- **Chemical Industry**: Utilizes by-products wasted in small-scale production (iron filings from machinery manufacture returned as raw material)
- **Agriculture Importance**: Excretions of consumption most important for agriculture
- **Capitalist Waste**: Enormous waste in capitalist economy (e.g., London: 4.5 million people's excrement contaminates Thames at heavy expense)
- **Requirements for Re-employment**:
  - Large quantities (only available in large-scale production)
  - Improved machinery (converting previously useless materials to new production states)
  - Scientific progress (chemistry revealing useful properties)
- **Small-Scale Exception**: Great savings also in small-scale agriculture (Lombardy, southern China, Japan), but at cost of prodigal human labour-power use

#### Value vs. Use-Value in Constant Capital
- **Profit Rate Condition**: If surplus-value given, profit rate can be increased only by reducing value of constant capital required for commodity production
- **Use-Value Priority**: In production, it's constant capital's use-value, not exchange-value, that matters
- **Labour Absorption**: Quantity of labour flax can absorb in spinnery depends on quantity, not value (given productivity level)
- **Machine Assistance**: Assistance to 3 labourers depends on use-value as machine, not value
- **Technical Development**: At different technical levels, bad machine may be expensive, good machine may be cheap

#### Inter-Industry Dependence
- **Productivity Spillover**: Development of productive power in one industry (iron, coal, machinery, architecture) reduces value of means of production in other industries (textiles, agriculture)
- **Commodity as Means of Production**: Product of one industry enters another as means of production
- **Price Dependence**: Greater/lesser price depends on productivity of labour in originating industry
- **Capital Value Effect**: Cheaper means of production reduces constant capital value, increases profit rate

#### Social Nature of Economy
- **Social Labour Product**: Economy arises from social nature of labour (division of labour, intellectual labour development)
- **External Department**: Development of productive power in supplier industries lowers constant capital value for purchasing capitalists
- **System Advantage**: Capitalist utilizes entire system of social division of labour

### III. Savings In Labour Conditions At The Expense Of The Labourers

#### Capitalist Perspective on Economy
- **Two Types of Economy**:
  1. **Direct Labour Economy**: Saving in paid labour of own labourers
  2. **Constant Capital Economy**: Greatest possible appropriation of others' unpaid labour with minimal expense
- **Source Distinction**: First from combined labour within branch; second from productivity of social labour in constant capital production
- **Scale Requirement**: Constant capital economy requires even greater labour combination

#### Means of Production Value vs. Use-Value
- **Exchange-Value Irrelevance**: For labour absorption, exchange-value of machinery/buildings/raw materials immaterial
- **Use-Value Essence**: What matters = quantity technically required + suitability (good machinery, good raw materials)
- **Profit Rate Dependence**: Partly depends on raw material quality
- **Waste Reduction**: Good material produces less waste; less raw material needed to absorb same labour quantity
- **Surplus-Value Effect**: Bad raw materials require more labourer time to process same quantity → reduces surplus-labour (wages constant)
- **Accumulation Impact**: Affects reproduction and accumulation, which depend more on productivity than labour amount employed

#### Capitalist Fanaticism
- **Insistence on Economy**: Fanatical insistence that nothing lost/wasted, means of production consumed only as production requires
- **Worker Dependence**: Depends on labourer skill/intelligence and capitalist discipline for combined labour
- **Social System Dependence**: Discipline becomes superfluous under social system where labourers work for own account (already superfluous in piece-work)
- **Adulteration**: Economy also through production element adulteration = principal means of lowering constant capital value relative to variable capital
- **Cheating Element**: Sale of adulterated elements above value adds cheating element to profit
- **German Industry**: Adulteration plays essential part; maxim: send good samples first, then inferior goods afterward

#### Independence from Use-Value Type
- **Universal Application**: Economy raising profit rate by lowering constant capital value independent of industry type (luxuries, necessities, means of production)
- **Surplus-Value Assumption**: Surplus-value and rate of surplus-value assumed as given for analysis
- **Determination**: Relation of surplus-value to total capital (determining profit rate) depends exclusively on constant capital value, not use-value composition

#### Absolute vs. Relative Cheapening
- **Absolute Increase Possible**: Relative cheapening doesn't exclude absolute aggregate value increase
- **Productive Power Growth**: Absolute volume of means of production employment grows tremendously with productive power development and production level growth
- **Dual Source**: Economy in constant capital use partly from social nature of directly productive labour, partly from developing productivity in supplier industries
- **Social Labour Product**: When viewing total labour to total capital (not capitalist X to Y), economy = product of social productive force development
- **Capitalist Benefit**: Capitalist X enjoys productivity advantages from own establishment AND others

#### Capitalist Conception
- **Alienation**: Labourer appears indifferent to economical use of material labour conditions
- **Money Representation**: Means of production represent only capitalist's money (like Roman debtor's body representing creditor's money)
- **Use-Value Contact**: Labourer contacts means of production only as use-values in direct production process
- **Value Indifference**: Increase/decrease in means of production value has little bearing on labourer-capitalist relations
- **Capitalist Double Standard**: Capitalist views differently when means of production gain in value and reduce profit rate

#### Historical Note on Co-operation
- **Social Labour View**: Labourer looks at social nature of labour, combination with others for common purpose, as alien power
- **Alien Property**: Condition of realising combination = alien property; dissipation indifferent to labourer if not compelled to economise
- **Rochdale Exception**: Different in labourer-owned factories (e.g., Rochdale)

#### Inter-Industry Productivity
- **General Interconnection**: Productivity of labour in one industry as lever for cheapening/improving means of production in another, raising profit rate
- **Circulation Concealment**: Fact that capitalist buys others' labour product with own labourers' product, disposes of others' labourers' product by appropriating own, concealed by circulation process
- **Capitalist Thirst**: Thirst for profits + competition compelling cheapest production make constant capital economy appear peculiar to capitalist mode
- **Capitalist Function**: Economy appears as function of capitalist, not social labour development

#### Contradictory Nature of Capitalist Production
- **Productive Force Development**: Capitalist mode promotes social labour productive power development
- **Human Waste**: Also whips on to economy in constant capital use AND prodigious dissipation of labourer's life and health
- **Living Conditions**: Production process conditions = labourer's active living process conditions; economy in these = profit rate raising method
- **Overwork**: Transformation of labourer into work-horse = surplus-value production speeding method
- **Examples of Economy**:
  - Overcrowding close/unsanitary premises (space saving)
  - Crowding dangerous machinery without safety devices
  - Neglecting safety rules in pernicious processes (mining)
  - Absence of provisions for humane/agreeable/bearable production
  - Capitalist view: Such provisions = useless senseless waste
- **Prodigality**: Despite niggardliness, too prodigal with human material; conversely, through commerce/competition, prodigal with material means, losing for society what gained for individual capitalist

#### Economy's Double Character
- **Living Labour Reduction**: Tends to reduce direct living labour employment to necessary labour, cutting labour required for commodity production by exploiting social labour productiveness
- **Constant Capital Minimization**: Tends to employ this minimized labour under most economical conditions, reducing constant capital value to minimum
- **Necessary Labour Time**: Determines commodity value; capital realizes this determination while reducing necessary labour-time for given commodity
- **Price Effect**: Commodity price lowered to minimum as each portion of required labour reduced to minimum

### IV. Savings In Labour Conditions At The Expense Of The Labourers (Continued)

#### Coal Mines: Neglect of Safety
- **Competition Effect**: Among coal-owners/proprietors → only sufficient outlay to overcome obvious physical difficulties
- **Labourer Competition**: Among colliers (more numerous than work required) → willing to accept danger/noxious influences for slightly higher wages
- **Result**: Large proportion of pits worked with imperfect drainage/ventilation, ill-constructed shafts/gearing, incompetent engineers
- **Statistics**: Weekly average of 15 men lost in English collieries (1860); 8,466 killed 1852-61 (underreported)
- **Inspection Effect**: Accidents decreased since inspection system despite limited powers/insufficient numbers
- **Human Sacrifice Cause**: Inordinate avarice of mine owners; often only one shaft (no escape if obstructed)

#### Factories: Safety Device Resistance
- **Historical Resistance**: Manufacturers resisted legal safety device requirements
- **Example**: October 1855 Leonard Horner complaint about resistance to horizontal shaft safety devices despite fatal accidents, low cost, no production interference
- **Legal Support**: Manufacturers seconded by unpaid justices of peace (often manufacturers themselves)
- **Legal Subversion**: Superior Judge Campbell on justices' verdict: "not an interpretation... it is a repeal of the Act of Parliament"
- **Warning Neglect**: Many factories didn't warn labourers before machinery start-up; fingers/hands occupied with machinery during non-operation → continual accidents
- **Manufacturers' Association**: National Association for Amendment of Factory Laws (March 1855) collected £50,000+ to fight inspectors
- **Court Costs**: £2 per horse-power assessment for union membership vs. £9 1s. for protective shields
- **Union Purpose**: Organized to oppose 1844 law prescribing protection; manufacturers ignored law 1844-1854
- **Palmerston Action**: 1854 inspectors instructed to enforce law; manufacturers formed association
- **Grey Compromise**: 1855 Minister offered nominal safety appliances compromise; Association indignantly rejected
- **Engineering Support**: William Fairbairn supported capitalist freedom against safety requirements
- **Persecution**: Leonard Horner persecuted/maligned by manufacturers
- **Legal Victory**: 1856 Act through Wilson Patten services: deprived labourers of special protection, referred to common courts (mockery due to lawsuit cost), made manufacturer lawsuits almost impossible via expert testimony clause
- **Result**: Rapid accident increase; 21% increase May-October 1858 vs. preceding half-year; 36.7% preventable
- **Long-Term Decrease**: 1858-59 accidents 29% below 1845-46 despite 20% labourer increase
- **Reasons**: New machinery with built-in safety devices; heavy damage awards for lost arms upheld by courts

#### General Workplace Conditions
- **Space Economy**: Economy of space crowds labourers into close quarters
- **Ventilation Neglect**: Also economy in ventilation means
- **Sanitation**: Economy in means of sanitation/cleanliness
- **Accommodation**: Means of personal comfort/health/safety for labourers

### V. Utilisation Of The Excretions Of Production

#### Types of Excretions
- **Production Excretions**: Waste from industry/agriculture
- **Consumption Excretions**: Natural waste from human body, clothing remains (rags)

#### Chemical Industry Example
- **By-Product Utilization**: Utilizes own waste for new uses
- **Inter-Industry Waste**: Utilizes waste from many other industries
- **Gas-Tar**: Formerly almost useless → aniline dyes, alizarin, drugs

#### Flax Industry Problem
- **Waste Issue**: Great waste at water scutch mills; 28-30% loss with shameful preparation
- **Solution**: Water steeping + good machine scutching reduces disadvantage considerably
- **Tow Waste**: Valuable tow fell by wayside; sometimes used by scutchers for home fires

#### Wool Industry Success
- **Shoddy Trade**: Once decried preparation of waste/woollen rags for re-manufacture; prejudice entirely subsided
- **Economic Growth**: Became important branch of Yorkshire woollen trade
- **Price Increase**: Woollen rags: 30 years prior = £4 4s./ton; recent years = £44/ton
- **Technical Advancement**: Means found for utilizing mixed cotton/wool fabrics by destroying cotton, leaving wool intact
- **Employment**: Thousands engaged in shoddy manufacture
- **Consumer Impact**: Enabled purchase of fair/average quality cloth at moderate price
- **Quality Cost**: Shoddy clothes wear out in ⅓ previous time, threadbare in ⅙ time
- **By 1862**: Rejuvenated shoddy = ⅓ of entire English wool consumption

#### Silk Industry
- **Genuine Raw Silk**: Consumption decreased 1839-1862
- **Silk Waste**: Consumption doubled
- **Machinery Improvement**: Enabled useful silk production from otherwise worthless waste

#### Chemical Industry Striking Example
- **Comprehensive Utilization**: Utilizes not only own waste but many others' industry waste
- **Gas-Tar Transformation**: Converted formerly almost useless gas-tar into valuable chemicals

#### Economy Types Distinction
- **Waste Utilization**: Re-employment of excretions
- **Waste Prevention**: Reduction of excretions to minimum, immediate maximum utilization of raw/auxiliary materials

#### Waste Reduction Factors
- **Machinery Quality**: Depends on machinery quality for reducing waste
- **Auxiliary Materials**: Economy in oil, soap depends on mechanical part machining/polishing
- **Raw Material Quality**: Good machinery/tools produce less waste from raw materials
- **Extractive Industry**: Raw material quality depends on extractive industry/agriculture development
- **Processing Improvement**: Raw material improvement through better pre-manufacture processes

#### Historical Example: Grain Milling
- **Parmentier Demonstration**: French milling art improved since Louis XIV era
- **Efficiency Gain**: New mills produce up to 50% more bread from same grain vs. old mills
- **Consumption Decrease**: Parisian annual consumption: Louis XIV era = 4 setiers → 3 → 2 → currently 1⅓ setiers (≈342 lbs/capita)
- **Regional Improvement**: Perche region: crude granite/trap rock mills rebuilt with good La Ferté millstones, double grinding, circular milling sack motion → ⅙ output increase
- **Roman Comparison**: Explains Roman high flour prices relative to grain (Pliny: flour = 40, 48, or 96 as per modius)
- **Cause**: Imperfect milling/bread-making methods

### VI. Economy Through Inventions

#### Large-Scale Requirement
- **Indispensable Requirement**: Employment of labour conditions on large scale for mechanical/chemical invention utilization without increasing commodity price
- **Co-operative Consumption**: Only large-scale production permits savings from co-operative productive consumption
- **Combined Labourer Experience**: Discoveries/reveals where/how of saving, simplest application methods, overcoming practical frictions

#### Universal vs. Co-operative Labour
- **Universal Labour**: All scientific labour, discovery, invention
- **Dependency**: Partly on living co-operation, partly on utilization of past labour
- **Co-operative Labour**: Direct co-operation of individuals
- **Interaction**: Both play roles, flow into each other, but also differentiated

#### Economic Advantages of Inventions
1. **First Model Cost vs. Reproduction Cost**: Great difference between first model of new machine and reproduction cost
2. **Establishment Cost**: Far greater cost of operating establishment based on new invention vs. later establishments (ex suis ossibus)
   - **Historical Reality**: Trail-blazers generally go bankrupt; later purchasers make money
   - **Irony**: Most worthless/miserable capitalists draw greatest profit from universal labour spirit developments

#### Fixed Capital Utilization
- **Working Hours**: "Since in all factories there is a very large amount of fixed capital in buildings and machinery, the greater the number of hours that machinery can be kept at work the greater will be the return" (Factory Inspector Report, October 31, 1858)

## Key Concepts

- **Economy in Constant Capital**: Reducing value of constant capital relative to total capital to increase profit rate
- **Large-Scale Production Economy**: Savings from social labour, combined use of means of production
- **Overhead Cost Economy**: Fixed costs (supervision, taxes, insurance) distributed over more production hours
- **Waste Utilization**: Converting production/consumption excretions into new production elements
- **Adulteration Economy**: Cheapening constant capital through production element adulteration
- **Safety Economy**: Neglecting labourer safety/health as means of reducing costs
- **Invention Economy**: Savings from mechanical/chemical inventions in large-scale production
- **Inter-Industry Productivity**: Productivity gains in supplier industries reducing constant capital costs for purchasers
- **Social Labour Development**: Economy arising from division of labour, intellectual labour development

---

## Key Takeaways

1. **Given Surplus-Value**: With mass and rate of surplus-value given, profit rate can be increased by reducing constant capital value relative to total capital

2. **Working-Day Prolongation**: Extending working hours without increasing fixed capital increases profit rate by reducing constant capital's relative value

3. **Large-Scale Savings**: Concentration of means of production yields savings on buildings, power, machinery, fuel, lighting through social labour

4. **Waste Utilization**: Capitalist production extends utilization of waste from industry, agriculture, and consumption, converting it into new production elements

5. **Inter-Industry Dependence**: Productivity development in one industry (iron, coal, machinery) cheapens means of production for other industries, raising their profit rates

6. **Capitalist Alienation**: Labourer indifferent to economical use of production means; capitalist views constant capital as own money, alien to labourer

7. **Human Cost of Economy**: Capitalist economy in constant capital achieved through prodigious dissipation of labourers' lives, health, and development

8. **Safety Neglect**: Capitalists historically resisted safety measures, preferring to risk labourer lives to save costs, with human sacrifices due to inordinate avarice

9. **Invention Benefits**: Savings from inventions flow to later adopters, not innovators; most worthless capitalists often profit most from social labour developments

10. **Dual Character**: Capitalist production simultaneously develops social productive forces and squanders human resources
