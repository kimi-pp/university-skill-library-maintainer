# Chapter 16: The Turnover of Variable Capital

## Core Idea

This chapter extends the analysis of capital turnover from Chapter 15 by focusing specifically on the variable capital component, demonstrating how the frequency of turnover directly impacts the annual rate of surplus-value. Marx reveals that while the mass of surplus-value produced may be identical between capitals, the annual rate of surplus-value varies dramatically based on turnover speed, creating the illusion that circulation processes independently influence surplus-value production. The key insight is that only the portion of variable capital actually employed in the labour-process produces surplus-value, not the portion merely advanced but idle.

---

## Frameworks Introduced

### I. The Annual Rate of Surplus Value

#### Definition and Calculation
- **Annual Rate of Surplus-Value**: The ratio of total annual surplus-value to the advanced variable capital
- **Formula**: (Total Annual Surplus-Value / Advanced Variable Capital) × 100%
- **Component Relationship**: Annual rate = Rate of surplus-value per turnover period × Number of annual turnovers

#### Numerical Illustration (Capital A)
- **Setup**: Circulating capital = £2,500 (£2,000 constant, £500 variable)
- **Turnover Period**: 5 weeks (4 weeks working, 1 week circulation)
- **Capital I**: £2,000 (£1,600 constant + £400 variable) - constantly employed
- **Capital II**: £500 (£400 constant + £100 variable) - released during circulation
- **Annual Product**: £25,000 (50 weeks × £500 weekly investment)
- **Annual Turnovers**: 10 times
- **Variable Capital in Annual Product**: £5,000 (one-fifth of £25,000)
- **Annual Rate of Surplus-Value**: 1,000% (£5,000s / £500v × 100%)

#### Surplus-Value Production Mechanics
- **Weekly Variable Capital**: £100 produces £100 surplus-value (100% rate)
- **5-Week Turnover**: £500 variable capital produces £500 surplus-value
- **Annual Scale**: £5,000 variable capital employed produces £5,000 surplus-value
- **Rate Relationship**: 100% per period × 10 turnovers = 1,000% annually

### II. Contrasting Capital Turnover Scenarios

#### Capital A (Rapid Turnover)
- **Advanced Variable Capital**: £500
- **Turnover Frequency**: 10 times per year
- **Annual Surplus-Value**: £5,000
- **Annual Rate**: 1,000%
- **Characteristics**: Small advances, frequent recovery, rapid reinvestment

#### Capital B (Slow Turnover)
- **Advanced Variable Capital**: £5,000
- **Turnover Frequency**: 1 time per year (50-week period)
- **Annual Surplus-Value**: £5,000 (same mass as A)
- **Annual Rate**: 100%
- **Characteristics**: Large advances, single annual cycle, delayed realization

#### The Paradox Resolved
- **Identical Labour Exploitation**: Both employ £100 variable capital weekly at 100% rate
- **Equal Mass of Surplus-Value**: £5,000 annually for both
- **Divergent Annual Rates**: 1,000% vs 100%
- **Circulation Illusion**: Creates appearance that surplus-value depends on circulation factors
- **Historical Impact**: This phenomenon routed the Ricardian school in the 1820s

### III. Advanced vs. Employed Variable Capital

#### The Critical Distinction
- **Advanced Variable Capital**: Total capital set aside for wages over a period
- **Employed Variable Capital**: Portion actually functioning in the labour-process at any moment
- **Non-Employed Portion**: Advanced but idle capital awaiting future employment

#### Capital A Example
- **Advanced for 5 Weeks**: £500 total
- **Employed Weekly**: £100 (one-fifth of advanced)
- **Idling**: £400 advanced but not yet employed
- **Function**: Idle portion has no influence on value or surplus-value formation

#### Capital B Example
- **Advanced for 5 Weeks**: £5,000 total
- **Employed Weekly**: £100 (one-fiftieth of advanced)
- **Idling**: £4,900 advanced but not employed
- **Ratio**: Only 2% of advanced capital is actively producing surplus-value

### IV. The Law of Surplus-Value Production

#### Fundamental Principle
- **Active Capital Only**: Only variable capital actually employed in labour-process produces surplus-value
- **Rate Given**: With constant rate of surplus-value, equal functioning variable capitals produce equal surplus-value
- **Time Measurement**: Labour-process measured by time (working-days, weeks, hours)

#### Quantitative Relationships
- **Formula**: Quantity of surplus-value = Rate of surplus-value × Magnitude of employed variable capital
- **Labour-Power Scale**: Determined by number of workers × price per worker × time period
- **Equal Conditions**: Same working-day length, same division into necessary/surplus labour

#### Turnover Period Determinants
- **Working Period**: Duration of production process
- **Circulation Period**: Time for commodity to be sold and capital recovered
- **Combined Effect**: Differences in turnover periods create differences between advanced and employed capital ratios

## Key Concepts

- **Annual Rate of Surplus-Value**: The yearly measure of surplus-value production relative to advanced variable capital
- **Turnover Number**: How many times capital completes its circuit in a year
- **Advanced Variable Capital**: Total capital allocated for labour-power over an extended period
- **Employed Variable Capital**: Capital actually active in production at any given moment
- **Idling Capital**: Portion of advanced capital not currently employed in production
- **Realization Period**: Time between surplus-value production and its conversion to money
- **Circulation Influence Illusion**: Apparent effect of circulation on surplus-value production
- **Capital A vs Capital B**: Contrasting turnover scenarios with identical exploitation but divergent annual rates

---

## Key Takeaways

1. **Turnover Frequency Multiplies Surplus-Value Rate**: The annual rate of surplus-value equals the per-period rate multiplied by the number of annual turnovers (100% × 10 = 1,000%)

2. **Mass vs. Rate Distinction**: Two capitals can produce identical masses of surplus-value annually while exhibiting vastly different annual rates of surplus-value

3. **The Circulation Illusion**: Rapid turnover creates the false appearance that surplus-value production depends on circulation factors, not just exploitation of labour-power

4. **Only Employed Capital Produces**: Variable capital produces surplus-value only when and to the extent that it is actually functioning in the labour-process

5. **Advanced Capital Includes Idle Reserves**: Capital advanced for future periods sits idle and contributes nothing to current surplus-value production

6. **Equal Exploitation, Divergent Rates**: Capitals exploiting identical quantities of labour-power at identical rates can achieve dramatically different annual surplus-value rates through turnover differences

7. **Practical Capitalist Calculation**: The average calculation method (annual product value / advanced capital) serves practical purposes but does not exactly express all real circumstances

8. **Historical Significance**: This phenomenon contributed to the decline of the Ricardian school's understanding of value theory

9. **Turnover Period Components**: Differences arise from working period length, circulation period duration, or both combined

10. **Law of Production**: Equal functioning variable capitals, at equal rates of surplus-value, produce equal quantities of surplus-value regardless of their advanced amounts
