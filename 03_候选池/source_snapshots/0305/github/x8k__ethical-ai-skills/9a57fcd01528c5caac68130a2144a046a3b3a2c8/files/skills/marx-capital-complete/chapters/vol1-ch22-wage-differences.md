# Chapter 22: National Differences of Wages

## Core Idea
Wage differences between nations result from the same factors that cause wage fluctuations within a single country, applied internationally. National differences in labour intensity, productivity, and conditions of life create variations in the value of labour-power and thus in wage levels.

## Frameworks Introduced

### Factors Determining National Wage Differences
- **Prime necessaries**: Price and extent of basic goods (food, clothing, housing) in each country
- **Historical development**: Traditional standards of living and consumption patterns
- **Training costs**: Expenses of developing labour-power (education, apprenticeship)
- **Family labour**: Role of women and children in production and reproduction
- **Labour productivity**: Output per worker in each country
- **Extensive magnitude**: Working day length
- **Intensive magnitude**: Labour intensity per hour

### Comparison Methodology
- **Standardization**: Reduce average day-wages to uniform working day for comparison
- **Conversion**: Translate time-wages into piece-wages for accurate productivity measurement
- **Adjustment**: Account for national differences in working hours and output

### National Labour Intensity
- **Average intensity**: Each country has a baseline labour intensity below which work is not normal quality
- **International scale**: National averages form a scale measured against universal labour unit
- **Effect**: More intense national labour produces more value in the same time
- **Consequence**: More value expressed in more money

### Law of Value Internationally Modified
- **Productivity effect**: More productive national labour is treated as more intense on world market
- **Competition limitation**: Only until competition forces price reduction to value level
- **Result**: Advanced capitalist countries can sell commodities above their value internationally
- **Historical development**: Capitalist production development raises national intensity and productivity above international level

### Quantities of Commodities vs. Value
- **Different quantities**: Countries produce different amounts of the same commodity in same working time
- **Value vs. quantity**: More productive labour produces more commodities but not necessarily more value
- **International exchange**: More productive national labour can command higher prices
- **Tendency**: Richer countries tend to have higher wages and higher productivity

### The So-Called National Wages
- **Calculation**: Total wages paid in a country / Total population
- **Problem**: Doesn't account for working population vs. total population
- **Distortion**: Countries with large non-working populations appear to have higher "national wages"
- **Correction**: Must compare wage per worker, not per capita

## Key Concepts
- **National Wage Differences**: Variations in wage levels between countries
- **Labour Intensity**: Average effort and productivity per worker per country
- **International Scale**: Measurement of national labour against universal standard
- **World Market**: Arena where national labour intensities compete
- **National Averages**: Baseline productivity and intensity for each country
- **Productivity Advantage**: Advanced countries' ability to produce more value

## Key Takeaways
1. National wage differences result from the same factors as wage changes within countries
2. Labour intensity varies nationally and forms an international scale
3. More productive national labour can command higher prices on the world market
4. Comparing wages requires standardization of working hours and output measures
5. National wage statistics must account for working vs. total population
