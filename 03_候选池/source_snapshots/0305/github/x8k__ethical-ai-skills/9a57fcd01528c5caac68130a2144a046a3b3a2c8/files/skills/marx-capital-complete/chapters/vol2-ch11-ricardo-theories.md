# Chapter 11: Theories of Fixed and Circulating Capital — Ricardo

## Core Idea
Ricardo introduces the fixed/circulating capital distinction merely to illustrate exceptions to the rule of value where wage rates affect prices. His analysis suffers from Adam Smith's original confusion between constant/variable capital and fixed/circulating capital categories. Ricardo's logical inconsistency leads him to lump variable capital with circulating capital and to lose sight of the material of labour entirely, thereby obscuring the source of surplus-value and the true nature of capitalist exploitation.

## Frameworks Introduced

### Ricardo's Limited Purpose
- **Purpose**: Introduces distinction only to illustrate exceptions to value rule (wage rate effects on prices)
- **Deferral**: Full discussion reserved for Book III
- **Immaterial juxtaposition**: From outset, presents unclear comparison: "difference in degree of durability of fixed capital, and this variety in the proportions in which the two sorts of capital may be combined"

### Ricardo's Unclear Categories
- **Ambiguity**: Doesn't clearly specify which two sorts of capital he refers to
- **Definition**: "The proportions in which the capital that is to support labour, and the capital that is invested in tools, machinery, and buildings, may be variously combined"
- **Smith's influence**: "Capital that is to support labour" is senseless term from Adam Smith
- **Conflation**: Fixed capital = instruments of labour; circulating capital = capital laid out in labour
- **Double error**: Circulating capital lumped with variable capital (wages) due to Adam Smith's old confusion between circulation process and self-expansion process

### Two Levels of Confusion

#### First Confusion: Equating Different Differences
- **Equal significance error**: Differences in fixed capital durability treated as equally significant as differences from constant/variable capital composition
- **Surplus-value production**: Constant/variable distinction determines surplus-value production
- **Value transfer only**: Fixed capital durability affects only manner of value transfer from means of production to product
- **Circulation aspect**: In circulation process, difference refers only to period of capital renewal or advancement time
- **Surface phenomena**: If only considering accomplished phenomena (not internal machinery), distinctions appear to coincide
- **Profit equalisation**: In surplus-value distribution among different branches, both different advancement periods and different organic compositions contribute to profit rate equalisation and value conversion to prices of production

#### Second Confusion: Mixing Perspectives
- **Circulation perspective**: Instruments of labour = fixed capital; material of labour + wages = circulating capital
- **Labour process perspective**: Means of production (instruments + material) = constant capital; labour-power = variable capital
- **Irrelevance for organic composition**: Organic composition (Book I, Chapter XXIII, 2) doesn't depend on whether constant capital value consists of many instruments and little material or vice versa
- **Ratio importance**: Only ratio of capital in means of production to capital in labour-power matters
- **Circulation perspective irrelevance**: From circulation perspective, immaterial how circulating capital divides between material of labour and wages
- **Category mixing**: From one perspective, material of labour classed with instruments against wages; from other, wages classed with material against instruments

### Ricardo's Missing Category
- **Material of labour disappearance**: Part of capital-value laid out in material of labour (raw and auxiliary materials) doesn't appear on either side in Ricardo
- **Classification problem**: Can't class with fixed capital (circulation mode matches wages); can't class with circulating capital (would abolish Smith's identification of fixed/circulating with constant/variable)
- **Logical instinct**: Ricardo senses this problem, causing material of labour to vanish from his analysis

### Wage Advance Misconception
- **Capitalist jargon**: Capitalist "advances" capital laid out in wages for various periods (weekly, monthly, quarterly)
- **Reality**: Labourer advances labour to capitalist for those periods
- **Payment timing**: Capitalist pays after labour performed, doesn't buy labour in advance
- **Capitalist quid pro quo**: Advance labour gives capitalist is turned into advance of money to labourer
- **Irrelevance**: Doesn't matter that capitalist gets product value back after longer/shorter periods
- **Seller's indifference**: Seller of commodity doesn't care what buyer does with it
- **Cost constancy**: Machine not cheaper because entire value advanced at once; cotton not more expensive because entire value enters product at once

### Ricardo's Variable Capital Error
- **Variable capital characteristic**: Definite, given (constant) part of capital exchanged for self-expanding, value-creating power (labour-power)
- **Value reproduction**: Labour-power not only reproduces its paid value but produces surplus-value
- **Distinctive property**: This property distinguishes variable from constant capital toto coelo
- **Circulation perspective loss**: When variable capital considered only from circulation perspective as circulating capital, its distinctive property disappears
- **Classification effect**: Appears in same category as material of labour, opposed to instruments of labour as fixed capital
- **Surplus-value ignored**: Circumstance converting laid-out sum into capital (surplus-value production) entirely ignored

### Value Reproduction Difference
- **New production**: Value added by wages is newly produced and really reproduced
- **Preservation**: Value added by raw material is preserved, conserved, merely reappears as product value component
- **Circulation perspective**: From fixed/circulating contrast, distinction = value transfer mode
- **Value transfer**: Instruments of labour value enters commodity only partially, replaced by sale only partially, replaced altogether only piecemeal/gradually
- **Full transfer**: Labour-power and subjects of labour (raw materials) values enter commodity entirely, replaced by sale entirely
- **Formal distinction**: In circulation process, one part fixed (piece-meal replacement), other fluent/circulating (bulk replacement)
- **Commonality**: Both cases involve transferring given advanced values to product and their replacement by sale

### Confusion Consequences
- **Bourgeois political economy**: Instinctively clung to Smith's confusion for a century, repeating it without criticism
- **Category loss**: Variable capital no longer distinguished from raw material capital; differs only formally from constant capital (piece-meal vs. lump circulation)
- **Understanding blocked**: Basis for understanding real capitalist movement and exploitation buried
- **Reduction**: All becomes question of reappearance of advanced values, not production of surplus-value

### Ricardo vs. Smith vs. Physiocrats
- **Ricardo's error**: More disturbing in Ricardo than later apologists or Smith himself
- **Consistency**: Ricardo more consistent and incisive in value/surplus-value analysis than Smith
- **Esoteric vs. exoteric**: Ricardo upholds esoteric Adam Smith against exoteric Adam Smith
- **Physiocratic clarity**: No such confusion among Physiocrats; distinction between avances annuelles/primitives refers only to reproduction periods, independent of surplus-value theory
- **Surplus-value source**: Physiocrats attributed surplus-value to agriculture, not capital as such

### Variable Capital Definition
- **Essential point**: Capitalist exchanges definite, given magnitude of value for value-creating power
- **Form independence**: Whether paid in money or means of subsistence doesn't affect definition
- **Mode of existence**: Value advanced exists as money (labourer buys subsistence) or as means of subsistence (consumed directly)
- **Developed capitalism**: Assumes labourer paid in money, monetary system, circulation process
- **Surplus-value source**: Creation of surplus-value and capitalisation of advanced sum has source neither in money-form of wages nor form of wage payment

## Key Concepts
- **Ricardo's Analysis**: Classical economist's treatment of capital categories
- **Value Rule Exceptions**: Cases where wage rates affect prices
- **Durability**: Length of capital's useful life
- **Organic Composition**: Ratio of constant to variable capital
- **Circulation Perspective**: View focusing on value transfer and replacement
- **Labour Process Perspective**: View focusing on production and surplus-value creation
- **Material of Labour**: Raw and auxiliary materials consumed in production
- **Instruments of Labour**: Tools, machinery, buildings used in production
- **Value Transfer**: How capital value moves to product
- **Replacement Periods**: Time between capital renewal

## Key Takeaways
1. Ricardo introduced fixed/circulating distinction only for price analysis, not for understanding capital nature
2. Ricardo inherited and repeated Smith's confusion between constant/variable and fixed/circulating categories
3. Ricardo lumped variable capital (wages) with circulating capital, losing sight of surplus-value source
4. Ricardo's analysis completely omits material of labour category due to classification problems
5. From circulation perspective, fixed/circulating distinction depends only on whether value transfer is piece-meal or bulk
6. From labour process perspective, constant/variable distinction determines surplus-value production
7. Bourgeois political economy clung to this confusion, obscuring understanding of capitalist exploitation
8. Physiocrats maintained clearer separation between reproduction period distinctions and surplus-value theory
9. Ricardo's error is more disturbing because his value analysis was otherwise more consistent than Smith's
10. Variable capital's essence is exchange of given value magnitude for value-creating power (labour-power), regardless of payment form
