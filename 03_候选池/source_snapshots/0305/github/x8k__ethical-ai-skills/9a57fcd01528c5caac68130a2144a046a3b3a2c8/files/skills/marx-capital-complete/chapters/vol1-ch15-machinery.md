# Chapter 15: Machinery and Modern Industry

## Core Idea
Machinery represents a revolution in the mode of production, beginning with the instruments of labour rather than labour-power. Its capitalist application aims to cheapen commodities, shorten necessary labour time, and lengthen surplus labour time — producing surplus-value. Machinery transforms the labour-process from manual to mechanical, creating modern industry.

## Frameworks Introduced

### Machine vs. Tool
- **Not a simple difference**: Machines are not just complex tools (as mathematicians claim)
- **Not about motive power**: Whether powered by human, animal, water, or steam doesn't define the essence
- **Economic distinction**: A machine is a mechanism that, after being set in motion, performs with its tools the same operations formerly done by workers
- **Historical development**: Machinery develops from handicraft tools through manufacture to automated systems

### Three Parts of Machinery
- **Motor Mechanism**: Generates or receives motive power (steam engine, water-wheel, electromagnetic machine)
- **Transmitting Mechanism**: Regulates motion, changes form, distributes to working machines (fly-wheels, shafting, gears, pulleys)
- **Working Machine (Tool)**: The part that seizes and modifies the subject of labour; the revolutionary element

### The Value Transfer Problem
- **Machine Value**: Machinery transfers its value to products piece by piece, over its lifetime
- **Depreciation**: Only the portion of machine value consumed in production is transferred
- **Cheapening Effect**: Machinery cheapens commodities by reducing the labour-time required for their production
- **Contradiction**: While machinery may increase constant capital, it reduces the value of individual commodities

### Machinery and Surplus-Value
- **Purpose**: "To cheapen commodities, and, by shortening that portion of the working day in which the labourer works for himself, to lengthen the other portion that he gives, without an equivalent, to the capitalist"
- **Relative Surplus-Value**: Machinery produces relative surplus-value by reducing necessary labour time
- **Intensification**: Machinery allows capital to extract more surplus-value through increased productivity

### Effects on Labour
- **Skill Degradation**: Machinery deskills workers, reducing labour to simple machine-tending
- **Work Intensification**: Machine pace dictates work speed, increasing labour intensity
- **Unemployment**: Machinery displaces workers, creating a reserve army of labour
- **Factory System**: Machinery enables large-scale production in factories under capitalist control

### The Struggle Against Machinery
- **Worker Resistance**: "It is a self-evident fact that machinery has seized upon the work of children to a quite terrible extent" — workers resist machine introduction
- **Compensation Theories**: Bourgeois economists claim machinery creates new employment (Ricardo, etc.)
- **Reality**: Machinery's immediate effect is displacement; compensation comes later and elsewhere
- **Historical Examples**: Luddites, textile workers' resistance to power looms

### Machinery and the Working Day
- **Extension**: Machinery enables longer working days through night work, shift systems
- **Intensification**: Machine speed determines work pace, increasing output per hour
- **Children's Labour**: Machinery employs children and women, cheapening labour costs

### Large-Scale Industry
- **Characteristics**: System of machinery, factory production, scientific application
- **Continuous Process**: Machinery creates continuous production, unlike intermittent handicraft
- **Social Impact**: Destruction of traditional skills, creation of industrial proletariat
- **Preparation for**: Modern automated industry, computer-controlled production

## Key Concepts
- **Machinery**: System of machines that performs operations formerly done by workers
- **Tool**: Simple implement directly controlled by human labour
- **Modern Industry**: Production based on machinery, the capitalist use of machinery
- **Factory System**: Large-scale production under capitalist control using machinery
- **Machine Components**: Motor, transmitting mechanism, working machine
- **Depreciation**: Gradual transfer of machine value to products
- **Relative Surplus-Value**: Surplus-value from reduced necessary labour via productivity
- **Reserve Army**: Workers displaced by machinery, available for future employment

## Key Takeaways
1. Machinery begins the industrial revolution in the instruments of labour, not labour-power
2. A machine performs operations formerly done by workers, not just complex tools
3. Machinery's capitalist purpose is producing surplus-value through relative surplus-value
4. Machinery deskills workers while intensifying their labour
5. Machinery creates both displacement and new forms of employment
6. Machinery enables the factory system and continuous production
7. The struggle over machinery reveals the class conflict in technological change
