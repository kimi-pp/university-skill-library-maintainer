# Chapter 3: The Circuit of Commodity-Capital

## Core Idea
The general formula for commodity-capital circuit is C'-M'-C...P...C'. Unlike the other circuits, this form opens with circulation (C'-M'-C) rather than production, and always begins with capital-value augmented in commodity-form. It includes from the start the circuit of not only capital-value but also surplus-value, making C' inherently two-fold as both product and capital-value plus surplus-value.

## Frameworks Introduced

### The Formula
- **Formula**: C'-M'-C...P...C'
- **Extended reproduction**: If reproduction on extended scale, final C'' > initial C'
- **Commodity-capital premise**: C' appears as product and premise of previous circuits; means of production are commodity-products of other capitals

### Distinctive Features vs. Other Forms
- **Circulation opens circuit**: In Form III, total circulation with two antithetical phases opens the circuit
- **Form I (M...M')**: Circulation interrupted by production process
- **Form II (P...P)**: Total circulation appears as means of effecting reproduction
- **Form III circulation**: C'-M'.M-C = C-M-C (same form as simple commodity circulation)
- **Renewal difference**: In Forms I and II, M' and P' lose their produced form when renewed; in Form III, C must be designated as C'

### Capital-Value vs. Commodity-Capital
- **M' in Form I**: As soon as M' opens new circuit, functions as M (money-capital), simple capital-value
- **P' in Form II**: Must continue as P, capital-value to produce surplus-value
- **C' in Form III**: Opens with capital-value augmented in commodity-form; includes surplus-value circuit
- **Simple reproduction**: C' at terminal = C' at starting-point
- **Expanded reproduction**: C'' (enlarged C') appears at close with larger accumulated capital-value and surplus-value
- **Inauguration**: C' always inaugurates circuit as commodity-capital = capital-value + surplus-value

### C' as Two-Fold Form
- **Cannot open as mere C**: C' can never open circuit as mere C, mere commodity-form of capital-value
- **From use-value perspective**: C' is product (e.g., yarn) of P's functioning; elements L and MP come from circulation as factors in product creation
- **From value perspective**: C' is capital-value P plus surplus-value s produced by P's functioning
- **Separation in circuit**: Only in C' circuit can C (equal to P, equal to capital-value) separate from part of C' containing surplus-value
- **Realisation**: Separation occurs regardless of physical separability (yarn vs. machine); becomes separable once C' transformed into M'

### Successive Sales and Capital-Value Separation
- **Homogeneous products**: If entire commodity-product can be separated into independent homogeneous partial products (like 10,000 lbs. yarn)
- **Successive sales**: Act C'-M' represented by number of successive sales; capital-value can function as C before surplus-value realised
- **Example**: 10,000 lbs. yarn worth £500; 8,440 lbs. worth £422 = capital-value; 1,560 lbs. worth £78 = surplus-value
- **Early separation**: Selling 8,440 lbs. at £422 first; these represent C (capital-value in commodity-form); surplus-product circulates later
- **Partial replacement**: Capitalist can complete C-M-C before circulation of surplus-product c-m-c accomplished

### Detailed Separation Example
- **First sale option**: 8,440 lbs. at £422 = C (capital-value); surplus-product 1,560 lbs. circulates later
- **Second sale option**: 7,440 lbs. at £372 (constant capital c); + 1,000 lbs. at £50 (variable capital v); then proceed as before
- **Aliquot preparation**: If successive sales and conditions permit, capitalist may prepare for separation with partial sales

### Complex Division of Value Components
- **7,440 lbs. yarn (£372)**: Represents constant capital; can be divided into:
  - 5,535.360 lbs. (£276.768): Replaces constant part (means of production value)
  - 744 lbs. (£37.200): Replaces variable capital
  - 1,160.640 lbs. (£58.032): Surplus-product, depository of surplus-value
- **Replacement mechanism**: On selling 7,440 lbs., replace capital-value from sale of 6,279.360 lbs. at £313.968; spend surplus-product value £58.032 as revenue

### Variable Capital Division
- **1,000 lbs. yarn (£50)**: Represents variable capital-value; can be divided into:
  - 744 lbs. (£37.200): Constant capital-value contained in 1,000 lbs.
  - 100 lbs. (£5.000): Variable capital-value
  - 844 lbs. (£42.200): Replacement of capital-value in 1,000 lbs.
  - 156 lbs. (£7.800): Surplus-product, consumed as revenue

### Surplus-Value Division
- **1,560 lbs. yarn (£78)**: Can be divided into:
  - 1,160.640 lbs. (£58.032): Replaces value of means of production
  - 156 lbs. (£7.800): Replaces variable capital-value
  - 1,316.640 lbs. (£65.832): Total capital-value replacement
  - 243.360 lbs. (£12.168): Surplus-product spent as revenue

### Per-Unit Value Composition
- **Each pound of yarn (1s. = 12d.)**:
  - c = 0.744 lbs. = 8.928d. (constant capital)
  - v = 0.100 lbs. = 1.200d. (variable capital)
  - s = 0.156 lbs. = 1.872d. (surplus-value)
  - Total = 1 lb. = 12d.
- **Universal divisibility**: All elements c, v, s contained in yarn divisible into same component parts; same for every individual pound

### C' as Product of Other Capitals
- **Inter-capital relations**: C' as C does not appear in individual capital circuit as its form but as form of other industrial capital
- **Means of production**: When M-C involves MP, for purchasing capital this is C'-M' for selling capital
- **L and MP relations**: In M-C, L and MP bear identical relations as commodities in sellers' hands
- **Difference**: MP may be C' (capital) in seller's hands if it's commodity-form of their capital; L is always commodity for labourer, becomes capital only in purchaser's hands as P constituent

### Three Circuit Forms Summary
- **Form I (M...M')**: Money-capital circuit; production interrupts circulation
- **Form II (P...P)**: Productive capital circuit; circulation is means of reproduction
- **Form III (C'...C')**: Commodity-capital circuit; circulation opens and closes circuit; always begins with augmented capital-value

## Key Concepts
- **Commodity-Capital Circuit**: C'-M'-C...P...C' formula
- **Augmented Capital-Value**: C' = capital-value + surplus-value in commodity form
- **Circuit Opening**: Begins with commodity-capital, not money or productive capital
- **Successive Sales**: Multiple sales allowing capital-value separation before surplus-value realisation
- **Value Divisibility**: Ability to divide commodity into capital-value and surplus-value components
- **Inter-Capital Circulation**: C' of one capital is M-C for another
- **Three Circuit Forms**: Comparison of money, productive, and commodity capital circuits

## Key Takeaways
1. Commodity-capital circuit: C'-M'-C...P...C' opens with circulation
2. C' always begins as augmented capital-value (capital-value + surplus-value)
3. Form III includes surplus-value circuit from the start
4. C' is inherently two-fold: product from use-value perspective, capital-value + surplus-value from value perspective
5. Separation of C from C' occurs only in commodity-capital circuit
6. Successive sales allow capital-value to function as C before surplus-value realised
7. Each commodity unit contains same proportions of c, v, s value components
8. C' of one capital appears as C (commodity) for purchasing capital
9. MP may be C' for seller but C for buyer in inter-capital transactions
