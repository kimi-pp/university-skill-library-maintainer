# Chapter 8: Fixed Capital and Circulating Capital

## Core Idea
Fixed capital and circulating capital are distinctions arising from the different manners in which capital-value circulates. Fixed capital is that portion of constant capital whose value circulates gradually, piecemeal, as it passes from instruments of labour to products over their useful life. Circulating capital is all other capital-value that circulates entirely in one turnover. The distinction is not based on the physical properties of capital goods but on the manner of value circulation determined by how instruments of labour transmit value to products.

## Frameworks Introduced

### I. Distinctions of Form

#### Instruments of Labour and Fixed Capital
- **Use-form retention**: Portion of constant capital retains definite use-form in which it enters production process
- **Multiple labour-processes**: Functions in repeated labour-processes over longer or shorter period
- **Gradual value transfer**: Yields up value to product in proportion as it loses exchange-value with use-value (wear and tear)
- **Average calculation**: Value transition determined by average duration of function from entry to complete exhaustion
- **Fixed capital definition**: Capital-value fixed in instruments of labour performing this function

#### Value Circulation Peculiarity
- **All capital circulates**: All capital-value is constantly in circulation in general sense
- **Fixed capital peculiarity**: Portion of capital does not circulate in its use-form; only its value circulates
- **Gradual circulation**: Value circulates gradually, piecemeal, as it passes to product which circulates as commodity
- **Value retention**: During entire functioning period, part of value always remains fixed independently of commodities it helps produce
- **Definition**: This peculiarity gives this portion of constant capital the form of fixed capital

#### Other Material Capital Parts
- **Contrast**: All other material parts of capital advanced in production form circulating/fluid capital by contrast
- **Auxiliary materials**: Do not enter materially into product (coal for steam-engine, gas for lighting)
- **Value only**: Only their value forms part of product value; product circulates their value
- **Consumption**: Entirely consumed in every labour-process; must be wholly replaced in every new labour-process
- **No fixed value**: No portion of capital-value remains fixed in old use-form while functioning
- **Economist error**: Ramsay and others confused these with fixed capital due to value-only transfer and production sphere confinement

#### Raw Materials and Circulating Capital
- **Bodily entry**: Raw materials etc. that bodily enter product assume forms enabling later individual consumption as use-values
- **Instruments vs. raw materials**: Instruments of labour (fixed capital vehicles) consumed only productively, cannot enter individual consumption because they don't enter product
- **Transportation exception**: Means of transportation's useful effect (change of location) passes simultaneously into individual use like other consumption articles

#### Agricultural Improvements
- **Soil improvements**: Substances added for soil improvement blend partly into plants, help form product
- **Long-term effect**: Effect distributed over lengthy period (4-5 years)
- **Value division**: Portion passes bodily into product transferring value; other portion remains fixed in old use-form retaining value
- **Fixed capital persistence**: Continues as means of production, keeps fixed capital form
- **Example**: Ox as beast of toil = fixed capital; if eaten, no longer functions as instrument or fixed capital

### Determining Factor of Fixed Capital
- **Value circulation manner**: Exclusively the peculiar manner in which value circulates
- **Circulation origin**: Specific manner arises from how instrument of labour transmits value to product
- **Value creation behaviour**: How it behaves as value creator during production process
- **Labour-process function**: Specific way instruments function in labour-process

### Product as Instrument of Labour
- **Product as means of production**: Use-value emerging as product from one labour-process enters another as means of production
- **Fixed capital transformation**: Only functioning as instrument of labour in production makes product fixed capital
- **Manufacturing example**: Machine as manufacturer's commodity-capital, not fixed capital until productively employed by purchaser
- **Function dependency**: Fixed capital character depends on use, not inherent properties

### Degree of Fixity
- **Durability factor**: All other circumstances equal, degree of fixity increases with instrument durability
- **Value yield magnitude**: Determines difference between capital-value fixed in instruments and value yielded to product in repeated labour-processes
- **Slower yield**: Slower value yielded = larger fixed capital, greater difference between capital employed and consumed in process
- **Use-value retention**: Longer instrument lasts in production, longer constant capital-value remains fixed in it
- **Value transfer mechanism**: Instrument parts with value to product only as it loses value with use-value

### Economists' Confusion
- **Category mixing**: Fundamental mistake mixing "fixed" and "circulating" with "constant" and "variable" capital
- **Physical properties error**: Turning material properties (immobility of house) into direct properties of fixed capital
- **Counter-examples**: Other fixed capital instruments have opposite properties (ship's mobility)
- **Objective vs. social**: Confusing economic form definiteness (from value circulation) with objective properties
- **Capital nature**: Objects not capital in themselves; become capital only under definite social conditions

### Labour-Process Foundation
- **Means of production division**: From Book I, Chapter V — instruments of labour and subjects of labour in every labour-process regardless of social conditions
- **Capital transformation**: Both become capital only under capitalist mode as "productive capital"
- **Distinction reflection**: Labour-process division reflected in new form as fixed vs. circulating capital distinction
- **Functional basis**: Thing becomes fixed capital only when performing instrument of labour function
- **Multiple capacities**: If material can function in other capacities besides instrument, may be fixed or not depending on specific function
- **Cattle example**: Beasts of toil = fixed capital; beef cattle = raw material (circulating capital)

### Fixation Period vs. Fixed Capital
- **Long-term fixation**: Mere fixation of means of production for considerable length in connected, continuous labour-processes forming production period
- **Advance term**: Obliges capitalist to advance for longer/short term but doesn't make capital fixed
- **Seeds example**: Not fixed capital, only raw material held for ~1 year in production
- **Productive capital**: All capital held in production so long as it functions as productive capital, including all elements regardless of material forms, functions, value circulation modes
- **Irrelevance**: Fixation period length doesn't affect fixed vs. circulating capital distinction

### Physical Mobility and Fixed Capital
- **Localised instruments**: Some instruments (machinery, buildings, blast furnaces, canals, railways) localised/immovable from outset
- **Physical attachment**: Constant attachment to production process due to physical mode of existence
- **Mobile instruments**: Others (locomotive, ship, beasts of burden) change location continually but still constantly in production
- **Mobility non-determinative**: Neither immobility nor mobility determines fixed capital character
- **National economy role**: Localised fixed capital has peculiar role; cannot be sent abroad, cannot circulate as commodities in world-market
- **Ownership circulation**: Title to fixed capital can change, be bought/sold, circulate ideally; titles may circulate in foreign markets as stocks
- **No change**: Change of persons owning this class doesn't alter relation of immovable materially fixed capital

## Key Concepts
- **Fixed Capital**: Capital-value fixed in instruments of labour transmitting value gradually
- **Circulating/Fluid Capital**: Capital-value circulating entirely in one turnover
- **Instruments of Labour**: Means of production retaining use-form through multiple labour-processes
- **Value Transfer**: Gradual passing of instrument value to products
- **Wear and Tear**: Physical deterioration of instruments during use
- **Durability**: Length of instrument's useful life
- **Auxiliary Materials**: Consumed by instruments in function (coal, gas, lighting)
- **Raw Materials**: Bodily enter product, assume use-value forms
- **Use-Form Retention**: Instruments maintain independent form while functioning
- **Capital Fixation Period**: Time capital tied up in production process

## Key Takeaways
1. Fixed vs. circulating capital distinction arises from manner of value circulation, not physical properties
2. Fixed capital value circulates gradually as instrument of labour transfers value to products over useful life
3. Circulating capital includes all other capital-value that circulates entirely in one turnover
4. Auxiliary materials (coal, gas) are not fixed capital despite not entering product bodily
5. Raw materials are circulating capital as they bodily enter and are consumed in product
6. Degree of fixity increases with instrument durability
7. The distinction reflects the labour-process division between instruments and subjects of labour
8. Economists often confuse fixed/circulating with constant/variable capital
9. Physical mobility/immobility doesn't determine fixed capital character
10. Localised fixed capital (buildings, railways) cannot circulate as commodities in world-market
