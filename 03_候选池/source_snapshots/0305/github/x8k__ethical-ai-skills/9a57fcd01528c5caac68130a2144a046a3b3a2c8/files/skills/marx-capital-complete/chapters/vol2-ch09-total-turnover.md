# Chapter 9: The Aggregate Turnover of Advanced Capital, Cycles of Turnover

## Core Idea
Different parts of productive capital (fixed and circulating) turn over at different rates and periods. The aggregate turnover is the average turnover of various constituent parts. Due to qualitative differences (circulating capital transfers entire value, fixed capital transfers only wear and tear), it's necessary to reduce specific turnovers to homogeneous form for calculation. The aggregate turnover determines how much capital-value is turned over annually, which may exceed the advanced capital-value, and creates cycles that form material basis for periodic crises.

## Frameworks Introduced

### Different Turnover Periods
- **Fixed vs. circulating**: Fixed and circulating component parts turn over in various ways and periods
- **Fixed capital variation**: Different fixed capital constituents have different turnover periods depending on durability and reproduction times
- **Circulating capital note**: Real or apparent turnover differences within circulating capital in same business discussed at chapter end

### Aggregate Turnover Definition
- **Definition**: Average turnover of various constituent parts of advanced capital
- **Calculation**: Mode of calculation given later
- **Quantitative vs. qualitative**: Not only quantitative but also qualitative differences exist

### Qualitative Differences

#### Circulating Capital
- **Value transfer**: Transfers entire value to product
- **Replacement requirement**: Must be continually replaced in kind by sale of product for uninterrupted production
- **Turnover frequency**: Must be replaced more frequently than fixed capital

#### Fixed Capital
- **Partial value transfer**: Transfers only part of value (wear and tear) to product
- **Continued functioning**: Continues functioning despite wear and tear
- **Replacement timing**: Need not be replaced in kind until lapse of various intervals, not as frequently as circulating capital
- **Reproduction terms**: Different for various fixed capital constituents; longer-lasting fixed capital may be replaced annually or at shorter intervals
- **Replacement modes**: Some fixed capital must be replaced all at once at end of durability period; others can be replaced partially

### Homogenisation Requirement
- **Problem**: Qualitative identity doesn't come from P...P form (continuous production process) because definite P elements must be constantly replaced while others need not
- **Solution**: Form M...M' yields qualitative identity of turnover
- **Example**: Machine worth £10,000 lasting 10 years; £1,000 annually reconverted into money
- **Annual cycle**: £1,000 converted from money-capital → productive capital → commodity-capital → money-capital in one year
- **Return to original form**: Returns to money-form like circulating capital
- **Method**: For aggregate turnover calculation, fix all productive capital elements in money-form so return to that form concludes turnover
- **Assumption**: Value always advanced in money, even in continuous production where money-form is only money of account

### Aggregate Turnover Effects
- **Annual turnover magnitude**: Even if most capital is fixed with multi-year reproduction, circulating capital's repeated turnovers may make annual capital-value turned over larger than advanced capital
- **Example**: Fixed capital £80,000, reproduction period 10 years → £8,000 annually returns to money-form (1/10 turnover)
- **Circulating capital**: £20,000, turnover 5 times/year → £100,000 turned over
- **Total**: £100,000 capital; £108,000 turned over in one year (£8,000 more than advanced capital)
- **Result**: 1 + 2/25 of capital turned over

### Turnover Time vs. Reproduction Time
- **Distinction**: Turnover time of advanced capital-value differs from actual time of reproduction or actual turnover time of component parts
- **Example**: Capital £4,000, turnover 5 times/year → turned-over capital = £20,000
- **Return**: What returns at end of each turnover to be advanced anew = originally advanced £4,000
- **Magnitude constancy**: Original capital magnitude unchanged by number of turnover periods (apart from surplus-value)

### Capital Return Structure
- **Annual return (from example)**: 
  - (a) £20,000 reinvested in circulating capital constituents
  - (b) £8,000 set free by wear and tear from fixed capital value
- **Fixed capital state**: Remains in production process but with reduced value (£72,000 instead of £80,000)
- **Replacement cycle**: Advanced capital-value must pass through cycle of turnovers determined by fixed capital life, reproduction/turnover time

### Cycle of Turnovers and Crises
- **Life extension**: With capitalist development, fixed capital magnitude and durability develop, lengthening industry/life of industrial capital to many years (10 years average)
- **Life shortening**: Continuous revolution in means of production shortens life on other hand
- **Moral depreciation**: Necessity of constant replacement due to moral depreciation before physical expiry
- **Cycle basis**: Cycle of interconnected turnovers embracing number of years furnishes material basis for periodic crises
- **Business periods**: During cycle, business undergoes successive periods of depression, medium activity, precipitancy, crisis
- **Investment timing**: Capital investment periods differ greatly and don't coincide in time
- **Crisis trigger**: Crisis always forms starting-point of large new investments
- **Social perspective**: From society's viewpoint, more or less new material basis for next turnover cycle

### Turnover Calculation Methods

#### American Economist (Scrope)
- **Average period**: Capitalist must calculate profits based on average period entire capital takes to pass through hands/make one revolution
- **Example 1**: 
  - 1/2 capital in buildings/machinery → turned once in 10 years = £2,500/year
  - 1/4 capital in tools → turned once in 2 years = £6,500/year  
  - 1/4 capital in wages/material → turned twice in 1 year = £25,000/year
  - Total capital: £50,000; Annual expenditure: £33,750; Mean turnover term: ~16 months
- **Example 2**:
  - 1/4 capital → turned once in 10 years = £1,250/year
  - 1/4 capital → turned once in 1 year = £12,500/year
  - 1/2 capital → turned twice in 1 year = £50,000/year
  - Total turned over in 1 year: £63,750

### Real vs. Apparent Turnover Differences
- **Scrope's confusion**: Confuses differences in flow of circulating capital parts (from payment terms/credit) with turnover differences from capital nature
- **Wages example**: Wages paid weekly from weekly receipts doesn't affect turnover nature
- **Payment terms variation**: Differences occur relative to wages depending on payment term length (credit labourer gives capitalist)

## Key Concepts
- **Aggregate Turnover**: Average turnover of all capital constituent parts
- **Fixed Capital Turnover**: Gradual turnover through wear and tear replacement
- **Circulating Capital Turnover**: Complete turnover with each circuit
- **Turnover Cycle**: Multi-year cycle of interconnected turnovers
- **Homogeneous Turnover**: Turnover reduced to common form for calculation
- **Capital Replacement**: Renewal of capital elements after wear or consumption
- **Moral Depreciation**: Obsolescence requiring replacement before physical expiry
- **Periodic Crises**: Economic crises recurring at regular intervals
- **Business Cycle**: Successive periods of economic activity
- **Turnover Calculation**: Method for determining average capital turnover

## Key Takeaways
1. Different capital parts (fixed and circulating) have different turnover periods
2. Aggregate turnover = average turnover of all constituent parts
3. Circulating capital transfers entire value and must be replaced frequently
4. Fixed capital transfers only wear-and-tear value and is replaced less frequently
5. Aggregate turnover calculation requires reducing all parts to homogeneous money-form
6. Annual capital-value turned over may exceed advanced capital-value due to circulating capital's multiple turnovers
7. Turnover time of capital-value differs from reproduction time of its component parts
8. Cycle of interconnected turnovers (determined by fixed capital life) forms material basis for periodic crises
9. Fixed capital life lengthens with capitalist development but shortens with continuous revolution in means of production
10. Moral depreciation forces replacement before physical wear-out
