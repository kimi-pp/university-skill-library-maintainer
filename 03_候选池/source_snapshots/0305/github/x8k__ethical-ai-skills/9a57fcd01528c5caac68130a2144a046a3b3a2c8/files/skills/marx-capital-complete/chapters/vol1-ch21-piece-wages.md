# Chapter 21: Piece-Wages

## Core Idea
Piece-wages are a transformed form of time-wages, where payment is based on the quantity of work performed rather than time worked. This form appears to pay for the product of labour but in reality still pays for labour-power, while intensifying exploitation.

## Frameworks Introduced

### Piece-Wages as Transformed Time-Wages
- **Basis**: Still fundamentally time-wages, but with a different measurement
- **Mechanism**: Piece rate is determined by the time socially necessary to produce a unit
- **Formula**: Piece wage = (Daily labour-power value) / (Number of pieces produced in a normal working day)
- **Result**: Worker is paid per piece, but the rate is based on time

### The Illusion of Product Payment
- **Appearance**: Worker is paid for the product of their labour
- **Reality**: Worker is still paid for their labour-power, measured by output
- **Why it works**: Piece-wages appear to reward productivity directly
- **Capitalist advantage**: Encourages workers to increase output without direct supervision

### Quality Considerations
- **Standard quality**: Piece-wages assume work of average quality
- **Above average**: More skilled workers produce more pieces, earn more
- **Below average**: Less skilled workers earn less per hour effectively
- **Control**: Capital uses piece-rates to discipline labour and ensure quality

### Piece-Wages and Exploitation
- **Intensification**: Workers are incentivized to work faster and longer
- **Hidden extension**: Workers often work unpaid overtime to meet piece targets
- **Wage reduction**: Capital can cut piece rates when productivity increases
- **Result**: Piece-wages often result in higher exploitation than time-wages

### Two Forms of Piece-Wages
- **Pure Piece-Wages**: Payment solely by the piece, no time component
- **Mixed Piece-Time**: Combination of piece-wages with time-wages (guaranteed minimum)
- **Historical development**: Pure piece-wages in some industries, mixed in others

### Piece-Wages in Different Industries
- **Manufacturing**: Common in industries with measurable output
- **Garment industry**: Classic example of piece-work
- **Textiles**: Piece-wages widely used in early factory system
- **Construction**: Some forms of piece-work for specific tasks

## Key Concepts
- **Piece-Wages**: Payment per unit of output produced
- **Piece Rate**: Money paid per completed piece/unit
- **Socially Necessary Time**: Basis for determining piece rates
- **Productivity Incentive**: Workers' motivation to produce more
- **Quality Control**: Mechanism to ensure standard work quality
- **Mixed Wages**: Combination of piece and time payment systems

## Key Takeaways
1. Piece-wages are fundamentally time-wages with output-based measurement
2. Piece-wages create the illusion that workers are paid for their products
3. Piece rates are based on socially necessary production time
4. Piece-wages intensify labour and often increase exploitation
5. Capital uses piece-wages to discipline labour and reduce costs
