# Chapter 4: The Effect of the Turnover on the Rate of Profit

## Core Idea

This chapter analyzes how the turnover period affects the rate of profit, building on the foundation established in Chapters 1-3. Marx demonstrates that because not all capital can be employed simultaneously in production (some always lies idle as money-capital, raw material supplies, unsold commodities, or outstanding claims), the shorter the turnover period, the smaller the idle capital portion and the larger the appropriated surplus-value. Through concrete numerical examples, Marx shows that for capitals with equal composition and surplus-value rates, profit rates vary inversely with turnover periods. The chapter also provides the corrected formula for annual rate of profit: p' = s'n(v/C), where n = number of annual turnovers.

## Frameworks Introduced

### I. Turnover's General Effect on Surplus-Value and Profit

#### The Turnover Problem (Recap from Book II)
- **Idle Capital**: Due to turnover time span, not all capital can be employed in production simultaneously
- **Idle Forms**: Money-capital, raw material supplies, finished unsold commodity-capital, outstanding claims
- **Active Capital Shortfall**: Capital in active production always short by idle amount
- **Surplus-Value Curtailment**: Produced and appropriated surplus-value curtailed to same extent
- **Inverse Relationship**: Shorter turnover period → smaller idle portion → larger appropriated surplus-value (other conditions equal)

#### Book II Foundation
- **Detailed Analysis**: Book II Part II detailed how turnover reductions augment surplus-value quantity
- **Applicability**: Same analysis applies to profit and profit rate
- **No Repetition**: Current chapter stresses principal points only

### II. Means of Reducing Turnover Time

#### Production Time Reduction
- **Primary Means**: Higher labour productivity (industrial progress)
- **Condition**: Without simultaneous considerable increase in total capital outlay from expensive machinery
- **Historical Examples**:
  - **Metallurgy**: Bessemer, Siemens, Gilchrist-Thomas processes cut arduous processes to minimum at relatively small costs
  - **Chemical Industry**: Alizarin (red dye from coal-tar) production requires weeks instead of years (madder required 1+ years to mature plus additional years before processing)
- **Effect**: Rate of profit must rise if turnover reduction not offset by capital outlay increase

#### Circulation Time Reduction
- **Primary Means**: Improved communications
- **Historical Revolution**: Last 50 years brought revolution comparable to late 18th century industrial revolution
- **Transport Improvements**:
  - Land: Macadamised roads displaced by railways
  - Sea: Slow sailing vessels displaced by rapid steamboat lines
  - Global: Telegraph wires girdling entire globe; Suez Canal opening East Asia and Australia to steamer traffic
- **Concrete Impact**: East Asia shipment circulation time reduced from 12 months (1847) to 12 weeks
- **Crisis Effect**: America and India (1825-57 crisis centres) brought 70-90% nearer to European industrial countries, losing explosive nature
- **World Commerce**: Total world commerce turnover period reduced, efficacy of involved capital doubled or trebled
- **Profit Rate Impact**: These changes not without effect on profit rate

### III. Mathematical Analysis of Turnover Effect

#### Comparison Methodology
- **Controlled Comparison**: To isolate turnover effect, assume all other conditions equal:
  - Rate of surplus-value
  - Working-day
  - Per cent composition (c:v ratio)

#### Case Comparison: Different Turnovers, Equal Composition

**Capital A:**
- Composition: 80c + 20v = 100C
- Turnovers: 2 per year
- Rate of surplus-value: 100%
- Annual product: 160c + 40v + 40s
- Rate of profit calculation: 40s / 100C = 40% (not calculated on turned-over capital of 200)

**Capital B:**
- Composition: 160c + 40v = 200C (same percentage composition: 80%c + 20%v)
- Turnovers: 1 per year
- Rate of surplus-value: 100%
- Annual product: 160c + 40v + 40s (same absolute product as A)
- Rate of profit calculation: 40s / 200C = 20%

**Conclusion**: With equal percentage composition, equal surplus-value rates, equal working-days → rates of profit inversely related to turnover periods (A: 40%, B: 20%; turnover ratio 2:1, profit ratio 2:1)

#### Direct Effect on Surplus-Value Production
- **Variable Capital Efficiency**: Reduced turnover period increases efficiency of variable capital portion
- **Book II Chapter XVI Reference**: Variable capital of 500 turned over 10 times/year produces same surplus-value as 5,000 variable capital turned over once/year (same rate of surplus-value and wages)

#### Complex Examples with Fixed and Circulating Capital

**Capital I:**
- Fixed capital: 10,000 (10% annual depreciation = 1,000c)
- Circulating constant: 500
- Variable: 500
- Turnovers: 10 per year
- Rate of surplus-value: 100%
- Turnover product: 100c (depreciation) + 500c + 500v + 500s = 1,600
- Annual product: 1,000c + 5,000c + 5,000v + 5,000s = 16,000
- Capital: C = 11,000
- Surplus-value: s = 5,000
- Rate of profit: p' = 5,000/11,000 = 45 5/11%

**Capital II:**
- Fixed capital: 9,000 (1,000 annual wear)
- Circulating constant: 1,000
- Variable: 1,000
- Turnovers: 5 per year
- Rate of surplus-value: 100%
- Turnover product: 200c + 1,000c + 1,000v + 1,000s = 3,200
- Annual product: 1,000c + 5,000c + 5,000v + 5,000s = 16,000
- Capital: C = 11,000
- Surplus-value: s = 5,000
- Rate of profit: p' = 5,000/11,000 = 45 5/11%

**Capital III:**
- Fixed capital: 0
- Circulating constant: 6,000
- Variable: 5,000
- Turnovers: 1 per year
- Rate of surplus-value: 100%
- Annual product: 6,000c + 5,000v + 5,000s = 16,000
- Capital: C = 11,000
- Surplus-value: s = 5,000
- Rate of profit: p' = 5,000/11,000 = 45 5/11%

**Conclusion**: All three capitals have same annual surplus-value (5,000) and same total capital (11,000) → same rate of profit (45 5/11%) despite different turnover periods and compositions

**Turnover Reduction in Capital I:**
- New assumption: Only 5 turnovers/year (instead of 10)
- Turnover product: 200c + 500c + 500v + 500s = 1,700
- Annual product: 1,000c + 2,500c + 2,500v + 2,500s = 8,500
- Capital: C = 11,000
- Surplus-value: s = 2,500
- Rate of profit: p' = 2,500/11,000 = 22 8/11%
- **Effect**: Rate of profit falls one-half because turnover period doubled

### IV. The Annual Rate of Profit Formula

#### Simple vs. Annual Rate
- **Annual Surplus-Value**: S = sn (s = surplus-value per turnover, n = annual turnovers)
- **Annual Rate of Surplus-Value**: S' = s'n
- **Formula Correction**: p' = s'v/C only correct when v in numerator = v in denominator

#### Formula Derivation
- **Simple Formula**: p' = s/C = s/(c + v)
- **Transformation**: s = s'v → p' = s'v/(c + v)
- **Annual Correction**: Substitute annual rate s'n for simple rate s'
- **Annual Rate of Profit Formula**: p' = s'n(v/C)

#### The v Consistency Problem
- **Denominator v**: Entire variable capital portion of total capital C
- **Numerator v**: Must equal denominator v for formula accuracy
- **Condition**: s must stand for surplus-value produced in one turnover of variable capital
- **Underestimation**: If s < surplus-value of one turnover, v in numerator < v in denominator
- **Overestimation**: If s > surplus-value of one turnover, portion of v serves twice (in first and subsequent turnovers)

### V. Practical Calculation Example: Cotton Spinnery

#### Factory Data (from Book I, Chapter on Machinery)
- **Fixed Capital**: £10,000 (incorporated in machinery)
- **Circulating Capital**: £2,500 (high estimate, assuming no credit operations)
- **Total Capital**: £12,500
- **Time Period**: April 1871 week, assumed constant throughout year

#### Weekly Composition
- Depreciation: £20c
- Circulating constant: £358c (rent £6, cotton £342, coal/gas/oil £10)
- Variable capital (wages): £52v
- Surplus-value: £80s
- **Weekly Product**: 20c + 358c + 52v + 80s = £510

#### Annual Calculations
- **Weekly Circulating Capital**: £410 (358c + 52v)
- **Circulating Capital Composition**: 87.3%c + 12.7%v
- **Total Circulating Capital**: £2,500 = £2,182c + £318v
- **Annual Wage Expenditure**: 52 × £52 = £2,704
- **Variable Capital Turnovers**: £2,704 / £318 ≈ 8½ turnovers per year
- **Rate of Surplus-Value**: 80/52 = 153 11/13%

#### Annual Rate of Profit Calculation
- **Formula**: p' = s'n(v/C)
- **Values**: s' = 153 11/13, n = 8½, v = 318, C = 12,500
- **Calculation**: p' = 153 11/13 × 8½ × 318/12,500 = 33.27%
- **Verification**: Total annual surplus-value = 52 × £80 = £4,160
  - p' = £4,160 / £12,500 = 33.28% (matches)

#### Composition Analysis
- **Total Capital**: £12,500 = £12,182c + £318v
- **Percentage Composition**: 97½%c + 2½%v = 100C
- **Implication**: Only 1/40th of total capital serves for wage payment, but turns over more than 8 times annually

#### Annual Rate of Surplus-Value
- **Calculation**: 153 11/13% × 8½ = 1,307 9/18% (over 1,300%)
- **Historical Context**: In times of greatest prosperity, such rates not rare in Manchester
- **Modern Industry**: Illustrates actual capital composition in large-scale industry

#### Statistical Note
- **Capitalist Ignorance**: Very few capitalists calculate these relations for their own business
- **Statistical Silence**: Statistics almost completely silent on constant vs. variable capital portion relation
- **American Census Exception**: Provides sum of wages and profits by business line, though based on capitalist's own uncontrolled statements

## Key Concepts

- **Turnover Period**: Time required for capital to complete its circuit (production + circulation)
- **Idle Capital**: Portion of capital not actively employed in production due to turnover requirements
- **Turnover Reduction Means**: Labour productivity improvements (production time); communications improvements (circulation time)
- **Inverse Turnover-Profit Relationship**: For equal capitals, profit rates inversely related to turnover periods
- **Annual Rate of Profit**: p' = s'n(v/C) where n = annual turnovers of variable capital
- **Annual Rate of Surplus-Value**: S' = s'n where S = annual surplus-value, s = per-turnover surplus-value
- **Turnover Efficiency**: Variable capital turned over multiple times annually produces surplus-value proportionally

---

## Key Takeaways

1. **Turnover's Profit Impact**: Shorter turnover periods reduce idle capital, increasing active capital and appropriated surplus-value

2. **Production Time Reduction**: Industrial progress (higher labour productivity) reduces production time, potentially increasing profit rate

3. **Circulation Time Reduction**: Communications revolution (railways, steamboats, telegraph) dramatically reduces circulation time, doubling/trebling capital efficacy

4. **Inverse Relationship**: For capitals with equal composition and surplus-value rates, profit rates vary inversely with turnover periods

5. **Annual Formula**: p' = s'n(v/C) corrects simple profit rate formula for annual turnover effects

6. **Concrete Example**: Cotton spinnery with 8½ annual variable capital turnovers achieves 33.28% profit rate

7. **Modern Composition**: Advanced industry shows extremely high constant capital portions (97½%) with rapidly turning over variable capital

8. **Statistical Gap**: Capitalists rarely calculate true capital composition, leading to statistical silence on constant/variable relations

9. **High Annual Rates**: Annual surplus-value rates can exceed 1,300% in prosperous periods, contrary to bourgeois sensibilities

10. **Formula Consistency**: Annual profit rate formula must ensure v in numerator matches v in denominator (total variable capital)
