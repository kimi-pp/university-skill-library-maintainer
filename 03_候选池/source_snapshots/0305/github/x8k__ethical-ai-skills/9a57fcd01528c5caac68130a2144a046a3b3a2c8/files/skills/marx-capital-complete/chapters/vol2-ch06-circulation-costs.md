# Chapter 6: The Costs of Circulation

## Core Idea
The costs of circulation are expenses that do not add value to commodities but are necessary for capital's metamorphosis through its circuit. They include the time of purchase and sale, book-keeping, and other unproductive but necessary functions. These costs constitute deductions from capital's productive functioning and represent faux frais (incidental expenses) of production.

## Frameworks Introduced

### Genuine Costs of Circulation
- **Definition**: Costs arising purely from change of form of capital (C-M and M-C)
- **Character**: Do not add value to commodities; necessary for capital circuit
- **Capitalist perspective**: Part of business hours; necessary for capitalist to function as personified capital

### I. Genuine Costs of Circulation

#### a. The Time of Purchase and Sale
- **Form transformations**: Capital transformations from commodities to money and vice versa are capitalist transactions
- **Time dimension**: Time for these transformations = time of purchase and sale from capitalist's standpoint
- **Necessary function**: As necessary as production time itself for capitalist reproduction

##### Value Perspective on Purchase/Sale
- **At-value assumption**: If commodities bought/sold at values, acts constitute conversion of value from one form to another
- **State of being**: Change in value's state of being, not in magnitude
- **Non-value-creating**: If not at values, sum of converted values remains unchanged; plus on one side = minus on other

##### Bargaining Process
- **Transaction nature**: Metamorphoses C-M and M-C are transactions between buyers and sellers
- **Time requirement**: Require time to conclude bargains, especially with struggle to get best of each other
- **"Greek meets Greek"**: Businessmen facing one another; competition intensifies bargaining

##### Labour of Circulation
- **Purpose**: To accomplish conversion of value from one form to another, not to create value
- **No value creation**: Like work in judicial proceeding doesn't increase value of subject matter
- **Necessary element**: Part of capitalist production process as a whole, including circulation
- **Analogy**: Like work of combustion in heat generation — necessary but doesn't generate heat itself
- **Chemical analogy**: Combining coal with oxygen requires physical change (solid to gaseous state), molecular separation requiring energy expenditure not transformed into heat

##### Historical Context
- **Independent producers**: If commodity owners are independent direct producers, time in buying/selling = diminution of labour-time
- **Holiday deferral**: In ancient and medieval times, such transactions deferred to holidays

##### Division of Labour in Circulation
- **Specialisation**: Division of labour can transform incidental occupation of many into exclusive occupation of few
- **Function nature unchanged**: Function itself remains unproductive despite specialisation
- **Merchant's capital illusion**: Gives rise to illusion that circulation creates value
- **Efficiency gain**: One merchant can shorten purchase/sale time for many producers; like machine reducing useless energy expenditure
- **Example**: Buying/selling agent as wage-labourer; expends labour-power in C-M and M-C operations

##### Wage-Labour in Circulation
- **Necessary function**: Agent performs necessary function because reproduction process includes unproductive functions
- **No value creation**: Labour creates neither value nor product intrinsically
- **Faux frais**: Belongs to faux frais of production
- **Usefulness**: Reduces amount of society's labour-power/time tied up in unproductive function
- **Wage relation**: Even if better paid, as wage-labourer works part of time for nothing (surplus-labour)
- **Social perspective**: Labour-power used for circulation function cannot be used for productive labour
- **Cost reduction**: Capitalist employing such labour reduces circulation costs, deduction from income

##### Capital Advance for Circulation Labour
- **Additional investment**: Part of variable capital laid out for circulation labour-power
- **No productivity**: Creates neither product nor value
- **Opportunity cost**: Reduces dimensions in which advanced capital functions productively
- **Machine analogy**: Like part of product transformed into machine that buys/sells rest of product; reduces product but doesn't participate in production

#### b. Book-keeping
- **Labour and material**: Labour-time expended on book-keeping plus materialised labour (pens, ink, paper, desks, office paraphernalia)
- **Two-fold cost**: Expenditure of labour-power and instruments of labour
- **Same condition**: Same situation as time of purchase and sale

##### Ideal Existence of Capital
- **Capital as value in motion**: Exists ideally as money of account, primarily in capitalist's mind
- **Book-keeping function**: Fixes and controls movement; includes price determination/calculation
- **Symbolic reflection**: Movement of production (especially surplus-value production) symbolically reflected in imagination
- **Commodities as value depositories**: Figure only as names of things whose ideal existence as values crystallised in money of account

##### Individual vs. Capitalist Production
- **Peasant example**: Individual producer keeping accounts only in head; books expenditures, receipts, etc. incidentally
- **Deduction**: Function and instruments represent additional consumption of labour-time and instruments
- **Opportunity cost**: Necessary but constitutes deduction from time/instruments available for productive consumption

##### Scale and Concentration Effects
- **Concentration**: Dimensions assumed on account of concentration in capitalist hands
- **Function aggregation**: Instead of function of many small commodity-producers, appears as function of one capitalist
- **Specialisation**: Divisibility from productive functions; conversion into independent function of special agents
- **Intrinsic nature unchanged**: Division of labour and independence don't make function value-creating if it wasn't intrinsically

##### Capital Investment in Book-Keeping
- **New investment**: Capitalist investing capital anew must invest part in hiring book-keeper and book-keeping wherewithal
- **Continuing investment**: If capital already functioning, must continually reconvert part of product into book-keeper, clerks, etc.
- **Withdrawal from production**: Part of capital withdrawn from production process; belongs to circulation costs
- **Deduction from yield**: Deductions from total yield (including labour-power expended exclusively for this function)

##### Book-Keeping vs. Purchase/Sale Time
- **Difference**: Book-keeping costs arise from social scale of production process
- **Necessity**: More necessary in capitalist production than scattered handicraft/peasant economy; more necessary in collective capital than individual

## Key Concepts
- **Costs of Circulation**: Expenses for capital metamorphosis not adding value
- **Genuine Costs**: Pure form-change costs (purchase/sale time, book-keeping)
- **Time of Purchase and Sale**: Time spent in buying/selling transactions
- **Book-Keeping**: Record-keeping and control function
- **Faux Frais**: Incidental/overhead expenses of production
- **Circulation Labour**: Labour expended in circulation not creating value
- **Unproductive Labour**: Labour necessary but not value-creating
- **Division of Labour**: Specialisation in circulation functions
- **Money of Account**: Ideal existence of capital as value in motion
- **Capital Deduction**: Withdrawal from productive investment

## Key Takeaways
1. Costs of circulation do not add value but are necessary for capital's metamorphosis
2. Time of purchase and sale is necessary capitalist business time but creates no value
3. Book-keeping is necessary for control and ideal synthesis of production process
4. Circulation labour (purchase/sale, book-keeping) is unproductive but necessary
5. Division of labour in circulation reduces total circulation time for society
6. Capitalist employing circulation labour reduces own circulation costs
7. Part of variable capital must be advanced for circulation labour-power
8. Book-keeping becomes more necessary as production assumes social scale
9. Capital invested in circulation functions is withdrawn from productive process
10. Genuine circulation costs are deductions from total yield and productive capital dimensions
