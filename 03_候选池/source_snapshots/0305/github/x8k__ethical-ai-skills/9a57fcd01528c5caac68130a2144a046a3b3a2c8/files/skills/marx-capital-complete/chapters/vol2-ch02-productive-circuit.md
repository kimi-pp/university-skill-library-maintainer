# Chapter 2: The Circuit of Productive Capital

## Core Idea
The circuit of productive capital has the general formula P...C'-M'-C...P. It represents the periodical renewal of productive capital's function — reproduction as a process aiming at self-expansion of value, not just production but periodical reproduction of surplus-value. This form shows circulation as merely the connecting link between two phases of productive capital, and reveals the opposite form of circulation compared to money-capital.

## Frameworks Introducted

### The Formula of Productive Capital Circuit
- **Formula**: P...C'-M'-C...P
- **Meaning**: Periodical renewal of productive capital functioning; reproduction of surplus-value
- **Character**: Industrial capital in productive form, function performed periodically
- **Renewal**: Determined by starting-point P

### Re-entry of Values into Production
- **Direct re-entry**: Portion of C' may re-enter directly as means of production into same labour-process
- **Saves transformation**: Avoids transformation into real money or token-money; may exist only as money of account
- **Non-circulation**: Values enter process of production without entering process of circulation
- **Surplus-product consumption**: Part of C' consumed by capitalist in kind as surplus-product; insignificant for capitalist production except agriculture

### Contrast with Money-Capital Circuit
- **Money-capital form (M...M')**: Process of production (P) interrupts circulation; acts as mediator between M-C and C'-M'
- **Productive capital form (P...P)**: Entire circulation process constitutes interruption; acts as connecting link between opening P and closing P
- **Continuity**: Circulation promotes periodically renewed reproduction, rendered continuous by renewal
- **Form difference**: Circulation presents opposite form: money-capital = M-C-M; productive capital = C-M-C (simple commodity circulation form)

### Simple Reproduction Analysis
- **C'-M'-C process**: Takes place in sphere of circulation between P...P extremes
- **Commodity-capital**: C' = C + c = P + c (capital-value + surplus-value)
- **Function**: Realisation of capital-value (P, now constituent C of C') and surplus-value (c)
- **Phase comparison**: In money-capital circuit, this function formed second phase of interrupted circulation and concluding phase of entire circuit
- **Here**: Forms second phase of circuit but first phase of circulation

### The M' Division Problem
- **M' composition**: M' contains original M + m (surplus-value)
- **Decision point**: Must decide whether M and m continue together or pursue separate courses
- **Circuit character**: This decision determines whether formula represents simple reproduction or reproduction on extended scale
- **Must be decided**: In productive capital circuit, this is necessary because C'-M' appears as first phase of circulation requiring complement M-C

### Simple Reproduction Assumptions
- **Conditions**: Constant conditions; commodities bought and sold at values
- **Surplus-value destination**: Entire surplus-value enters individual consumption of capitalist
- **Capital-value continuation**: Capital-value portion continues to circulate in circuit of industrial capital after C'-M'
- **Surplus-value separation**: Surplus-value changed into money enters general circulation of commodities; circulation outside individual capital

### Numerical Illustration (Yarn Example)
- **Commodity-capital**: C' = 10,000 lbs. of yarn valued at £500
- **Capital-value**: £422 representing productive capital; continues as money-form of 8,440 lbs. of yarn
- **Surplus-value**: £78 (money-form of 1,560 lbs. of yarn) leaves capital circulation
- **Circulation split**: Capital-value continues in capital circulation; surplus-value enters general circulation

### Circulation Diagram
```
                C     —            M      —     C
                                                     < MP
         C'     +     —      M'    +
                c     —            m      —     c
```
- **m-c**: Series of purchases by capitalist using surplus-value money (m)
- **Purchases**: For commodities or personal services for capitalist/family consumption
- **Hoard form**: Money temporarily exists as supply/hoard for current consumption
- **Not advanced**: This money is not advanced but spent; does not enter capital circulation in money-form

### Capital Advance Assumption
- **Total passage**: Advanced capital passes wholly from one phase to another
- **Commodity representation**: Commodities produced by P represent total value of P (£422) + surplus-value (£78)
- **Discrete vs. continuous**: Discrete commodity (yarn) allows separation; continuous commodity (machine) requires whole sale

### Machine vs. Yarn Example
- **Yarn**: Surplus-value exists as 1,560 lbs. of yarn; can be divided and sold separately
- **Machine**: Surplus-value exists as £78 in machine as a whole; cannot be divided without destroying value and use-value
- **Ideal components**: Two value-components (capital-value and surplus-value) can be represented only ideally as components of commodity C'
- **Separate elements**: Yarn allows representation as independent elements; machine requires whole sale before m enters separate circulation

### Value Component Representation
- **Total product**: 10,000 lbs. of yarn divisible into:
  - Constant capital (c): 7,440 lbs. worth £372
  - Variable capital (v): 1,000 lbs. worth £50
  - Surplus-value (s): 1,560 lbs. worth £78
- **Per unit**: Each pound of yarn divisible into:
  - c: 11.904 oz. worth 8.928d.
  - v: 1.600 oz. worth 1.200d.
  - s: 2.496 oz. worth 1.872d.
- **Successive sale**: Capitalist may sell portions successively and consume portions of surplus-value
- **Final requirement**: In final analysis, operation premises sale of entire lot

### Separable Existence After C'-M'
- **Result**: By C'-M', both capital-value and surplus-value contained in C' acquire separable existence as different sums of money
- **Conversion**: M and m are converted form of value originally in C' with only ideal expression as commodity price
- **Circulation split**: c-m-c represents simple circulation of commodities
- **Inclusion/exclusion**: c-m included in capital circulation C'-M'; m-c falls outside capital circuit as separate act in general commodity circulation

## Key Concepts
- **Productive Capital Circuit**: P...C'-M'-C...P formula
- **Simple Reproduction**: Production maintaining same scale
- **Commodity-Capital (C')**: C + c = P + c (capital-value + surplus-value in commodity form)
- **Capital-Value (C)**: Value of productive capital
- **Surplus-Value (c or s)**: Value created beyond capital-value
- **Money-Form (M, m)**: Capital-value and surplus-value in money form
- **Re-entry**: Direct use of portion of C' as means of production
- **Circulation vs. Production**: Distinction between movement in circulation and production spheres

## Key Takeaways
1. Productive capital circuit: P...C'-M'-C...P represents periodical renewal of production
2. Circulation appears as interruption connecting two phases of productive capital
3. Form is opposite of money-capital circuit: C-M-C vs. M-C-M
4. Simple reproduction assumes entire surplus-value consumed individually by capitalist
5. C'-M' divides into capital-value (continuing circulation) and surplus-value (entering general circulation)
6. Capital-value and surplus-value acquire separable existence as money after C'-M'
7. Different commodities (discrete vs. continuous) affect how capital-value and surplus-value can be separated
8. Successive sale still premises eventual sale of entire commodity product
