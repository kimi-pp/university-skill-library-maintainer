# Chapter 19: The Transformation of the Value (and Respective Price) of Labour-Power into Wages

## Core Idea
Wages appear as the price of labour, but this is an illusion. Labour cannot have a value or price because it only exists as the activity of labour-power, which has already been sold. The wage form mystifies the true relation: workers are paid for their labour-power, not for their labour.

## Frameworks Introduced

### The Wage Illusion
- **Appearance**: "The wage of the labourer appears as the price of labour"
- **Reality**: Wages are the price of labour-power (the capacity to work)
- **Problem**: "What is the value of a 12-hour working day? By the 12 working-hours contained in it" — a tautology
- **Contradiction**: Labour cannot exist as a commodity before it is sold (it is the activity itself)

### Why Labour Cannot Have a Value
- **Value determination**: Commodity value = quantity of social labour expended in its production
- **Labour as substance of value**: Labour creates value but cannot itself have value
- **Tautology problem**: Measuring labour's value by labour-time is circular
- **Marx's insight**: "Labour is the substance, and the immanent measure of value, but has itself no value"

### The Reality Behind the Wage Form
- **What is sold**: Labour-power (the capacity to work), not labour (the activity)
- **When it is sold**: Before labour begins; the worker sells the capacity, not the activity
- **Capitalist control**: Once labour begins, it belongs to the capitalist, not the worker
- **Consequence**: The worker cannot sell labour that has already been performed

### The Mystification of Wage Labour
- **Appearance**: Worker sells labour for wages
- **Reality**: Worker sells labour-power for wages, then performs labour for capital
- **Effect**: "In the expression 'value of labour,' the idea of value is not only completely obliterated, but actually reversed"
- **Historical basis**: This illusion arises from the capitalist mode of production itself

### Why the Illusion Persists
- **Surface appearance**: Wages are paid after labour is performed, appearing as payment for labour
- **Traditional usage**: People have long spoken of "value of labour" and "price of labour"
- **Capitalist advantage**: The illusion hides the true nature of exploitation
- **Result**: Even workers believe they are paid for their labour, not for their labour-power

### The Fetishism of Wages
- **Fetish character**: Wages appear as a direct exchange between labour and money
- **Reality**: It is an exchange between labour-power (commodity) and money (its price)
- **Social relation hidden**: The wage form conceals the class relation between capitalist and worker
- **Naturalization**: The wage form makes capitalist exploitation appear as a natural transaction

## Key Concepts
- **Wage**: Price of labour-power; appears as price of labour
- **Labour-Power**: Commodity sold by worker; the capacity to work
- **Labour**: Activity performed by worker; creates value but has no value itself
- **Wage Form**: The phenomenal form in which labour-power's value appears as labour's price
- **Fetishism**: The mystification by which social relations appear as natural things
- **Value of Labour**: Imaginary expression; labour has no value
- **Price of Labour**: Imaginary expression; labour has no price

## Key Takeaways
1. Wages are the price of labour-power, not labour
2. Labour cannot have a value because it is the substance of value
3. The wage form creates the illusion that workers are paid for their labour
4. This illusion mystifies the true class relation and hides exploitation
5. The transformation of labour-power's value into wages is a necessary appearance of capitalist production
