# Chapter 31: The Genesis of the Industrial Capitalist

## Core Idea
The industrial capitalist emerged not through gradual accumulation but through violent historical processes. While some small guild-masters and artisans became capitalists slowly, the commercial requirements of the new world market created by the great discoveries of the late 15th century demanded faster transformation. The key forms were usurer's capital and merchant's capital, which combined with colonial plunder, the slave trade, national debts, and protectionist systems to create the primitive accumulation that birthed industrial capitalism.

## Frameworks Introduced

### The Two Pre-Capitalist Forms of Capital
- **Usurer's capital**: Lending capital at interest, existing across different economic formations
- **Merchant's capital**: Trading capital, also existing before capitalist mode of production
- **Feudal constraints**: Guild organization in towns and feudal constitution in countryside prevented transformation into industrial capital
- **Historical release**: Dissolution of feudal society and expropriation of country population removed these fetters
- **Geographic location**: New manufactures established at sea-ports or inland points beyond control of old municipalities and guilds
- **Result**: Embittered struggle of corporate towns against new industrial nurseries

### Colonialism as Primitive Accumulation
- **"Rosy dawn"**: Discovery of gold and silver in America; extirpation, enslavement, entombment of aboriginal population; conquest and looting of East Indies; Africa turned into warren for commercial hunting of black-skins
- **Idyllic proceedings**: Marx's ironic description of colonial violence as "chief momenta of primitive accumulation"
- **Commercial wars**: European nations with globe as theatre; begins with Netherlands' revolt from Spain; England's Anti-Jacobin War; opium wars against China
- **Chronological distribution**: Different momenta distributed over Spain, Portugal, Holland, France, England
- **English system**: By end of 17th century, systematic combination embracing colonies, national debt, modern taxation, protectionist system

### Colonial System's Economic Role
- **Hothouse effect**: "Ripened, like a hot-house, trade and navigation"
- **Monopolies**: Luther's "societies Monopolia" as powerful levers for capital concentration
- **Market creation**: Colonies secured markets for budding manufactures; monopoly ensured increased accumulation
- **Capital flow**: Treasures captured outside Europe floated back to mother-country and were turned into capital
- **Holland's example**: By 1648, Holland in acme of commercial greatness with East Indian trade monopoly; fisheries, marine, manufactures surpassed all others
- **Hidden cost**: "People of Holland were more over-worked, poorer and more brutally oppressed than those of all the rest of Europe put together"

### Commercial Supremacy and Industrial Development
- **Manufacturing period**: Commercial supremacy gave industrial predominance
- **Strange God**: Colonial system as new deity replacing old European gods
- **Sole aim**: "Proclaimed surplus-value making as the sole end and aim of humanity"
- **Modern relation**: Today industrial supremacy implies commercial supremacy; in manufacturing period, reverse was true

### The Colonial Horror Stories
- **Howitt's assessment**: "The barbarities and desperate outrages of the so-called Christian race... are not to be paralleled by those of any other race"
- **Holland's colonial administration**: "Extraordinary relations of treachery, bribery, massacre, and meanness"
- **Java slave trade**: Men-stealers trained; thief, interpreter, seller as chief agents; native princes as chief sellers
- **Secret dungeons**: Young people stolen thrown into secret dungeons of Celebes until ready for slave-ships
- **Macassar**: "Full of secret prisons, one more horrible than the other, crammed with unfortunates, victims of greed and tyranny fettered in chains, forcibly torn from their families"
- **Malacca**: Dutch corrupted Portuguese governor; assassinated him to avoid payment of £21,875 for his treason
- **Devastation**: "Wherever they set foot, devastation and depopulation followed"
- **Banjuwangi, Java**: 80,000+ inhabitants in 1750; only 18,000 in 1811

### British East India Company Plunder
- **Monopolies**: Exclusive monopoly of tea-trade, Chinese trade, transport of goods to/from Europe
- **Coasting trade**: Internal Indian trade monopolized by higher company employees
- **Salt, opium, betel**: Inexhaustible mines of wealth through monopolies
- **Price fixing**: Employees fixed prices and plundered Hindus at will
- **Governor-General**: Participated in private traffic; favourites received contracts to make gold from nothing
- **Fortune creation**: "Great fortunes sprang up like mushrooms in a day; primitive accumulation went on without the advance of a shilling"
- **Warren Hastings trial**: Swarms with cases of corruption and plunder
- **Opium contract example**: Sullivan received contract, sold to Binn for £40,000; Binn sold same day for £60,000; ultimate purchaser still made enormous gain
- **Parliamentary evidence**: Company and employees got £6,000,000 from Indians as gifts (1757-1766)
- **Famine creation**: 1769-1770, English manufactured famine by buying up all rice and refusing to sell except at fabulous prices

### Plantation Colonies and Aboriginal Treatment
- **Worst treatment**: Plantation-colonies for export trade (West Indies) and plundered countries (Mexico, India)
- **Christian character**: Even in proper colonies, primitive accumulation's brutal nature unchanged
- **New England Puritans**: "Sober virtuosi of Protestantism" set premiums on Indian scalps: £40 in 1703, £100 in 1720
- **1744 Massachusetts**: After tribe proclaimed rebels — male scalp (12+ years): £100; male prisoner: £105; women/children prisoners: £50; women/children scalps: £50
- **Colonial revenge**: Decades later, descendants of pious pilgrims (grown seditious) tomahawked by red-skins at English instigation and pay
- **Parliamentary endorsement**: Bloodhounds and scalping proclaimed "means that God and Nature had given into its hand"

### National Debt as Primitive Accumulation Lever
- **Public credit system**: Origin in Genoa and Venice in Middle Ages; took possession of Europe during manufacturing period
- **Colonial connection**: Colonial system with maritime trade and commercial wars served as forcing-house
- **Holland's lead**: System first took root in Holland
- **National debt meaning**: "Alienation of the state" — whether despotic, constitutional, or republican
- **National wealth paradox**: "Only part of so-called national wealth that actually enters into collective possessions of modern peoples is their national debt"
- **Modern doctrine**: Nation becomes richer the more deeply it is in debt
- **Credo**: Public credit becomes the credo of capital; want of faith in national debt = unforgivable blasphemy

### National Debt's Magical Power
- **Enchanter's wand**: Endows barren money with power of breeding, turning it into capital without industry or usury risks
- **State creditors**: Actually give nothing away; sum lent transformed into public bonds functioning as hard cash
- **Lazy annuitants**: Class created by national debt
- **Financier wealth**: Improvised wealth of middlemen between government and nation
- **Tax-farmers**: Beneficiaries of national loans alongside merchants and private manufacturers
- **Capital from heaven**: Good part of every national loan renders service of capital fallen from heaven
- **Stock exchange**: National debt gave rise to joint-stock companies, negotiable effects, agiotage, stock-exchange gambling, modern bankocracy

### Bank of England and Modern Bankocracy
- **Origin**: Founded in 1694; began lending money to government at 8%
- **Double privilege**: Parliament empowered bank to coin money out of same capital by lending again to public as banknotes
- **Banknotes**: Used for discounting bills, making advances on commodities, buying precious metals
- **Credit-money**: Bank's own creation became coin for loans to state and payment of public debt interest
- **Eternal creditor**: Bank gave with one hand, took back more with other; remained eternal creditor down to last shilling
- **Metallic hoard**: Gradually became receptacle of country's metallic hoard and centre of gravity of commercial credit
- **Contemporary reaction**: Bolingbroke's writings prove effect on contemporaries of sudden uprising of bankocrats, financiers, rentiers, brokers, stock-jobbers

### International Credit and Primitive Accumulation
- **Venetian-Holland connection**: Villainies of Venetian thieving system formed secret bases of Holland's capital-wealth; Venice lent large sums to Holland in decadence
- **Holland-England connection**: Same pattern with Holland lending to England; England lending to United States
- **Blood capital**: "A great deal of capital, which appears today in the United States without any certificate of birth, was yesterday, in England, the capitalised blood of children"
- **Modern continuation**: Pattern continues between England and United States

### Taxation System as Expropriation Engine
- **Public revenue**: National debt finds support in public revenue covering yearly interest payments
- **Automatic progression**: Modern fiscality with taxes on most necessary means of subsistence contains germ of automatic progression
- **Overtaxation**: Not an incident but a principle
- **Holland's model**: DeWitt in "Maxims" extolled system as best for making wage labourer submissive, frugal, industrious, overburdened
- **Expropriating efficacy**: Forcibly expropriated peasants, artisans, all elements of lower middle class — acknowledged even by bourgeois economists
- **Protection system**: Integral part enhancing expropriating efficacy

### Protectionist System
- **Artificial manufacturing**: Artificial means of manufacturing manufacturers
- **Expropriation mechanism**: Expropriated independent labourers; capitalised national means of production and subsistence
- **Transition acceleration**: Forcibly abbreviated transition from medieval to modern mode of production
- **European competition**: States tore each other to pieces over patent of this invention
- **Direct contribution**: Once in service of surplus-value makers, laid under contribution their own people through protective duties and export premiums
- **Colonial destruction**: Forcibly rooted out all industry in dependent countries (England with Irish woollen manufacture)
- **Continental method**: After Colbert's example, primitive industrial capital came directly from state treasury
- **Mirabeau's observation**: "180,000,000 of debts contracted by the sovereigns" as cause of Saxony's manufacturing glory before war

### The Birth of Modern Industry
- **Gigantic increase**: Colonial system, public debts, heavy taxes, protection, commercial wars increased gigantically during infancy of Modern Industry
- **Slaughter of innocents**: Birth heralded by great slaughter of innocents
- **Press-gang recruitment**: Like royal navy, factories recruited by press-gang
- **Eden's blindness**: While understanding agricultural expropriation, blind to necessity of child-stealing and child-slavery for factory system

### Child Labour and Factory Exploitation
- **Eden's question**: "It may, perhaps, be worthy the attention of the public to consider, whether any manufacture, which... requires that cottages and workhouses should be ransacked for poor children... will add to the sum of individual or national felicity?"
- **Derbyshire, Nottinghamshire, Lancashire**: New machinery in large factories on stream sides required thousands of hands
- **Population solution**: Lancashire (previously thinly populated) needed population; nimble fingers of children most in demand
- **Parish apprentice system**: Custom of procuring apprentices from parish workhouses of London, Birmingham, etc.
- **Scale**: Many thousands of children aged 7-14 sent to northern factories
- **Apprentice houses**: Master clothed, fed, lodged children in separate houses; overseers appointed
- **Overseer incentives**: Paid in proportion to work extracted; interest was to work children to utmost
- **Cruelty**: Consequence was cruelty; "cruelties the most heart-rending were practised"
- **Conditions**: Harassed to brink of death by excess labour; flogged, fettered, tortured
- **Starvation**: "Starved to the bone while flogged to their work"
- **Suicide**: Some driven to suicide; beautiful valleys became "dismal solitudes of torture, and of many a murder"
- **Night-working**: Tired one set of hands by day work, another set ready for night; day-set got into beds night-set just quitted

### The System's Fundamentals
- **Not fundamental cause**: Cobbett, Doubleday incorrectly sought fundamental cause of modern misery in public debt and fiscal system
- **Essential elements**: Colonial system, public debts, taxation, protection, commercial wars as children of manufacturing period
- **Combined effect**: These forces worked together to accelerate transition to capitalist mode of production

## Key Concepts
- **Primitive Accumulation**: Historical process of separating producers from means of production
- **Colonialism**: System of colonial exploitation as driver of primitive accumulation
- **National Debt**: State borrowing creating public credit and financial capital
- **Protectionism**: Tariff system protecting infant industries and expropriating independent producers
- **Commercial Wars**: European conflicts over colonial and trade advantages
- **Bankocracy**: Rule by banks and financial institutions
- **Usurer's Capital**: Lending capital at interest
- **Merchant's Capital**: Trading capital
- **Stock Exchange**: Market for buying and selling securities
- **Joint-Stock Companies**: Corporate forms enabling capital concentration
- **Press-Gang**: Forced recruitment of labour
- **Child Slavery**: Exploitation of children in early factories

## Key Takeaways
1. Industrial capitalists emerged through violent primitive accumulation, not gradual accumulation
2. Usurer's and merchant's capital were pre-existing forms that became industrial capital when feudal fetters were removed
3. Colonialism — with its plunder, slavery, and genocide — was a chief moment of primitive accumulation
4. The colonial system ripened trade and navigation, providing markets and capital for industrial development
5. National debt system transformed barren money into capital, creating financial aristocracies
6. Bank of England and modern bankocracy arose from national debt, creating new forms of capital accumulation
7. Taxation and protectionist systems worked together to expropriate independent producers and capitalise national wealth
8. The birth of modern industry was heralded by the press-gang recruitment of labour, including systematic child-stealing
9. Factory system's early development relied on brutal exploitation of child labour through the parish apprentice system
10. The combination of colonial plunder, national debt, taxation, and protection created the capital necessary for industrial revolution
