# Chapter 7: The Turnover Time and the Number of Turnovers

## Core Idea
The turnover time of capital is the period from the moment of advance of capital-value in a definite form to its return in the same form. It equals the sum of production time and circulation time. The number of turnovers (n) in a given period (usually a year) is T/t, where T is the period (year) and t is the turnover time. The turnover is the periodical process of capital renewal, and its duration determines the periodicity of capital's self-expansion.

## Frameworks Introduced

### Turnover Time Definition
- **Definition**: Entire time from advance of capital-value in definite form to return of functioning capital-value in same form
- **Components**: Turnover time = production time + circulation time
- **Identity**: Capital-value identity fixed in capitalist's books or money of account despite form changes
- **Compelling motive**: Always creation of surplus-value by means of advanced value, regardless of form (money or commodities)

### Circuit Forms and Turnover
- **Form I (M...M')**: Explicitly shows self-expansion (M to M' = M + m) and return to money-form
- **Form II (P...P)**: Starting-point is elements of production (commodities with given value); includes self-expansion and return to original form
- **Form III (C'...C')**: Capital-value doesn't begin as advance but as already expanded value; not used for turnover analysis
- **Turnover requirement**: Always begins with advance of capital-value (money or commodities) and necessitates return in same form

### Reproduction and Self-Expansion
- **Capitalistic reproduction**: If production is capitalistic, so is reproduction; labour-process is means towards self-expansion of capital
- **Value advance**: Reproduction is means of reproducing advanced value as capital (self-expanding value)
- **Quote from Book I, Chapter XXI**: "If production be capitalistic in form, so, too, will be reproduction"

### Distinctions Among Circuit Forms
- **Form II (P...P)**: Expresses renewal of process and reproduction as reality
- **Form I (M...M')**: Expresses renewal only as potentiality
- **Both I and II**: Advanced capital-value (money or production elements) is starting-point and returning point
- **M...M' return**: M' = M + m; if renewed on same scale, M is starting-point, m shows self-expansion but doesn't enter process
- **P...P return**: P' (equal to M', C') reopens process as expanded capital-value if accumulation occurs, but begins again with advanced capital-value in initial form with greater magnitude

### Turnover Definition
- **Turnover**: Circuit performed by capital as periodical process, not individual act
- **Duration**: Determined by sum of production time and circulation time
- **Time of turnover**: Measures interval between one circuit period and next; periodicity in capital's life process; time of renewal/repetition of self-expansion or production

### Turnover Formula
- **Variables**: T = year (natural unit), t = turnover time, n = number of turnovers
- **Formula**: n = T/t
- **Examples**:
  - t = 3 months → n = 12/3 = 4 turnovers/year
  - t = 18 months → n = 12/18 = 2/3 turnover/year
  - t > 1 year → compute in multiples of one year

### Natural Unit of Measure
- **Working day**: Natural unit for measuring labour-power function
- **Year**: Natural unit for measuring capital turnovers
- **Basis**: Most important crops of temperate zone (capitalism's mother country) are annual products

### Capitalist Perspective on Turnover
- **Capital advance time**: From capitalist's view, turnover time is time for which capital must be advanced to create surplus-value and receive it back in original shape

### Economists' Views on Turnover
- **General neglect**: Economists little distinguish between different circuit forms or examine them individually regarding turnover
- **Dominant form**: Generally consider M...M' because it dominates individual capitalist calculations, even if money is only money of account
- **Newman's view**: "Economic Cycle... whole course of production, from time outlays are made till returns are received" (seedtime to harvesting in agriculture)
- **Chalmers' view**: "World of trade revolves in economic cycle... accomplishes one revolution by business, coming round again through successive transactions to starting point"
- **Returns focus**: Some start with C' (returns received) without specifying return form

### New Forms from Circulation
- **Investigation needed**: Before examining turnover's influence on production/self-expansion, must investigate new forms capital assumes from circulation process affecting turnover form

## Key Concepts
- **Turnover Time**: Period from capital advance to return in same form
- **Production Time**: Time capital spends in production sphere
- **Circulation Time**: Time capital spends in circulation sphere
- **Number of Turnovers (n)**: How many times capital completes circuit in given period
- **Turnover Formula**: n = T/t (T = period, t = turnover time)
- **Circuit Period**: One complete passage through capital circuit
- **Renewal**: Repetition of capital's self-expansion process
- **Periodicity**: Regular interval of capital's life process
- **Capital Advance**: Initial investment of capital-value
- **Capital Return**: Recovery of capital-value in original form

## Key Takeaways
1. Turnover time = production time + circulation time
2. Turnover is the periodical process of capital renewal/repetition
3. Circuit forms I (M...M') and II (P...P) are useful for analyzing turnover influence on surplus-value formation and product creation
4. Turnover always begins with capital-value advance and requires return in same form
5. The year is the natural unit for measuring capital turnovers (matching annual agricultural cycles)
6. Number of turnovers n = T/t (period divided by turnover time)
7. Turnover time measures the periodicity of capital's self-expansion process
8. Form III (C'...C') is not used for turnover analysis
9. Economists generally focus on M...M' form for turnover calculations
10. Turnover duration determines capital's productivity and self-expansion rate
