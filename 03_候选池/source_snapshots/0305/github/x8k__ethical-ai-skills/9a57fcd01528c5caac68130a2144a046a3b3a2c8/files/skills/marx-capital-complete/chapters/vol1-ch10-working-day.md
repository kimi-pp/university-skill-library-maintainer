# Chapter 10: The Working Day

## Core Idea
The working day has both a necessary portion (reproducing labour-power value) and a surplus portion (creating surplus-value). Capital's inherent tendency is to extend the working day to maximize surplus labour, but this is limited by physical, moral, and social constraints.

## Frameworks Introduced

### Working Day Composition
- **Necessary Labour Time (A-B)**: Time to reproduce labour-power value (wage equivalent)
- **Surplus Labour Time (B-C)**: Time that creates surplus-value
- **Total Working Day (A-C)**: Necessary + Surplus = A-B + B-C
- **Formula**: Rate of surplus-value = B-C / A-B (surplus labour / necessary labour)

### The Variable Working Day
- **Not fixed**: The total working day is determinable but not predetermined
- **Depends on**: Duration of surplus labour (B-C) which is variable
- **Example**: With A-B=6 hours (necessary):
  - Working Day I: A-C=7 hours (B-C=1 hour, s'=16.67%)
  - Working Day II: A-C=9 hours (B-C=3 hours, s'=50%)
  - Working Day III: A-C=12 hours (B-C=6 hours, s'=100%)

### Limits to the Working Day

**Physical Limits:**
- **24-hour bound**: Within a natural day, a person can expend only a definite quantity of vital force
- **Rest requirement**: Part of the day must be devoted to rest and sleep
- **Biological needs**: Time for eating, washing, clothing
- **Example**: "A horse... can only work... 8 hours" daily

**Moral/Social Limits:**
- **Intellectual needs**: Time for satisfying mental and social wants
- **Social advancement**: The extent varies with general social development
- **Elastic nature**: These limits allow significant variation (8-18 hour days historically)

### Capital's Drive vs. Worker's Resistance
- **Capital's tendency**: To extend the working day beyond all limits ("were it not for legal coercion, capital would without hesitation reduce the whole working class to the 16-hour day")
- **Worker's interest**: To limit the working day, preserve health, maintain family life
- **Class struggle**: The working day is determined by the struggle between these opposing forces

### Historical Struggle Over the Working Day
**Phases of Regulation:**
1. **No legal limits**: 14-16+ hour days in early industrial capitalism
2. **Limited regulation**: Factory Acts restrict women/children's hours
3. **General regulation**: Gradual extension to all workers, 8-10 hour standards

**Means of Extension:**
- **Direct coercion**: Fines, wage deductions, physical punishment
- **Indirect coercion**: Piece-wages, bonuses, threats of dismissal
- **Ideological pressure**: Preaching of diligence, condemnation of idleness
- **Economic pressure**: Competition among workers, reserve army

## Key Concepts
- **Working Day**: The period during which the worker labours for the capitalist
- **Necessary Labour**: Time to reproduce the value of labour-power
- **Surplus Labour**: Time beyond necessary labour that creates surplus-value
- **Rate of Surplus-Value**: s' = surplus labour / necessary labour
- **Physical Limits**: Biological constraints on labour time
- **Moral Limits**: Social and intellectual needs that require time
- **Factory Acts**: Legal limitations on working hours
- **Class Struggle**: Conflict between capital's drive to extend and labour's resistance to extension

## Mental Models

### Visualize the working day
A------B------C
| Nec. | Surplus |
A-B = Necessary labour (reproduces wage)
B-C = Surplus labour (creates surplus-value)
A-C = Total working day

### Calculate exploitation extension
If capital extends B-C from 2 to 4 hours (with A-B=4):
- Original s' = 2/4 = 50%
- New s' = 4/4 = 100%
- Surplus-value doubles while wage stays same

### Understand the limits
- **Minimum**: Approaches A-B (necessary labour) but can never equal it under capitalism
- **Maximum**: Physical collapse of workers (historically 16-18 hours)
- **Actual**: Determined by class struggle within these bounds

## Key Takeaways
1. The working day divides into necessary and surplus labour portions
2. Capital inherently seeks to maximize surplus labour time
3. Physical and moral limits constrain the working day extension
4. The actual working day is determined by class struggle, not natural laws
5. Historical development shows a trend from unlimited to regulated working hours
6. Factory legislation represents worker victories in the struggle over the working day
