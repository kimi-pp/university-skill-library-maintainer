# Chapter 25: The General Law of Capitalist Accumulation

## Core Idea
Capital accumulation reproduces the capitalist relation on an expanding scale: more capitalists with more capital at one pole, more wage workers at the other. The general law is that accumulation of capital is simultaneously accumulation of the proletariat, producing a relative surplus population (industrial reserve army) that serves capital's needs.

## Frameworks Introduced

### Capital Composition (Three Aspects)
- **Value Composition (c:v)**: Proportion of capital divided into constant capital (means of production value) and variable capital (labour-power value)
- **Technical Composition**: Proportion between mass of means of production and mass of living labour-power employed
- **Organic Composition**: Value composition as determined by technical composition; mirrors changes in technical composition
- **Usage**: When Marx refers to "composition of capital" without qualification, organic composition is meant

### Accumulation with Constant Composition
- **Assumption**: Value composition remains constant (same proportion of c and v)
- **Effect**: Demand for labour and subsistence fund increase proportionally with capital
- **Wage trend**: If accumulation grows faster than labour supply, wages may rise
- **Historical example**: England in 15th century and first half of 18th century experienced wage rises
- **Limitation**: This assumes constant composition, which is not the historical tendency

### The Fundamental Character of Capitalist Production
- **Reproduction of class relations**: Simple reproduction reproduces capitalist/wage-worker relation
- **Expanded reproduction**: Accumulation reproduces the relation "on a progressive scale"
- **Pole development**: More and larger capitalists at one pole; more wage workers at the other
- **Proletariat increase**: "Accumulation of capital is, therefore, increase of the proletariat"

### The Industrial Reserve Army
**Creation Mechanism:**
- **Relative Surplus Population**: Workers beyond capital's immediate employment needs
- **Constant formation**: Accumulation creates reserve army while also employing more workers
- **Function**: Provides flexible labour supply for capital's varying needs
- **Appearance**: Workers appear free to sell labour-power to different capitalists

**Forms of Reserve Army:**
- **Floating**: Workers temporarily unemployed between jobs
- **Latent**: Potential workers in pre-capitalist sectors (agriculture, domestic industry)
- **Stagnant**: Chronically unemployed, irregularly employed

**Effects:**
- **For capital**: Disciplines labour, depresses wages, provides flexible labour supply
- **For workers**: Creates insecurity, competition, poverty
- **For system**: Enables capital to expand and contract production as needed

### The General Law of Capitalist Accumulation
- **Statement**: "The greater the social wealth, the functioning capital, the extent and energy of its growth, and, therefore, also the absolute mass of the proletariat and the productiveness of its labour, the greater is the industrial reserve army. The same causes which develop the expansive power of capital, develop also the labour-power at its disposal. The relative mass of the industrial reserve army increases therefore with the increase of the wealth of capital."
- **Corollary**: "But the greater this reserve army in proportion to the active labour-army, the greater is the mass of a consolidated surplus-population, whose misery is in inverse ratio to its torment. The more extensive, finally, the laissez-faire wage-work, which is the same as the rule of capital over society at large, the greater is the official number of those who sink below the level of a mere vegetable life, and the greater is that of those who are not allowed time enough for this vegetation-time, and, under the pretext that they desire to work, are, at last, through the coaxing of capital and the whip of the State, transformed into professional thieves, or treated as such."
- **Significance**: The law reveals the inherent contradiction of capitalist accumulation: wealth for capital, misery for workers

### Absolute vs. Relative Growth
- **Absolute proletariat growth**: Total number of workers increases with accumulation
- **Relative proletariat growth**: Proportion of workers in population increases
- **Reserve army growth**: Relative mass of reserve army increases with capital's wealth
- **Polarisation**: Wealth accumulation at one pole, poverty accumulation at the other

### Capitalist Apologetics vs. Reality
- **Apologetics**: Claim that capital accumulation benefits workers through employment growth
- **Reality**: Accumulation reproduces class relations and creates a growing reserve army
- **Historical evidence**: England's experience shows accumulation accompanied by worker immiseration
- **Marx's critique**: Classical economists (Smith, Ricardo) understood accumulation's effects on proletariat

## Key Concepts
- **Capital Composition**: Proportion between constant and variable capital
- **Organic Composition**: Value composition determined by technical composition
- **Industrial Reserve Army**: Relative surplus population created by accumulation
- **Floating Reserve**: Temporarily unemployed workers
- **Latent Reserve**: Potential workers in non-capitalist sectors
- **Stagnant Reserve**: Chronically unemployed workers
- **General Law of Accumulation**: Capital accumulation increases both capital and proletariat
- **Pauperism**: The mass of workers sinking below subsistence level

## Key Takeaways
1. Capital accumulation reproduces capitalist class relations on an expanding scale
2. The organic composition of capital tends to rise with accumulation
3. Accumulation simultaneously increases employment and creates a reserve army of labour
4. The industrial reserve army disciplines labour and serves capital's flexible needs
5. The general law: the greater the social wealth, the greater the relative surplus population
6. Capital accumulation produces both wealth and poverty, accumulation and immiseration
7. The law reveals capitalism's inherent contradiction between expansion and exploitation
