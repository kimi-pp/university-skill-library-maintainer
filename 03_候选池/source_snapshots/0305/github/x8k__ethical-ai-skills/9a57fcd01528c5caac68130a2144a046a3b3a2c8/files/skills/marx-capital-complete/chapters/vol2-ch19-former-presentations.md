# Chapter 19: Former Presentations of the Subject

## Core Idea

This chapter provides a critical history of economic thought on reproduction, evaluating the Physiocrats and Adam Smith as the primary pre-Marxian attempts to understand the process. Marx credits Quesnay's Tableau Économique as the first systematic conception of capitalist production, while exposing Adam Smith's retrogressions and the persistent confusion in political economy between capital and revenue. The chapter demonstrates how earlier economists either partially grasped or completely missed the distinction between constant capital replacement and the creation of new value through labour, leading to fundamental errors in their theories of price composition and social reproduction.

---

## Frameworks Introduced

### I. The Physiocrats

#### Quesnay's Tableau Économique
- **Historical Significance**: First systematic conception of capitalist production
- **Analytical Focus**: Distribution of national production's annual result through circulation
- **Purpose**: Demonstrates conditions for simple reproduction (reproduction on same scale)
- **Starting Point**: Preceding year's harvest as production period origin

#### Social Mass Movement
- **Aggregation**: Innumerable individual circulation acts brought together as characteristic social movement
- **Class Circulation**: Movement between great functionally determined economic classes
- **Capital-Value Repository**: Portion of total product as use-object and new labour result simultaneously serves as depository of old capital-value re-appearing in same bodily form
- **Non-Circulating Capital**: Constant capital portion (e.g., in agriculture) remains with producers to resume service as capital

#### Physiocratic Limitations and Strengths
- **Agricultural Focus**: Only agriculture considered sphere of human labour producing surplus-value
- **Natural Reproduction**: Economic reproduction intertwined with natural reproduction in agriculture
- **Clarifying Natural Processes**: Obvious conditions of natural reproduction illuminate economic reproduction
- **Against Circulation Mirage**: Prevents confusion from the mirage of circulation

#### Capitalist Character of Physiocracy
- **System Label Critique**: Systems deceive both buyers and sellers; Physiocrats believed in their feudal shop-sign
- **Capitalist Reality**: Despite feudal appearance, Physiocratic system = first systematic capitalist production conception
- **Class Structure**:
  - **Industrial Capital Representative**: Class of tenants directing entire economic movement
  - **Capitalist Agriculture**: Large-scale capitalist farmer enterprise
  - **Wage-Labour**: Direct cultivators as wage-labourers
  - **Surplus-Value Production**: Production creates use-values and value; motive = surplus-value procurement
  - **Class Distinction**: Capitalist farmer (producer of surplus-value) vs. mere appropriators (landlords)

#### Opposition to Physiocracy
- **Left Critique**: Linguet and Mably challenged capitalist character
- **Right Critique**: Champions of small freeholders opposed large-scale capitalist farming

### II. Adam Smith

#### Smith's Retrogression
- **Paradox**: Despite elaborating Quesnay's correct analyses, Smith relapses into physiocratic errors
- **Progress**: Generalized "avances primitives" (fixed capital) and "avances annuelles" (circulating capital)
- **Retrogression**: Treated fixed vs. circulating as overriding distinction

#### Smith's Agricultural Fetishism
- **Farmer's Superiority**: Claims farmer produces more value than other capitalists
- **Labouring Cattle**: Treats cattle as productive labourers alongside human workers
- **Nature's Contribution**: Argues nature labours with man, creating value without expense
- **Agricultural Rent**: Rent = produce of nature's powers, not labour
- **Value Addition**: Agriculture adds greater value than manufacturing because nature contributes
- **Critique**: Smith fails to see re-appearance of constant capital value in renewed form, missing key reproduction element

#### Seed as Fixed Capital
- **Smith's Definition**: "The whole value of the seed, too, is properly a fixed capital"
- **Non-Circulation**: Seed goes between ground and granary without changing masters
- **Profit Source**: Farmer's profit from increase, not sale
- **Absurdity**: Smith misses capital-value re-appearance that Quesnay understood

#### The Three Component Parts of Price
- **Doctrine**: Every commodity's price resolves into wages, profit, and rent
- **Universal Claim**: In improved societies, all three enter into price of most commodities
- **Revenue Sources**: Wages, profit, rent = three original sources of all revenue and exchangeable value
- **Extension to Annual Produce**: Whole annual produce's price resolves into same three parts

#### The Gross vs. Net Revenue Distinction
- **Gross Revenue**: Whole annual produce of land and labour
- **Net Revenue**: Remainder after deducting expense of maintaining fixed and circulating capital
- **Consumption Fund**: What can be spent on subsistence, conveniences, amusements without encroaching on capital
- **Real Wealth Measure**: Proportional to net, not gross revenue

#### Marx's Critique of Smith's Revenue Theory

**First Critique: Capital as Fourth Element**
- **Assumption**: Smith deals only with simple reproduction, not accumulation
- **Capital Maintenance**: Expenses for "maintaining" capital in operation
- **Consumption Fund**: Net income = portion passing into consumption fund
- **Missing Element**: Portion of individual and social product value resolves into capital, not wages/profit/rent

**Second Critique: The Wordplay**
- **Revenue Ambiguity**: Smith uses "revenue" (French: revenir = to come back) in two senses
- **Revenue Upon Capital**: Value portion replacing expended capital-value = income upon capital
- **Common Income**: Value components parcelled out as wages, profits, rent = commonly called income
- **Contradiction**: Entire product value constitutes somebody's income, but it's both income upon capital and different revenue
- **Logical Error**: Capital brought in through side door via word ambiguity; but capital must first be expended to appear as revenue

**Third Critique: Profit and Capital Maintenance**
- **Lowest Profit Rate**: Must exceed compensation for occasional losses
- **Neat/Clear Profit**: Surplus beyond loss compensation
- **Gross Profit**: Includes both surplus and extraordinary loss compensation
- **Insurance Fund**: Part of surplus-value forms reproduction fund via surplus-labour
- **Fixed Capital Maintenance**: Replacement = renewal of old capital-value in new form, not new outlay
- **Repair Fund**: Expense for fixed capital repair; may be invested gradually from profits
- **Labour Source**: Worker delivers surplus-labour for insurance and repair funds

**Fourth Critique: Exclusion from Net Revenue**
- **Fixed Capital Exclusion**: Entire fixed capital excluded from neat revenue
- **Circulating Capital Portion**: Portion required for fixed capital maintenance/repair/renewal also excluded
- **Consumption Form**: Only capital in bodily form intended for consumption fund counts as revenue
- **Materials vs. Labour Price**: Materials for machines excluded, but wages for workmen producing materials may be included
- **Product Inclusion**: In other labour types, both wages and produce go to consumption stock

#### Later Economists' Failures

**Say's Dogma**
- **Discovery**: Revenue of nation = gross product; no capital to be deducted
- **Implication**: Nation can unproductively consume entire annual product value without future revenue detriment
- **Proudhon's Appropriation**: Adopted Say's discovery

**Storch's Critique and Confusion**
- **Acceptance**: Accepts Smith's doctrine in principle
- **Practical Rejection**: Finds Say's application doesn't hold water
- **Capital Non-Consumability**: Products representing nation's capital are not consumable
- **Inconsistency**: Fails to explain how constant capital exists if commodity values contain only wages and surplus-value
- **Conclusion**: "Impossible to resolve the necessary price into its simplest elements"

**Sismondi**
- **Focus**: Particularly concerned with capital-revenue relation
- **Pretension**: Makes this relation the differentia specifica of his Nouveaux Principes
- **Failure**: Contributes nothing scientific, no clarification of the problem

**Barton, Ramsay, Cherbuliez**
- **Attempt**: Try to advance beyond Smith's formulation
- **Failure**: Founder by posing problem one-sidedly
- **Root Error**: Fail to distinguish constant vs. variable capital-value and fixed vs. circulating capital

**John Stuart Mill**
- **Reproduction**: With usual pomposity, reproduces Smith's doctrine
- **Result**: Smithian confusion persists to present; dogma = orthodox article of faith

## Key Concepts

- **Tableau Économique**: Quesnay's economic table showing class circulation for simple reproduction
- **Physiocratic System**: First systematic capitalist production conception despite feudal appearance
- **Avances Primitives/Annuales**: Quesnay's distinction generalized by Smith as fixed/circulating capital
- **Revenue vs. Capital**: Fundamental confusion in classical economics between income and capital
- **Gross vs. Net Revenue**: Smith's distinction obscuring capital replacement in reproduction
- **Insurance Fund**: Portion of surplus-value reserved for production risks
- **Repair Fund**: Portion of surplus-value for fixed capital maintenance
- **Surplus-Labour Allocation**: Workers' unpaid labour creates capital for reproduction, insurance, and repair
- **Price Component Errors**: Classical attempts to resolve commodity prices into wages, profit, rent only

---

## Key Takeaways

1. **Physiocratic Achievement**: Quesnay's Tableau Économique provides first systematic capitalist reproduction analysis

2. **Agricultural Clarity**: Physiocrats' agricultural focus, while limited, illuminated reproduction processes by connecting economic and natural reproduction

3. **Smith's Dual Legacy**: Adam Smith both advanced (generalizing capital concepts) and regressed (agricultural fetishism, revenue-capital confusion) economic thought

4. **Capital-Value Reappearance**: Smith's failure to see constant capital value re-appearance in new forms represents fundamental gap in his reproduction theory

5. **Revenue Concept Confusion**: Smith's gross/net revenue distinction uses wordplay to obscure capital's role in reproduction

6. **Labour as Capital Source**: Surplus-labour creates not only surplus-value for consumption but also capital for reproduction, insurance, and repair

7. **Fixed Capital Misunderstanding**: Smith's treatment of seed as fixed capital reveals inability to trace capital-value through production circuits

8. **Classical Economics' Core Error**: Persistent failure to distinguish between capital replacement and new value creation

9. **Later Economists' Failure**: From Storch to Mill, economists perpetuate Smith's confusion rather than resolve it

10. **Historical Significance**: These errors demonstrate the necessity of Marx's systematic reconstruction of reproduction theory
