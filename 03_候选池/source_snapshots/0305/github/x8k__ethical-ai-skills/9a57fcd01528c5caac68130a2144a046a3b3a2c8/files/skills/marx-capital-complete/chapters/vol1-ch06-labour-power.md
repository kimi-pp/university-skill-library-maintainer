# Chapter 6: The Buying and Selling of Labour-Power

## Core Idea
The solution to the contradiction in the M-C-M' circuit is found in a special commodity: labour-power. This is the only commodity whose use-value (labour) creates more value than its own value, enabling capital to expand itself. The chapter explains the conditions for labour-power to become a commodity and how its value is determined.

## Frameworks Introduced

### Labour-Power as the Special Commodity
**Use when**: Understanding the unique nature of the commodity that solves capital's contradiction
- **Definition**: "By labour-power or capacity for labour is to be understood the aggregate of those mental and physical capabilities existing in a human being, which he exercises whenever he produces a use-value of any description"
- **Unique property**: Its use-value (labour) is itself an embodiment of labour and a creation of value
- **The key**: "The possessor of money does find on the market such a special commodity in capacity for labour or labour-power"
- **Result**: The consumption of labour-power by the capitalist produces surplus-value

### The Two Essential Conditions for Labour-Power as Commodity
**Use when**: Analyzing the historical preconditions for capitalism

**Condition 1: The Free Labourer**
- **Personal freedom**: The worker must be the untrammelled owner of their capacity for labour, i.e., of their person
- **Legal equality**: Worker and capitalist meet as equal rights-bearing individuals in the market
- **Temporal limitation**: Labour-power is sold only for a definite period, not permanently
- **Why**: Selling labour-power permanently would convert the worker from a free person into a slave
- **Significance**: The worker maintains ownership of their labour-power even while selling its use

**Condition 2: The Propertyless Worker**
- **Separation from means of production**: The worker must have no other commodity to sell besides labour-power
- **No independent production**: The worker cannot produce and sell their own commodities
- **Dependence on the market**: The worker must sell labour-power to obtain means of subsistence
- **Double freedom**: "Free in the double sense, that as a free man he can dispose of his labour-power as his own commodity, and that on the other hand he has no other commodity for sale"
- **Historical product**: This relation has no natural basis; it is "the result of a past historical development, the product of many economic revolutions"

### The Value of Labour-Power
**Use when**: Calculating wages and understanding exploitation
- **Determination**: "The value of labour-power is determined, as in the case of every other commodity, by the labour-time necessary for the production, and consequently also the reproduction, of this special article"
- **What it covers**: The value of the means of subsistence necessary for the maintenance of the labourer
- **Reproduction**: Includes the labour-time necessary for the production of the worker's means of subsistence
- **Historical and moral element**: "The value of labour-power resolves itself into the value of a definite quantity of the means of subsistence"
- **Family consideration**: Includes the means of subsistence necessary for the worker's family (reproduction of the working class)

### The Maintenance of Labour-Power
**Use when**: Understanding the difference between labour and labour-power
- **Labour-power vs. Labour**: Labour-power exists as capacity; it becomes reality only through its exercise (labour)
- **Wear and tear**: Labour consumes a definite quantity of human muscle, nerve, brain, etc., which must be restored
- **Daily maintenance**: The worker requires a given quantity of means of subsistence for their maintenance
- **Extended reproduction**: The worker's family must be maintained to reproduce the working class
- **Variable needs**: The quantity and quality of means of subsistence vary historically and by country

### Labour-Power as a Historical Category
**Use when**: Understanding that capitalism is not eternal
- **Not natural**: "Nature does not produce on the one side owners of money or commodities, and on the other men possessing nothing but their own labour-power"
- **Historical development**: The separation of workers from means of production is "the product of many economic revolutions"
- **Capital's announcement**: "Capital, therefore, announces from its first appearance a new epoch in the process of social production"
- **Preconditions**: The historical conditions of capital's existence are "by no means given with the mere circulation of money and commodities"
- **World history**: The creation of the free, propertyless worker "comprises a world's history"

## Key Concepts
- **Labour-Power**: The capacity for labour; a special commodity whose use-value creates value
- **Free Labourer**: A worker who owns their own labour-power and can sell it as a commodity
- **Double Freedom**: Free to sell labour-power AND free from ownership of means of production
- **Value of Labour-Power**: Determined by the labour-time necessary to produce the worker's means of subsistence
- **Means of Subsistence**: The goods necessary for the worker's maintenance and reproduction
- **Historical Preconditions**: The economic revolutions that separated workers from means of production
- **Primitive Accumulation**: The historical process that created the conditions for capitalism (covered in Ch26-32)
- **Wage**: The price of labour-power; appears as payment for labour but is actually payment for the capacity to work

## Mental Models

### Trace the source of surplus-value
Use this when analyzing capitalist profit:
1. **Money cannot expand itself**: Money as means of purchase or payment cannot change its own value
2. **Re-sale cannot create value**: Selling a commodity only transforms it back to money-form at its existing value
3. **The change must be in use**: The surplus must come from the use-value of the commodity purchased
4. **Labour-power is the answer**: Its use-value (labour) creates more value than its exchange-value (wage)

### Apply the two conditions test
Use this when examining whether labour can be exploited:
- **Condition 1**: Is the worker free to sell their labour-power? (Yes/No)
- **Condition 2**: Does the worker lack other commodities to sell? (Yes/No)
- **Result**: Only when both conditions are met can capital exploit labour

### Calculate the value of labour-power
Use this when analyzing wages:
1. Identify the means of subsistence necessary for worker maintenance
2. Calculate the labour-time required to produce those means of subsistence
3. Adjust for historical and moral elements (cultural standards, family size, etc.)
4. **Result**: This is the value of labour-power, which determines the wage

### Distinguish labour from labour-power
Use this when understanding Marx's terminology:
- **Labour-Power**: The capacity, the ability to work (what is sold)
- **Labour**: The actual exercise of labour-power, the work performed (what creates value)
- **Why it matters**: Capitalists buy labour-power, but consume labour; the difference produces surplus-value

## Anti-patterns

### Confusing labour with labour-power
**What to avoid**: Using "labour" and "labour-power" interchangeably
**Why it fails**: Labour is the activity that creates value; labour-power is the capacity sold as a commodity. Confusing them obscures the source of surplus-value.
**Correct approach**: Always distinguish: labour-power is sold, labour is consumed by capital, surplus-value is produced

### Naturalizing wage labour
**What to avoid**: Treating the wage labour relation as natural and eternal
**Why it fails**: The free, propertyless worker is a historical product, not a natural condition. Capitalism required the violent separation of workers from means of production.
**Correct approach**: Recognize that wage labour is historically specific and requires specific social conditions

### Individualizing the labour contract
**What to avoid**: Analyzing the sale of labour-power as a purely individual transaction
**Why it fails**: The contract appears as an equal exchange between free individuals, but it conceals the historical and social conditions that force the worker to sell their labour-power
**Correct approach**: Understand the labour contract within its historical context of class relations and primitive accumulation

### Neglecting the reproduction aspect
**What to avoid**: Calculating the value of labour-power based only on individual subsistence
**Why it fails**: The working class must be reproduced across generations; family maintenance is part of labour-power's value
**Correct approach**: Include the costs of raising the next generation of workers in the value of labour-power

### Accepting the wage as payment for labour
**What to avoid**: Believing that wages pay for the actual labour performed
**Why it fails**: Wages pay for the capacity to work (labour-power), not for the labour itself. The worker produces more value in labour than they receive in wages.
**Correct approach**: Wages are the price of labour-power; surplus-value is the unpaid labour performed beyond what the wage covers

## Key Takeaways

1. **Labour-power is the missing piece**: It is the special commodity whose use-value creates more value than its exchange-value, solving the contradiction in the M-C-M' circuit.

2. **Double freedom is the precondition**: Workers must be free to sell their labour-power AND free from ownership of means of production for capitalism to exist.

3. **Labour-power's value is social**: Determined by the labour-time necessary to produce the worker's means of subsistence, including family reproduction.

4. **The contract conceals exploitation**: The labour contract appears as a free exchange between equals, but it conceals the historical violence that created the conditions for wage labour.

5. **Capitalism is historically specific**: The separation of workers from means of production is not natural but the result of "a past historical development, the product of many economic revolutions."

6. **Labour-power vs. labour distinction**: Capitalists buy the capacity to work (labour-power), but consume actual labour, which produces surplus-value.

7. **Wages are not payment for labour**: Wages pay for the capacity to work; surplus-value is the unpaid portion of the labour performed.
