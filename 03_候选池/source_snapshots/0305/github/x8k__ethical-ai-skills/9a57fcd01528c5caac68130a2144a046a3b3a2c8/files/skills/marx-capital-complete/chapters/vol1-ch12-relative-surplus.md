# Chapter 12: The Concept of Relative Surplus-Value

## Core Idea
Relative surplus-value is produced by reducing necessary labour time through increased productivity, allowing more surplus labour within the same working day. Unlike absolute surplus-value (extending the working day), relative surplus-value relies on technical and organizational improvements that cheapen the worker's means of subsistence.

## Frameworks Introduced

### Absolute vs. Relative Surplus-Value
- **Absolute**: Extends surplus labour by lengthening the working day (B-C increases)
- **Relative**: Reduces necessary labour (A-B decreases) while keeping working day constant
- **Combined**: Capital uses both methods simultaneously to maximize surplus-value

### Productivity and Necessary Labour
- **Mechanism**: Increased productivity → cheaper commodities → lower value of labour-power
- **Effect**: Necessary labour time (A-B) decreases → surplus labour (B-C) increases proportionally
- **Example**: If productivity doubles in wage-goods industries, A-B halves, s' doubles

### The Cheapening of Labour-Power
- **Means of Subsistence**: Increased productivity in industries producing wage goods (food, clothing, housing)
- **Luxury Goods**: Productivity increases here don't affect v (labour-power value)
- **Result**: Only productivity in wage-goods sectors reduces necessary labour time

### Capital's Dual Role
- **Individual Capital**: Adopts productivity improvements to gain competitive advantage
- **Total Capital**: The sum of all capitals determines the value of labour-power
- **Contradiction**: Individual capital benefits from productivity, but total capital's surplus-value rate depends on social productivity

## Key Concepts
- **Relative Surplus-Value**: Surplus-value from reduced necessary labour time
- **Absolute Surplus-Value**: Surplus-value from extended working day
- **Necessary Labour**: Time to reproduce labour-power value
- **Productivity**: Output per labour hour; affects value of commodities
- **Wage-Goods**: Commodities consumed by workers; their cheapening reduces v
- **Luxury Goods**: Commodities not consumed by workers; don't affect v

## Key Takeaways
1. Relative surplus-value comes from productivity increases, not working day extension
2. Only productivity in wage-goods industries affects the value of labour-power
3. Capital simultaneously pursues absolute and relative surplus-value production
4. Relative surplus-value appears as a natural result of technological progress
5. The social average productivity determines the value of labour-power
