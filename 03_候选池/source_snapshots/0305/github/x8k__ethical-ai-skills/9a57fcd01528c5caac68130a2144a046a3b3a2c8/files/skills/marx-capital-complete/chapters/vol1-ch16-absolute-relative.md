# Chapter 16: Absolute and Relative Surplus-Value

## Core Idea
Absolute surplus-value (extending the working day) forms the general foundation of the capitalist system, while relative surplus-value (revolutionizing production techniques) builds upon it. The chapter also clarifies the capitalist definition of productive labour: labour that produces surplus-value for capital.

## Frameworks Introduced

### Absolute vs. Relative Surplus-Value Comparison
- **Absolute Surplus-Value**:
  - Method: Prolonging the working day beyond necessary labour time
  - Focus: Length of the working day
  - Historical role: "Forms the general groundwork of the capitalist system"
  - Starting-point for relative surplus-value production

- **Relative Surplus-Value**:
  - Method: Shortening necessary labour time through increased productivity
  - Focus: Revolutionizing technical processes of labour and society
  - Requirement: Presupposes working day already divided into necessary and surplus labour
  - Historical role: Develops on the foundation of absolute surplus-value

### Formal vs. Real Subjection of Labour to Capital
- **Formal Subjection**: Worker sells labour-power to capitalist; labour-process remains essentially unchanged
- **Real Subjection**: Capital controls and revolutionizes the labour-process itself
- **Transition**: Production of relative surplus-value replaces formal with real subjection
- **Result**: Complete transformation of production methods under capital's control

### Productive Labour Under Capitalism
- **Capitalist Definition**: "That labourer alone is productive, who produces surplus-value for the capitalist"
- **Not about use-value**: Labour can produce material objects but not be productive (if no surplus-value)
- **Not about individual work**: Even non-manual labour (schoolmaster example) can be productive
- **Social relation**: Productive labour implies a specific social relation that stamps the labourer as creator of surplus-value
- **Historical development**: Definition changes with economists' comprehension of surplus-value

### Productive Labour in Different Schools
- **Physiocrats**: Only agricultural labour is productive (because only agriculture yields surplus-value in form of rent)
- **Adam Smith**: Labour that produces material objects is productive (confuses use-value with surplus-value creation)
- **Marx**: Labour that produces surplus-value for capital is productive (class-based definition)
- **Example**: Schoolmaster is productive when enriching the school proprietor (creating surplus-value)

### Intermediate Forms of Exploitation
- **Usurer's Capital**: Feeds on independent producers like a parasite
- **Merchant's Capital**: Exploits producers without direct control of labour-process
- **Historical role**: These forms serve as transition to capitalist mode of production
- **Exclusion**: Predominance of these forms excludes capitalist production proper

## Key Concepts
- **Absolute Surplus-Value**: Surplus-value from extending the working day
- **Relative Surplus-Value**: Surplus-value from reducing necessary labour time through productivity
- **Formal Subjection**: Labour controlled by capital but process essentially unchanged
- **Real Subjection**: Labour process transformed and controlled by capital
- **Productive Labour**: Labour that produces surplus-value for capital
- **Unproductive Labour**: Labour that doesn't produce surplus-value (even if useful)
- **Intermediate Forms**: Usurer's and merchant's capital as transitional exploitation forms

## Key Takeaways
1. Absolute surplus-value is the foundation; relative surplus-value develops upon it
2. Relative surplus-value requires revolutionary changes in production techniques
3. Productive labour under capitalism is defined by surplus-value creation, not material production
4. The definition of productive labour has changed historically with understanding of surplus-value
5. Capitalist production develops from formal to real subjection of labour
6. Intermediate forms of exploitation (usury, merchant capital) precede full capitalist production
