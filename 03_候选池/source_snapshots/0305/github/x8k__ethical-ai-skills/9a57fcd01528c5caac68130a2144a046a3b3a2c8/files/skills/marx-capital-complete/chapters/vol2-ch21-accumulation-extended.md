# Chapter 21: Accumulation and Reproduction on an Extended Scale

## Core Idea

This chapter extends the social reproduction analysis from Chapter 20 to examine reproduction on an expanded scale through accumulation. Marx demonstrates that while individual capitalist accumulation involves converting surplus-value into additional productive capital, the social reproduction process must account for how this accumulation occurs across both departments simultaneously. The chapter reveals the complex mechanisms by which surplus-value is hoarded as potential money-capital, then converted into additional constant and variable capital, requiring proportional adjustments between Department I (means of production) and Department II (articles of consumption). The analysis shows that capitalist accumulation inherently creates imbalances and possibilities for crisis, while also revealing the conditions for expanded reproduction.

---

## Frameworks Introduced

### I. From Individual to Social Accumulation

#### Individual Capitalist Accumulation (Recap from Volume I)
- **Surplus-Value Conversion**: Commodity-capital → money converts surplus-product (bearer of surplus-value) into money
- **Reinvestment**: Surplus-value in money-form reconverted into additional natural elements of productive capital
- **Expanded Production**: Increased capital furnishes increased product in next cycle
- **Assumptions**: Surplus-value amount sufficient for expansion; production on extended scale already in process

#### Social Accumulation Parallel
- **Fixed Capital Precipitation**: Successive precipitation of used-up fixed capital components in money form (hoarding) finds expression in annual social reproduction
- **Example**: Individual capital = 400c + 100v, annual surplus-value = 100
  - Commodity-product = 400c + 100v + 100s = 600
  - Money conversion: 400c → constant capital natural form, 100v → labour-power, 100s → additional constant capital
- **Two Possibilities**:
  1. Surplus-value immediately converted to productive elements
  2. Surplus-value hoarded as money for longer period before real accumulation

#### Accumulation Requirements
- **Market Availability**: Elements of productive capital must be purchasable as commodities
- **Potential Existence**: Elements must exist potentially in their elements; order/anticipated sale sufficient to trigger production
- **Money's Role**: Money calls forth extended reproduction because possibility exists without money; money itself not element of real reproduction

#### Hoarding vs. Accumulation
- **Capitalist A's Hoarding**: Sells commodities including surplus-product over years, converts surplus-value to money, gradually accumulates potential money-capital
- **Hoarding Nature**: Simple hoarding not element of actual reproduction; mere withdrawal of circulating money from circulation
- **Money Origin**: Hoarded money may have been part of other hoards, wage money, commodity money, constant capital portions, or capitalist revenue
- **Not New Wealth**: Hoarded surplus-value not additional social wealth, but represents new potential money-capital by function
- **Gold Exception**: Only gold production creates new wealth (potential money) as surplus-product

### II. The Hoarding Paradox and Its Resolution

#### The General Process Problem
- **Universal Hoarding**: If all capitalists sell to hoard and none buy, where do buyers come from?
- **Contradiction**: Every individual capital may be in accumulation process simultaneously

#### Straight-Line Circulation Fallacy
- **Wrong Assumption**: Conceiving circulation as straight line rather than mutually opposite movements
- **Gold Producer Solution**: Start from gold producer who buys without selling; all others sell to him
- **Implication**: Entire yearly social surplus-product passes to gold producer; others distribute his surplus-product (money-form)
- **Limitation**: Explains universal hoard formation possibility but not reproduction advancement

#### Departmental Distinction
- **Resolution Approach**: Distinguish between accumulation in Department I and Department II
- **Starting Point**: Analysis begins with Department I (production of means of production)

### III. Accumulation in Department I

#### Stages of Accumulation
- **Diverse Capital Ages**: Different individual capitals in various stages of surplus-value to money-capital transformation
- **Two Categories**:
  1. **Active Accumulators**: Converting potential money-capital (grown to appropriate size) into productive capital
  2. **Hoarding Accumulators**: Still engaged in hoarding potential money-capital
- **Market Roles**: Hoarders as sellers (without buying); active accumulators as buyers (without selling)

#### The Hoard Formation Process
- **Example**: A sells 600 (400c + 100v + 100s) to B
  - A receives 600 in money, withdraws 100 (surplus-value) from circulation as hoard
  - 100 in money = money-form of surplus-product (value bearer of 100)
  - Hoarding = no production, no increment; merely withdrawing and impounding money
- **Multiple Points**: Numerous capitalists A', A'', A''' engaged in hoard formation simultaneously
- **Circulation Obstacles**: Hoard formation points immobilize money, depriving circulation of capacity
- **Historical Precedent**: Hoarding exists in simple commodity circulation before capitalist production

#### Hoarding and Commodity Supply
- **A's Action**: Withdraws money equal to surplus-value from circulation, but throws commodities into circulation without withdrawing commodities in return
- **B's Enablement**: Capitalists B, B', B'' enabled to throw money into circulation and withdraw only commodities
- **Commodity Destination**: These commodities enter constant capital of B, B', etc. as fixed or circulating elements

#### Exchange Complexity Beyond Simple Barter
- **Reproduction Requirements**: Exchange of annual product component parts must comprise capital reproduction in all determinations (constant, variable, fixed, circulating, money- and commodity-capital)
- **Not Mere Exchange**: Not simple commodity purchase + subsequent sale or vice versa
- **Fixed Capital Renewal**: Periodical renewal presupposes mere purchase of fixed part (reconverted from money) and mere sale of depreciation value
- **Balance Requirement**: Mere purchases must be offset by mere sales of equal value magnitude

#### Normal Exchange Conditions
- **One-Sided Exchanges**: Capitalist production necessitates one-sided metamorphoses (purchases without sales, sales without purchases)
- **Balance Condition**: Value of one-sided purchases must equal value of one-sided sales
- **Money's Role**: Money functions as medium of circulation AND money-capital in capitalist production
- **Crisis Potential**: Balance itself accidental due to spontaneous production nature; normal conditions become abnormal possibilities

### IV. Accumulation in Department II

#### The Money Source Problem
- **Initial Question**: How is capital consumed in production replaced in value from annual product, and how does this replacement intertwine with surplus-value and wage consumption?
- **Department II Challenge**: Where does money for accumulation come from in articles of consumption production?

#### Department II's Disadvantage
- **No Internal Hoard Formation**: Capitalists II sell to and buy from each other; money functions as circulation medium, flowing back to advance it again
- **Two Hoard Formation Ways**:
  1. **Cheating**: One part of capitalists II cheats another, robbing them of money
  2. **Variable Capital Conversion**: Part of II represented by necessities directly converted into new variable capital

#### Wage Depression Rejection
- **Potential Method**: Depressing wages below normal level releases variable capital money for hoarding
- **Critique**: Not normal capital formation; wages must be paid under given relations
- **Invalid Assumption**: Cannot assume 350v paid instead of 376v to solve theoretical difficulty

#### Truck System and Currency Falsification
- **Historical Practice**: Nominal wage payment with partial theft via truck system or currency falsification
- **Critique**: Same as wage depression, just disguised; must be rejected
- **Bourgeois Criticism**: Marx's critics accuse him of wronging capitalists by assuming they pay labour-power at real value

#### The 376 IIv Problem
- **Variable Capital Return**: 376 IIv money-capital continually returns through commodity II purchases as variable capital in money-form
- **No Net Addition**: Constant repetition doesn't add to circulating money; cannot be withdrawn for hoard formation

### V. Numerical Illustrations of Accumulation

#### First Illustration: From Simple to Extended Reproduction

**Simple Reproduction (Scheme A):**
```
I. 4,000c + 1,000v + 1,000s = 6,000
II. 2,000c + 500v + 500s  = 3,000
Total: 9,000
```

**Initial Extended Reproduction (Scheme B):**
```
I. 4,000c + 1,000v + 1,000s = 6,000
II. 1,500c + 750v + 750s  = 3,000
Total: 9,000
```

**Assumption**: Half of I's surplus-value (500) accumulated
- **Replacement**: (1,000v + 500s) I = 1,500 replaced by 1,500 IIc (simple reproduction process)
- **Remaining in I**: 4,000c + 500s (to be accumulated)
- **Capitalisation**: 400s → constant capital, 100s → variable capital

**Accumulation Process:**
1. 400s exchanged within I, annexed to Ic → I: 4,400c + 1,000v + 100s
2. II buys 100 Is (means of production) for accumulation → additional constant capital II
3. II pays 100 in money → converted to money-form of additional variable capital of I
4. I: 4,400c + 1,100v (money) = 5,500
5. II: 1,600c; needs additional 50v for new labour-power → variable capital grows to 800
6. II surplus-value: 600s remains as consumption-fund

**Resulting Capital:**
```
I. 4,400c + 1,100v = 5,500
II. 1,600c + 800v = 2,400
Total Capital: 7,900
```

**Product After One Year:**
```
I. 4,400c + 1,100v + 1,100s = 6,600
II. 1,600c + 800v + 800s   = 3,200
Total: 9,800
```

#### Continued Accumulation Over Five Years

**Year 2:**
- I: 550s accumulated (440c + 110v)
- II: 150s used for accumulation (100c + 50v)
- Capital adjustment required: I(v + s) = 1,650 > IIc = 1,600 → 50 from IIs added to IIc
- Final capitals: I: 4,840c + 1,210v = 6,050; II: 1,760c + 880v = 2,640

**Year 3:**
```
I. 5,324c + 1,331v + 1,331s = 7,986
II. 1,936c + 968v + 968s  = 3,872
Total: 11,858
```

**Year 4:**
```
I. 5,856c + 1,464v + 1,464s = 8,784
II. 2,129c + 1,065v + 1,065s = 4,259
Total: 13,043
```

**Year 5:**
```
I. 6,442c + 1,610v + 1,610s = 9,662
II. 2,342c + 1,172v + 1,172s = 4,686
Total: 14,348
```

**Five-Year Growth:**
- **Initial Capital**: 5,500c + 1,750v = 7,250
- **Final Capital**: 8,784c + 2,782v = 11,566
- **Growth Ratio**: 100:160
- **Surplus-Value Growth**: 1,750 → 2,782
- **Consumed Surplus-Value**: 1,100 → 1,477 (ratio 100:134)

#### Second Illustration: Different Capital Composition

**Assumption**: General average ratio v:c = 1:5 in annual product of 9,000
- **Implication**: Considerable development of productive power, large constant capital component
- **Department I**: Produces 5,500 in means of production
- **Department II**: Produces 3,500 in articles of consumption

#### Accumulation Dynamics Analysis

**Three Cases of I(v + s) vs. IIc:**
1. **I(v + ½s) = IIc**: IIc smaller than I(v + s); must always be so for I to accumulate
2. **I(v + ½s) > IIc**: Replacement effected by adding portion of IIs to IIc; implies accumulation in II
3. **I(v + ½s) < IIc**: II doesn't fully reproduce constant capital via exchange; must purchase from I; no further II variable capital accumulation

**Key Finding**: Under capitalist production, I(v + s) cannot equal IIc; two cannot balance in mutual exchange
- **Reason**: Capitalist accumulation excludes this equality
- **Exception**: May occur temporarily due to accumulation course in preceding periods
- **Resolution**: Requires great crash transferring capital from II to I

**Modified Equality**: I(v + s/x) may equal, be larger, or smaller than IIc, where s/x = portion of Is spent as revenue
- **Constraint**: I(v + s/x) must always be smaller than II(c + s) by portion of IIs consumed by capitalist class II

### VI. Fixed Capital and Accumulation

#### Fixed Capital Replacement Considerations
- **Annual Variation**: Portion of fixed capital IIc expiring varies yearly
- **Inverse Relationship**: As expiring portion increases, portion to be replaced in money decreases (total fixed capital constant)
- **Production Implications**:
  - Greater fixed capital IIc expiration → greater portion of I's commodity-capital for fixed capital IIc
  - Smaller portion for circulating components
  - More money flows from II to I for fixed capital renewal

#### Crisis Tendencies from Fixed Capital
- **Overproduction Possibility**: Disproportion between fixed and circulating capital production can cause crisis even under simple reproduction
- **Two Scenarios**:
  1. **Expiring Capital Increase**: Circulating reproduction same, fixed reproduction increase → I production must grow or reproduction deficit
  2. **Expiring Capital Decrease**: Fixed reproduction decrease → I production must curtail or surplus not convertible to money
- **Resolution Limits**: Labour productivity increases or capital/labour shifting causes disturbances; depreciation or surplus results in crisis

#### Foreign Trade and Fixed Capital
- **Temporary Relief**: Foreign trade can help convert money surplus or dispose of commodity surplus
- **Limitation**: Transfers contradictions to wider sphere, gives greater latitude but doesn't resolve

#### Socialist Planning Contrast
- **Control Requirement**: Continuous relative overproduction required for stable reproduction
- **Social Control**: Over-production = society's control over material reproduction means
- **Capitalist Anarchy**: Within capitalism, this becomes element of anarchy

### VII. The Reproduction of the Money Material (Brief)

#### Gold Production in Accumulation Context
- **Department I**: Gold production belongs to means of production category
- **Money Supply**: Annual gold production = 30 (20c + 5v + 5s) in example
- **Exchange**: 20c exchanged for other Ic elements; 5v + 5s exchanged for IIc elements
- **Wage Payment**: Gold producers buy labour-power with existing money supply

#### Money Supply Dynamics
- **New Money Effect**: Annual reproduction changes money quantity in circulation
- **Partial Return**: Not all advanced money returns; portion remains as hoard
- **Hoard Function**: Additional money serves as latent money-capital

### VIII. Supplementary Remarks on Money Sources and Hoarding

#### Gold Industry's Role
- **Original Source**: Money for II originates from v + s of gold industry I exchanged for part of IIc
- **Accumulation Limitation**: v + s of gold producer doesn't enter II to extent he accumulates surplus-value or converts to means of production I
- **Eventual Expansion**: Gold producer's money accumulation leads to reproduction on extended scale; portion of surplus-value passes as additional variable capital to II

#### Hoard Formation Mechanisms
- **For Department I**: Part of Is sold one-sidedly to II, serves as additional constant capital II
- **For Department II**: Part of Is bought one-sidedly by I for additional variable capital; part of surplus-value spent by I as revenue not covered by IIc, converting part of IIs to money

#### I(v + s/x) vs. IIc Relationship
- **Normal Case**: I(v + s/x) may be equal to, larger, or smaller than IIc
- **Constraint**: I(v + s/x) must always be smaller than II(c + s) by portion of IIs consumed by capitalist class II

#### Direct Accumulation in Department II
- **Internal Mechanism**: Direct conversion of portion of IIs into variable capital
- **Age Categories**: Various stages of accumulation within II's lines of business
- **Exchange Dynamics**: Some sell without buying (hoarding); others buy without selling (expansion)
- **Money Flow**: Additional variable money-capital invested in additional labour-power; labour-power buys means of subsistence from hoarding owners; money doesn't return to point of departure

### IX. Resolution of the Mill-Bailey Controversy

#### The Accumulation Debate
- **James Mill Position**: Capital accumulation possible without changing capital magnitude
- **S. Bailey Position**: Opposed Mill's view
- **Marx's Resolution**: Analysis demonstrates accumulation mechanisms, resolving controversy discussed in Volume I, Chapter 24

## Key Concepts

- **Social Accumulation**: Accumulation at level of total social capital, not just individual capitals
- **Potential Money-Capital**: Surplus-value hoarded in money-form, not yet functioning as capital
- **Hoard Formation**: Withdrawal of money from circulation for future capital expansion
- **Departmental Accumulation**: Different accumulation patterns in Department I vs. Department II
- **One-Sided Metamorphoses**: Purchases without sales, sales without purchases in capitalist reproduction
- **Accumulation Balance**: Equilibrium between hoard formation and productive capital expansion
- **Expanded Reproduction Schemes**: Mathematical models of accumulation over multiple years
- **Fixed Capital Replacement**: Annual variation in expiring fixed capital portions
- **Crisis from Disproportion**: Imbalances between fixed and circulating capital as crisis sources
- **Money Material Reproduction**: Annual gold production as part of accumulation process
- **Direct Accumulation**: Immediate conversion of surplus-value into additional capital without hoarding

---

## Key Takeaways

1. **Social Accumulation Mechanism**: Individual capitalist accumulation must be understood within total social reproduction context

2. **Hoarding Function**: Hoarded surplus-value represents potential money-capital, enabling future expanded reproduction

3. **Departmental Interdependence**: Accumulation in Department I requires proportional adjustments in Department II, and vice versa

4. **Equilibrium Conditions**: I(v + s) cannot equal IIc under capitalist production; imbalance inherent in accumulation process

5. **One-Sided Exchanges**: Normal capitalist reproduction requires purchases without sales and sales without purchases, balanced by value equality

6. **Accumulation Dynamics**: Mathematical illustrations show how accumulation compounds over years, transforming social capital composition

7. **Fixed Capital Complexity**: Annual variations in fixed capital expiration create potential for disproportion and crisis

8. **Foreign Trade Limitation**: While foreign trade can temporarily resolve imbalances, it transfers contradictions to wider sphere

9. **Money Supply Growth**: Annual gold production increases money material for potential money-capitals

10. **Theoretical Resolution**: Marx's analysis resolves classical controversies (Mill-Bailey debate) by demonstrating actual accumulation mechanisms
