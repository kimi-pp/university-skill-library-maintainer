# Chapter 14: The Time of Circulation

## Core Idea
While previous chapters examined factors distinguishing turnover periods originating in production (fixed vs. circulating capital, working periods), this chapter focuses on how differences in circulation time affect turnover. The time of circulation, particularly selling time, significantly impacts turnover periods and capital advance requirements. Geographic distance, market conditions, transportation infrastructure, and credit terms all influence circulation time and thus the overall turnover of capital.

## Frameworks Introduced

### Circulation Time and Turnover
- **Turnover composition**: Turnover time = production time + circulation time
- **Circulation time impact**: Differences in circulation time cause differences in turnover time and period length
- **Isolation method**: To understand effect, compare two capitals with equal production conditions but varying circulation times

### Selling Time as Key Factor
- **Most decisive section**: Selling time (period during which capital exists as commodity-capital) is relatively most decisive part of circulation time
- **Turnover correlation**: Circulation time, hence turnover period, lengthen/shorten with selling time length
- **Storage costs**: Additional capital outlay may be necessary for storage expenses

### Individual Capital Variation
- **Same industry differences**: Time required for finished goods sale may differ considerably among individual capitalists in same branch
- **Aggregate capital**: Differences apply not only to capitals in different branches but also to individual capitals within same sphere
- **Market fluctuations**: Selling period varies with general market fluctuations or particular line of business fluctuations
- **Individual factors**: Differences arise when circumstances operate individually (faster selling, shorter working periods, etc.)

### Geographic Distance Effects
- **Market distance**: Permanent cause of selling time differentiation
- **Transport time**: During trip to market, capital fettered in commodity-capital state
- **Made-to-order**: Up to delivery time; if not made to order, add time for market waiting
- **Communication improvement**: Sailing vessels/steamships shorten travelling equally for near and distant ports
- **Relative difference persistence**: Relative circulation time differences remain, though often diminished
- **Shifting differences**: Development may shift relative differences without corresponding to geographical distances
- **Example**: Railway to inland centre may lengthen relative distance to nearer inland point not connected
- **Economic impact**: Relative distance changes explain deterioration of old centres and rise of new ones
- **Cost factor**: Long hauls relatively cheaper than short ones

### Transportation Development Effects
- **Velocity acceleration**: Development accelerates movement velocity, shortening geographic distance in time
- **Mass development**: More communication facilities (simultaneous vessels/trains)
- **Successive shipments**: Commodities can start passage at shorter intervals
- **Market distribution**: Successive shipments reach market one after another without accumulating
- **Return distribution**: Capital return distributed over shorter successive periods
- **Turnover abridgement**: Total circulation time and turnover abridged by spreading return
- **Frequency increase**: Means of transportation function more frequently
- **Concentration effect**: Development tends toward existing markets, great centres of production/population, export ports
- **Capital concentration**: Accelerated turnover leads to quicker concentration of production centres and markets
- **Mass concentration**: Accelerated concentration of masses of men and capital at certain points
- **Ownership concentration**: Concentration of capital masses in fewer hands
- **Locational shifting**: Changes in transport facilities cause shifting/relocation of production places and markets

### Local Competition
- **Railway wrangling**: Importance evidenced by commercial/industrial representatives wrangling with railway managements over service
- **Bluebook reference**: Railway Committee reports document these struggles

### Market Dependency
- **Local consumption**: Branches dependent mainly on local consumption (breweries) develop most in population centres
- **Turnover compensation**: Rapid turnover compensates for higher costs (building lots, etc.)

### World Market Development
- **Dual effect**: While transportation improvement reduces circulation time for particular commodity quantities, capitalist progress makes it imperative to work for more remote markets (world-market)
- **Mass in transit**: Mass of commodities in transit for distant places grows enormously
- **Social capital**: Part of social capital remaining in commodity-capital stage grows absolutely and relatively
- **Infrastructure investment**: Growing investment in means of transportation/communication and required capital

### Circulation Time Components
- **Selling time**: First part of circulation time
- **Buying time**: Second part — reconversion of money into productive capital elements
- **Geographic example**: Commodity shipped to India, 4 months transit
- **Payment terms**: Assume selling time = 0 (made to order, paid on delivery)
- **Return time**: Money return requires another 4 months
- **Total effect**: 8 months before capital can function as productive capital again
- **Credit system basis**: These differences form material basis for various credit terms
- **Historical example**: Overseas commerce in Venice/Genoa as source of credit system

### Usance (Billing Terms)
- **1847 crisis effect**: Enabled banking/mercantile community to reduce India/China usance from 10 months' date to 6 months' sight
- **1866 situation**: 20 years later, further reduction needed
- **Calcutta-London voyage**: Sailing vessel via Cape averages under 90 days
- **Proposed terms**: Four months' sight = ~150 days currency; six months' sight = ~210 days currency
- **Merchant perspective**: Fair opportunity to realise merchandise proceeds within reasonable time
- **Indian produce**: Sold in London with 3 months' prompt; 5 months to realise after accounting for sales time and previous periods
- **Total period**: 10 months total (5 months realisation + 5 months previous) vs. 7 months bill life
- **1866 notice**: Five big London banks and Paris Comptoir d'Escompte announced from January 1, 1867, branches would only buy/sell bills at terms not exceeding 4 months' sight
- **Outcome**: Reduction miscarried and had to be abandoned
- **Technological change**: Suez Canal later revolutionized all these calculations

## Key Concepts
- **Circulation Time**: Time capital spends in circulation sphere
- **Selling Time**: Time from production completion to sale
- **Buying Time**: Time for reconverting money into productive capital
- **Market Distance**: Geographic separation between production and consumption
- **Transportation Time**: Time for physical movement of commodities
- **Usance**: Billing terms, time allowed for bill of exchange currency
- **Credit System**: System enabling deferred payment
- **Turnover Time**: Total time for capital circuit completion
- **Commodity-Capital**: Capital in commodity form during circulation
- **Money-Capital**: Capital in money form during circulation

## Key Takeaways
1. Circulation time (especially selling time) significantly affects overall turnover time
2. Geographic distance from market is permanent cause of circulation time differentiation
3. Transportation improvement reduces absolute circulation time but maintains relative differences
4. Increased transportation frequency allows more rapid capital turnover
5. Transportation development concentrates production and markets, accelerating capital turnover
6. Local consumption-dependent industries (breweries) develop most in population centres
7. World market expansion increases mass of commodities in transit and capital in circulation stage
8. Circulation time = selling time + buying time; both affected by geographic and market factors
9. Overseas commerce historically formed basis for credit system development
10. Usance (billing terms) reflects circulation time realities and affects credit availability
