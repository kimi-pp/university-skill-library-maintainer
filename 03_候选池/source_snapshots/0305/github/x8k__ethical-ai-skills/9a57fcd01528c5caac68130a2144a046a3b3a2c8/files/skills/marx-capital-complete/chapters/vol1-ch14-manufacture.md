# Chapter 14: Division of Labour and Manufacture

## Core Idea
Manufacture is the form of co-operation based on division of labour, prevalent from mid-16th to late 18th century. It arises either by combining independent crafts under one capitalist or by dividing a single craft into specialized operations, creating a social product rather than individual commodities.

## Frameworks Introduced

### Two Origins of Manufacture
- **Origin 1: Combining Independent Crafts**
  - Example: Carriage-making combines wheelwrights, harness-makers, tailors, etc. under one roof
  - Process: Independent artisans assembled in one workshop
  - Transformation: Gradually lose ability to perform full craft; specialize in partial processes
  - Result: "Splitting up of carriage-making into its various detail processes"

- **Origin 2: Dividing a Single Craft**
  - Example: Needle-making splits 20 operations among 20 workers
  - Process: Workers initially make entire commodity, then operations are redistributed
  - Advantage: Simultaneous performance of operations increases output
  - Result: Commodity becomes "social product of a union of artificers"

### Detail Labour vs. Individual Labour
- **Detail Labourer**: Worker performing one isolated operation continuously
- **Individual Artificer**: Worker performing all operations of a craft sequentially
- **Advantage**: Detail labour increases productivity through specialization
- **Disadvantage**: Worker loses comprehensive skill, becomes dependent on the system

### The Manufacturing Period (Mid-16th to Late 18th Century)
- **Historical context**: Transition from guild handicraft to capitalist manufacture
- **Technical basis**: Hand tools, human skill, division of labour
- **Social basis**: Concentration of workers in workshops under capitalist control
- **Preparation for**: Modern industry with machinery (covered in Ch15)

### Advantages of Division of Labour in Manufacture
- **Increased productivity**: Specialization reduces time lost in transitions
- **Skill development**: Continuous repetition improves speed and quality
- **Tool simplification**: Specialized tools for each operation
- **Collective worker**: The workshop functions as one combined mechanism

### Disadvantages for Workers
- **Skill degradation**: Workers lose ability to perform the full craft
- **Dependence**: Workers become appendages of the capitalist workshop
- **Monotony**: Repetitive, one-sided operations lead to physical and mental degradation
- **Exploitation**: Capital uses division of labour to intensify control and extract more surplus-value

## Key Concepts
- **Manufacture**: Co-operation based on division of labour; characteristic form of capitalist production in manufacturing period
- **Division of Labour**: Splitting of production process into specialized operations
- **Detail Labourer**: Worker specialized in one partial operation
- **Workshop**: Site of manufacture under capitalist control
- **Social Product**: Commodity produced collectively by many workers
- **Collective Worker**: The combined mechanism of all detail labourers in a workshop

## Key Takeaways
1. Manufacture arises from two paths: combining crafts or dividing a single craft
2. Division of labour increases productivity but degrades workers' skills
3. The manufacturing period (16th-18th century) prepares the ground for modern industry
4. Manufacture creates a system where workers are appendages of capital's mechanism
5. Division of labour is both a technical necessity and a means of capitalist control
