# Chapter 17: The Circulation of Surplus Value

## Core Idea

This chapter extends the turnover analysis to examine how differences in turnover periods generate differences in the capitalisation of surplus-value, accumulation patterns, and the annual quantity of surplus-value produced. Marx demonstrates that rapid turnover (Capital A) enables periodic revenue streams that self-finance business operations and individual consumption, while slow turnover (Capital B) requires constant advances from the capitalist's own funds. The chapter introduces the critical phenomenon of latent money-capital — surplus-value hoarded as money, representing capitalists' legal titles to future social production. A lengthy quotation from William Thompson critiques the fetishism of accumulated wealth versus society's productive powers.

---

## Frameworks Introduced

### I. Turnover and Capitalisation of Surplus-Value

#### The Two Scenarios Contrasted

**Capital A (Rapid Turnover)**
- **Periodical Revenue**: Generates regular income streams throughout the year
- **Self-Financing**: After initial turnover period, operations pay for themselves
- **Individual Consumption**: Funded from realized surplus-value, not advances
- **Productive Consumption**: Surplus-value directly converted into capital for repairs, maintenance, improvements

**Capital B (Slow Turnover)**
- **Delayed Realization**: Surplus-value produced but unrealized for extended periods
- **Advance Requirements**: Must fund individual consumption from original capital
- **Capital Constraint**: Repair and maintenance capital must be advanced upfront
- **Anticipated Consumption**: Surplus-value consumed before realization

#### Implications for Fixed Capital Maintenance
- **Capital A**: Repair capital emerges from business itself via surplus-value capitalisation
- **Capital B**: Repair capital must be part of originally advanced capital
- **Accounting Illusion**: Both appear as advanced capital in books, but sources differ fundamentally
- **Revelation**: "Not only the accumulated capital but also a portion of the originally advanced capital may simply be capitalised surplus-value"

### II. Credit and the Complication of Capital Relations

#### The Credit System Intervention
- **Initial Borrowing**: Capitalist A may borrow productive capital from banker C
- **Surplus-Value Origin**: Banker C's funds consist of surplus-value deposited by capitalists D, E, F
- **Agency Relationship**: A acts as agent capitalising surplus-value appropriated by D, E, F
- **Accumulation Appearance**: From A's perspective, no accumulated capital exists initially

#### The Development of Credit
- **Historical Parallel**: Credit system develops alongside capitalist production
- **Money-Capital Function**: Unemployed capital earns interest when lent to others
- **Capital Distinction**: Money-capital as distinct from productive capital
- **Social Function**: Another's productive capital while idle in owner's hands

### III. The Accumulation of Money (Latent Money-Capital)

#### Forms of Latent Money-Capital

**1. Hoard Form (Absolute Growth)**
- **Source**: Additional gold/silver from precious metal-producing countries
- **Mechanism**: Direct or indirect exchange with gold-producing nations
- **Result**: Absolute increase in hoarded money within a country

**2. Withdrawn Circulation (Relative Growth)**
- **Source**: Money withdrawn from domestic circulation
- **Mechanism**: Capitalists accumulate money in hoard form
- **Majority Case**: Most latent money-capital takes this form

**3. Token and Claim Forms**
- **Tokens of Value**: Non-commodity money representations
- **Legal Claims**: Documents conferring capitalist titles against third persons
- **Credit Money Excluded**: Analysis focuses on non-credit forms

#### The Nature of Latent Money-Capital
- **Capital in Spe**: Potential capital, not yet functioning as active capital
- **Legal Titles**: Represents reserved claims to future annual social production
- **Proportional Growth**: Increases with more frequent surplus-value realization and rising production scale
- **Money-Market Effect**: Thrown upon money-market and absorbed by extended production

### IV. William Thompson's Critique of Accumulation Fetishism

#### The Insignificance of Accumulated Wealth
- **Magnitude Comparison**: Real accumulated wealth insignificant compared to society's productive powers
- **Legislative Focus**: Attention misdirected toward "accumulated wealth that strikes the eye"
- **Nominal Wealth**: Most "accumulated wealth" consists of mere demands on future production
- **Institutions of Insecurity**: Expedients perpetuating class domination

#### The Proportions (Britain and Ireland Example)
- **Population**: 20 million
- **Annual Consumption**: £400 million (£20 per individual)
- **Accumulated Capital**: £1,200 million (3× annual labour)
- **Per Capita**: £60 capital each
- **Interest Support**: Capital stock interest could maintain population for 2 months
- **Full Capital Support**: Could maintain idleness for 3 years, then starvation

#### The Eternal Round
- **Production-Consumption Cycle**: Continuous, wave-like process
- **Handful of Accumulation**: Actual accumulation minuscule compared to annual production
- **Class Control**: Few seize accumulation as instrument to appropriate others' labour
- **Public Burdens**: One-third of annual products abstracted as unproductive consumption
- **Vulgar Illusion**: Accumulated masses in few hands strike the vulgar eye

#### The Paramount Importance
- **Annual Products**: Quantity and distribution should be primary consideration
- **Secondary Importance**: Actual accumulation derives importance from influencing distribution
- **Misplaced Priority**: Accumulation and distribution considered subordinate to preserving existing modes
- **Human Sacrifice**: "To perpetuate the results of force, fraud, and chance, has been called security"

### V. The Two Normal Cases of Reproduction

#### Apart from Disturbances
- **Simple Reproduction**: Surplus-value consumed individually (unproductively) by capitalist
- **Accumulation**: Surplus-value capitalised, converted into additional productive capital
- **Binary Choice**: Only two normal possibilities for surplus-value destination

#### Simple Reproduction
- **Definition**: Annual or periodic surplus-value consumed unproductively
- **Agent**: Individual capitalist as consumer
- **Value Composition**: Product value contains surplus-value portion for consumption

## Key Concepts

- **Capitalisation of Surplus-Value**: Conversion of surplus-value into additional capital
- **Latent Money-Capital**: Surplus-value hoarded as money, not yet functioning as active capital
- **Periodical Revenue**: Regular income streams from frequent turnover
- **Self-Financing Operations**: Business funding its own continuation from surplus-value
- **Repair and Maintenance Capital**: Portion of capital for fixed capital upkeep
- **Credit System**: Mechanism for employing idle capital at interest
- **Money-Capital**: Capital in money form, distinct from productive capital
- **Hoard**: Accumulated money withdrawn from circulation
- **Capital in Spe**: Capital in potential, not yet actual form
- **Legal Titles to Production**: Capitalists' claims on future social labour
- **Simple Reproduction**: Production on unchanged scale with consumption of surplus-value
- **Accumulation**: Reinvestment of surplus-value for expanded reproduction

---

## Key Takeaways

1. **Turnover Drives Capitalisation Patterns**: Different turnover periods create different patterns of surplus-value capitalisation and accumulation

2. **Self-Financing vs. Advance-Financing**: Rapid turnover enables self-financing of operations and consumption, while slow turnover requires constant capital advances

3. **Surplus-Value as Capital Source**: A portion of what appears as originally advanced capital may actually be capitalised surplus-value (especially for repairs and maintenance)

4. **Credit System Development**: Credit emerges alongside capitalist production, enabling employment of idle capital and creating complex capital relations

5. **Latent Money-Capital Forms**: Hoards, withdrawn circulation, tokens, and legal claims all represent potential capital awaiting employment

6. **Capital as Future Claims**: Latent money-capital represents reserved legal titles to future social production, not current productive wealth

7. **Accumulation vs. Productive Powers**: The mass of real accumulated wealth is insignificant compared to society's productive capabilities

8. **Nominal Wealth**: Most so-called accumulated wealth consists of claims on future production, not actual material wealth

9. **Class Domination Mechanism**: Accumulation serves as instrument for appropriating the annual products of the labouring majority

10. **Reproduction Binary**: Only two normal cases exist - simple reproduction (consumption) or accumulation (capitalisation) of surplus-value
