# Chapter 11: Rate and Mass of Surplus-Value

## Core Idea
While the rate of surplus-value (s/v) measures exploitation intensity, the mass of surplus-value (s × n) depends on both the rate and the number of workers employed. Capital increases total surplus-value by either raising the rate (absolute surplus-value) or employing more workers (extensive expansion).

## Frameworks Introduced

### Mass of Surplus-Value Formula
- **Formula**: Mass of s = s' × v × n (rate × variable capital per worker × number of workers)
- **Alternative**: Mass of s = s' × (v total) where v total = v × n
- **Key insight**: Total surplus-value = rate × total variable capital

### Two Methods to Increase Surplus-Value Mass
- **Increase Rate (s')**: Raise exploitation per worker (absolute or relative surplus-value)
- **Increase Workers (n)**: Employ more workers with existing rate
- **Combined**: Capital uses both methods simultaneously

### The Compensation Effect
- **Scenario**: Rate of surplus-value rises but total variable capital falls
- **Result**: Mass of surplus-value may stay constant or even fall
- **Example**: s' doubles but v halves → mass of s unchanged
- **Capital's dilemma**: Higher exploitation per worker may reduce total employment

### Variable Capital and Labour Productivity
- **Relation**: Higher productivity → more surplus-value per worker → potentially higher s'
- **But**: Higher productivity may reduce v (fewer workers needed) → offsets mass increase
- **Net effect**: Depends on balance between rate increase and employment reduction

## Key Concepts
- **Mass of Surplus-Value**: Total surplus-value produced by all workers
- **Rate of Surplus-Value (s')**: s/v per individual worker
- **Variable Capital (v)**: Capital advanced for labour-power
- **Number of Workers (n)**: Total labour-power employed
- **Absolute Increase**: More surplus-value from longer working day or more workers
- **Relative Increase**: More surplus-value from higher productivity

## Key Takeaways
1. Mass of surplus-value = rate × total variable capital
2. Capital increases total surplus-value by raising exploitation rate or employing more workers
3. Higher rate doesn't always mean more total surplus-value (compensation effect)
4. Productivity increases can both raise rate and reduce employment
5. Capital seeks maximum mass of surplus-value through both intensive and extensive methods
