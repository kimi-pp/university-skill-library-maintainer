# DTRI Infrastructure: Digital Connectivity & Access

## Overview

**Pillar 3: Digital Infrastructure** measures the physical and technological foundation that enables digital trade. This includes telecommunications networks, internet penetration, mobile connectivity, and the affordability of digital services.

Without robust digital infrastructure, even the most progressive digital trade policies cannot be effectively implemented.

## Detailed Indicator Breakdown

### 1. Internet Penetration

**Definition**: Percentage of individuals who have used the Internet from any location in the last 3 months.

**RDTII 2025 Regional Averages**:
| Subregion | Average | Range |
|-----------|---------|-------|
| East & North-East Asia | 89% | 73-98% |
| South-East Asia | 75% | 35-92% |
| South & South-West Asia | 55% | 27-82% |
| North & Central Asia | 78% | 70-88% |
| Pacific | 71% | 12-95% |
| Developed Economies | 92% | 88-98% |

**Key Considerations**:
- Urban-rural divide significant in many economies
- Age and education level impact usage rates
- COVID-19 accelerated digital adoption across region

### 2. Mobile Cellular Penetration

**Definition**: Number of active SIM cards per 100 inhabitants.

**Regional Leaders (>150%)**:
- Hong Kong, China: 280%
- Macao, China: 260%
- Singapore: 175%
- Maldives: 165%

**Challenges**:
- Multiple SIM ownership inflates penetration rates
- Active vs. inactive subscriptions not always distinguished
- Remote coverage gaps despite high national penetration

### 3. Fixed Broadband Subscriptions

**Definition**: Fixed broadband subscriptions per 100 inhabitants (wired connections).

**Performance Categories**:
| Category | Subscriptions/100 | Examples |
|----------|-------------------|----------|
| Very High | >40 | Singapore (52), ROK (48), HK (45) |
| High | 25-40 | Japan (35), Australia (32), NZ (28) |
| Medium | 15-25 | Malaysia (22), Thailand (18), Vietnam (17) |
| Low | 5-15 | Philippines (8), Indonesia (7), India (5) |
| Very Low | <5 | Most LDCs, Pacific Island states |

**Technology Mix**:
- **FTTH/FTTP**: Fiber to the home/premises (fastest growing)
- **Cable**: Hybrid fiber-coaxial (mature, declining)
- **xDSL**: Digital subscriber line (legacy, being phased out)

### 4. Mobile Broadband Subscriptions

**Definition**: Active mobile broadband subscriptions per 100 inhabitants (3G, 4G, 5G).

**Regional Leaders (>130%)**:
- Singapore: 175%
- Hong Kong, China: 160%
- Republic of Korea: 155%
- Brunei Darussalam: 140%

**Growth Trends**:
- Mobile broadband is primary access in developing economies
- 4G/LTE now dominant; 5G rollout accelerating
- Smartphone affordability drives adoption

### 5. Internet Affordability

**Definition**: Cost of fixed broadband (1GB/month at minimum speed) as percentage of GNI per capita.

**Affordability Thresholds (ITU Target)**:
| Affordability Level | Cost (% of GNI per capita) |
|---------------------|---------------------------|
| Affordable | <2% |
| Moderately Affordable | 2-5% |
| Expensive | 5-10% |
| Very Expensive | >10% |

**Regional Performance**:
| Category | Economies |
|----------|-----------|
| Affordable (<2%) | Singapore, HK, ROK, Japan, Australia, NZ, Brunei |
| Moderately Affordable (2-5%) | Malaysia, Thailand, Vietnam, China, Georgia |
| Expensive (5-10%) | Philippines, Indonesia, India, Pakistan, Pacific Islands |
| Very Expensive (>10%) | Most LDCs, Kiribati, Solomon Islands, PNG |

**Policy Levers**:
- Spectrum pricing and allocation
- Infrastructure sharing requirements
- Competition policy
- Universal service funds
- Subsidy programs for underserved areas

### 6. 5G Coverage

**Definition**: Percentage of population with access to 5G networks.

**Deployment Status (2025)**:
| Status | Economies |
|--------|-----------|
| Nationwide (>80%) | ROK, Singapore, HK, Japan, Australia, NZ, China, UAE |
| Major Cities (30-80%) | Malaysia, Thailand, Vietnam, Philippines, India, Kazakhstan |
| Pilot/Limited (<30%) | Indonesia, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, Pakistan, Pacific Islands |
| Not Deployed | Most LDCs, some Pacific Island states |

**5G Use Cases for Digital Trade**:
- Enhanced mobile broadband for e-commerce
- Ultra-reliable low-latency for real-time transactions
- Massive IoT for supply chain tracking
- Network slicing for enterprise services

### 7. International Bandwidth

**Definition**: International Internet bandwidth per Internet user (bits/second/user).

**Capacity Tiers**:
| Tier | Bandwidth/ user | Examples |
|------|-----------------|----------|
| Very High | >100,000 | Singapore, ROK, HK, Japan |
| High | 50,000-100,000 | Australia, NZ, Malaysia, Thailand |
| Medium | 20,000-50,000 | China, Vietnam, Philippines, India |
| Low | 5,000-20,000 | Indonesia, Pakistan, Pacific Islands |
| Very Low | <5,000 | Most LDCs, Landlocked Central Asia |

**Key Submarine Cables in Asia-Pacific**:
| Cable | Landing Points | Capacity |
|-------|----------------|----------|
| SEA-ME-WE 5 | Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, India | 3.8 Tbps |
| Asia Pacific Gateway | Japan, Korea, Taiwan, Philippines, Malaysia | 60+ Tbps |
| Bifrost | Singapore, Indonesia, Brunei, Philippines | 190 Tbps |
| Echo | Singapore, Indonesia, Timor-Leste | 13 Tbps |

## Regional Infrastructure Initiatives

### ASEAN

**ASEAN ICT Masterplan 2025**:
- Goal: Seamless and integrated ASEAN digital economy
- Key initiatives: ASEAN Broadband Corridor, One ASEAN One Network
- Progress: Interconnectivity projects progressing, cross-border roaming reduced

### SAARC

**SAARC Regional ICT Framework**:
- Focus: Regional connectivity through optical fiber networks
- Challenge: Political tensions slow implementation
- Status: Limited progress on cross-border infrastructure

### PIFS

**Pacific Regional ICT Initiative**:
- Focus: Submarine cable connectivity to Pacific Islands
- Key projects: Southern Cross NEXT, Hawaiki, MANA cable
- Challenge: High cost, small markets, vulnerability to natural disasters

### ESCAP

**Asia-Pacific Information Superhighway (AP-IS)**:
- Goal: Enhanced regional connectivity through inland and submarine networks
- Progress: 26 countries connected, 8 more planned
- Components: Hard infrastructure, soft infrastructure, capacity building

## Infrastructure Challenges by Economy Type

### Landlocked Developing Countries (LLDCs)

**Challenges**:
- Dependence on transit countries for international connectivity
- Higher costs due to multiple border crossings
- Limited competition in domestic markets

**Examples**: Mongolia, Nepal, Bhutan, Central Asian states

**Solutions**:
- Regional infrastructure cooperation
- Diversified transit routes
- Satellite connectivity as backup

### Small Island Developing States (SIDS)

**Challenges**:
- High per-capita infrastructure costs
- Vulnerability to natural disasters
- Limited economies of scale

**Examples**: Pacific Island states, Maldives

**Solutions**:
- Submarine cable pooling
- Satellite connectivity
- Regional infrastructure sharing

### Least Developed Countries (LDCs)

**Challenges**:
- Limited domestic investment capacity
- Weak regulatory frameworks
- Low demand from low-income populations

**Examples**: Afghanistan, Bangladesh, Cambodia, Laos, Myanmar, Nepal

**Solutions**:
- Development finance
- Universal service funds
- Competition policy reforms

## Policy Recommendations

### For Low-Scoring Economies (0-40)

1. **Immediate Actions**:
   - Establish universal service fund
   - Liberalize telecommunications sector
   - Reduce spectrum fees
   - Develop national broadband plan

2. **Medium-term (1-3 years)**:
   - Deploy 4G/LTE to unserved areas
   - Implement infrastructure sharing
   - Introduce competition in mobile sector
   - Reduce roaming charges regionally

3. **Long-term (3-5+ years)**:
   - Connect to regional submarine cables
   - Begin 5G planning and spectrum allocation
   - Develop fiber backbone networks
   - Establish regional connectivity partnerships

### For Medium-Scoring Economies (41-60)

1. **Improve Affordability**:
   - Reduce taxes on devices and services
   - Promote low-cost data plans
   - Support device financing programs

2. **Expand Coverage**:
   - Focus on rural connectivity
   - Use shared infrastructure models
   - Explore alternative technologies (satellite, TV white space)

3. **Enhance Quality**:
   - Upgrade network speeds
   - Improve service reliability
   - Implement quality of service standards

### For High-Scoring Economies (61+)

1. **Lead Regional Initiatives**:
   - Share best practices
   - Support capacity building
   - Contribute to regional infrastructure projects

2. **Advance Technologies**:
   - Accelerate 5G deployment
   - Explore 6G research
   - Develop edge computing infrastructure

3. **Digital Inclusion**:
   - Address digital literacy gaps
   - Support accessibility for persons with disabilities
   - Ensure equitable access across demographic groups

## Analysis Framework

### Infrastructure Gap Analysis

When assessing an economy's digital infrastructure:

```
Step 1: Baseline Assessment
  └─ Collect all indicator scores
  └─ Compare with regional averages
  └─ Identify top and bottom performers

Step 2: Root Cause Analysis
  └─ Geography (landlocked, island, remote)
  └─ Economic constraints (GDP per capita, investment)
  └─ Policy environment (competition, regulation)
  └─ Market structure (monopoly, oligopoly, competitive)

Step 3: Impact Assessment
  └─ How gaps affect digital trade
  └─ Which economic sectors most affected
  └─ Potential economic cost of inaction

Step 4: Solution Mapping
  └─ Short-term quick wins
  └─ Medium-term investments
  └─ Long-term structural changes
  └─ International cooperation opportunities
```

### Cost-Benefit Analysis Template

For infrastructure investment decisions:

| Factor | Description | Measurement |
|--------|-------------|-------------|
| Investment Cost | CAPEX for infrastructure | USD per capita |
| Operating Cost | OPEX for maintenance | USD per subscriber/month |
| Revenue Potential | Expected subscription revenue | ARPU x penetration |
| Economic Spillovers | GDP impact per $1 investment | Multiplier effect |
| Social Benefits | Education, health, governance | Qualitative + metrics |
