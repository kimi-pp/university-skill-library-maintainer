# DTRI Business Framework: E-commerce, Competition & Payments

## Overview

These three pillars create the enabling environment for businesses to participate in digital trade:

- **Pillar 8: E-commerce Regulations** - Legal frameworks governing online business
- **Pillar 9: Competition Policy** - Rules ensuring fair digital markets
- **Pillar 10: Digital Payments** - Payment infrastructure and regulation

Together, these pillars determine how easily and effectively businesses can operate in the digital economy.

---

## Pillar 8: E-commerce Regulations

### What It Measures

The legal and regulatory framework that governs e-commerce activities, including business registration, electronic transactions, and cross-border trade facilitation.

### Key Indicators

| Indicator | Description | Weight |
|-----------|-------------|--------|
| E-commerce Law | Existence of comprehensive e-commerce legislation | 30% |
| Electronic Transactions | Legal recognition of electronic contracts and signatures | 25% |
| Business Registration | Online business registration capabilities | 20% |
| Cross-border E-commerce | Customs and border procedures for online trade | 15% |
| Platform Regulations | Rules governing online marketplaces | 10% |

### E-commerce Legal Frameworks

| Economy | E-commerce Law | Electronic Transactions | Online Registration |
|---------|----------------|------------------------|---------------------|
| Singapore | ECTA 1998 | ETA 2010 | ACRA BizFile+ |
| Republic of Korea | E-commerce Act | Electronic Transactions Act | Online Biz Registration |
| Japan | E-commerce Guidelines | E-Signature Act | J-GoodTech Registration |
| China | E-commerce Law 2019 | E-Signature Law 2005 | National Enterprise Credit |
| India | IT Act 2000 (amended) | IT Act provisions | SPICe+ registration |
| Indonesia | E-commerce Regulation 2020 | ITE Law 2008 | OSS (Online Single Submission) |
| Thailand | E-commerce Protection Act | E-Transaction Act | DBD Online Registration |
| Vietnam | E-commerce Law 2013 | E-Transaction Law 2005 | National Business Registration |
| Philippines | E-Commerce Act 2000 | E-Commerce Act provisions | DTI Online Registration |
| Malaysia | E-commerce Guidelines | Digital Signature Act 1997 | SSM Online Registration |

### E-commerce Law Components

#### 1. Electronic Contracts

**Legal Recognition Requirements**:
- Offer and acceptance can be electronic
- Electronic signatures have same legal effect as handwritten
- Contract formation rules apply to electronic transactions
- Time and place of contract formation clarified

**Best Practice Economies**: Singapore, Japan, ROK, Australia, NZ

#### 2. Electronic Signatures

| Signature Type | Legal Status | Use Cases |
|----------------|--------------|-----------|
| Advanced E-Signature | Full legal recognition | High-value transactions |
| Qualified E-Signature | Highest trust level | Government services, finance |
| Simple E-Signature | Limited recognition | Low-value, low-risk transactions |
| Biometric Signature | Varies by jurisdiction | Banking, authentication |

**E-Signature Law Status**:
- **Comprehensive**: Singapore, Japan, ROK, India, China, EU-aligned economies
- **Partial**: Thailand, Vietnam, Indonesia, Philippines
- **Limited**: Some LDCs, Pacific Island states

#### 3. Online Business Registration

**Registration Process Maturity**:

| Maturity Level | Characteristics | Examples |
|----------------|-----------------|----------|
| Fully Digital | Complete online process, digital certificates | Singapore, ROK, NZ, UAE |
| Hybrid | Online application, in-person verification | Japan, Malaysia, Thailand |
| Limited Online | Some steps online, many offline | Indonesia, Vietnam, Philippines |
| Manual | Entirely offline process | Many LDCs, some Pacific states |

#### 4. Cross-border E-commerce

**Customs Facilitation Measures**:

| Measure | Description | Adoption |
|---------|-------------|----------|
| De Minimis Threshold | Duty-free limit for low-value shipments | Widely adopted |
| Single Window | One-stop customs clearance | Growing adoption |
| Trusted Trader Programs | Expedited clearance for certified traders | APEC, ASEAN initiatives |
| Digital Documentation | Electronic certificates, permits | Varying adoption |

**De Minimis Thresholds (2025)**:

| Economy | Threshold (USD) | Notes |
|---------|-----------------|-------|
| Singapore | No minimum | Fully liberalized |
| Australia | 1,000 | GST collected on all imports |
| New Zealand | 1,000 | GST registration for offshore sellers |
| China | 0 | All imports subject to duties/taxes |
| Japan | ~150 | Consumption tax on imports |
| ROK | ~150 | VAT on imports |
| Thailand | ~150 | Duties apply above threshold |
| Indonesia | 75 | Duties and taxes apply |
| Vietnam | ~150 | Low-value imports taxed |
| Philippines | ~200 | Partial de minimis |
| Malaysia | 500 | Above threshold taxed |

#### 5. Platform Regulations

**Regulatory Areas**:
- **Platform Liability**: Responsibility for third-party seller conduct
- **Data Portability**: User ability to move data between platforms
- **Interoperability**: Requirements for platform compatibility
- **Fair Competition**: Prevention of self-preferencing
- **Transparency**: Disclosure of algorithms, ranking criteria

**Regulatory Approaches**:

| Approach | Description | Adopters |
|----------|-------------|----------|
| Ex Ante Regulation | Preventive rules before harm occurs | EU, emerging in region |
| Ex Post Enforcement | Reactive enforcement after complaints | Most economies |
| Self-Regulation | Industry codes of conduct | Some economies |
| Sector-Specific | Different rules by sector | Financial services, health |

### Scoring Criteria

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 81-100 | Comprehensive e-commerce law, full online registration, efficient cross-border procedures |
| 61-80 | E-commerce law exists, partial online registration, some trade facilitation |
| 41-60 | Basic e-commerce regulations, limited online services, manual cross-border processes |
| 21-40 | Fragmented regulations, minimal online capabilities, no cross-border facilitation |
| 0-20 | No e-commerce legal framework |

### E-commerce Implementation Challenges

1. **MSME Digitalization** - Small businesses lack capacity and resources
2. **Cross-border Logistics** - Fragmented rules increase costs
3. **Consumer Trust** - Concerns about fraud and product quality
4. **Taxation** - Complex rules for cross-border sales
5. **Platform Dominance** - Market concentration concerns

## Pillar 9: Competition Policy

### What It Measures

The effectiveness of competition policy and enforcement in ensuring fair and competitive digital markets.

### Key Indicators

| Indicator | Description | Weight |
|-----------|-------------|--------|
| Competition Law | Existence and comprehensiveness of competition legislation | 30% |
| Competition Authority | Independence and capacity of enforcement body | 25% |
| Digital Competition Rules | Specific rules for digital markets | 20% |
| Enforcement Record | History of antitrust actions in digital sector | 15% |
| Merger Control | Review of digital economy mergers | 10% |

### Competition Law Status

| Economy | Competition Law | Competition Authority | Digital-Specific Rules |
|---------|----------------|----------------------|----------------------|
| Singapore | Competition Act | CCS | No specific digital rules |
| Republic of Korea | Monopoly Regulation | KFTC | Platform regulations |
| Japan | Antimonopoly Act | JFTC | Platform guidelines |
| China | Anti-Monopoly Law | SAMR | Platform economy rules |
| India | Competition Act | CCI | Digital market review |
| Indonesia | Competition Law | KPPU | Limited digital rules |
| Thailand | Trade Competition Act | TCCP | Draft platform rules |
| Vietnam | Competition Law | VCCA | Limited digital rules |
| Philippines | Competition Act | PCC | Emerging digital focus |
| Malaysia | Competition Act | MyCC | Limited digital rules |

### Digital Competition Issues

#### 1. Platform Dominance

**Concerns**:
- **Self-preferencing**: Platforms favoring their own products
- **Tying and Bundling**: Forced use of platform services
- **Exclusive Dealing**: Restricting multi-homing
- **Access Restrictions**: Denying access to essential facilities
- **Data Advantage**: Using data to entrench position

**Regulatory Responses**:

| Economy | Regulatory Action | Status |
|---------|-------------------|--------|
| ROK | Fair Trade Act amendments | Enforced |
| China | Platform economy guidelines | Enforced |
| Japan | Platform guidelines | Voluntary compliance |
| Singapore | Market studies and consultations | Monitoring |
| EU | Digital Markets Act | Regional model |

#### 2. Data-Driven Competition

**Issues**:
- Data as barrier to entry
- Data aggregation advantages
- Network effects
- Winner-takes-all dynamics

**Policy Approaches**:
- Data portability requirements
- Interoperability mandates
- Data access regimes
- Fair data sharing rules

#### 3. Merger Control

**Digital Mergers of Concern**:
- Killer acquisitions (acquiring potential competitors)
- Data concentration
- Ecosystem expansion
- Market foreclosure

**Merger Review Trends**:
- Earlier intervention (pre-emptive)
- Focus on data assets
- Consideration of non-price effects
- International cooperation

### Competition Authority Effectiveness

| Effectiveness Level | Characteristics |
|---------------------|-----------------|
| High | Independent, well-resourced, track record of enforcement |
| Medium | Reasonable independence, some digital enforcement |
| Low | Limited independence, minimal digital focus |
| Very Low | No dedicated authority or no enforcement |

### Scoring Criteria

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 81-100 | Strong competition law, independent authority, digital-specific rules, active enforcement |
| 61-80 | Competition law exists, authority operational, some digital enforcement |
| 41-60 | Basic competition law, limited authority capacity, minimal digital focus |
| 21-40 | Draft or limited competition law, weak authority |
| 0-20 | No competition law |

### Competition Policy Recommendations

1. **Develop Digital Competition Framework**
   - Platform-specific rules
   - Data access and portability
   - Merger review guidelines for digital deals

2. **Strengthen Enforcement Capacity**
   - Digital expertise in competition authority
   - Market monitoring tools
   - International cooperation

3. **Address New Competition Issues**
   - Algorithmic collusion
   - Self-preferencing
   - Essential facilities in digital markets

| Payment Infrastructure | POS, QR code, mobile money availability | 25% |

| Interoperability | Cross-platform and cross-border payment capabilities | 15% |
| Financial Inclusion | Access to digital payments by underserved populations | 15% |

|------------|--------------|------------|--------|
| mBridge | China, HK, Thailand, UAE | CBDC-based | Pilot |
| Project Nexus | Singapore, Malaysia, Philippines | Linking fast payment systems | Pilot |
| AML/CTF Standards | APEC economies | Harmonized rules | Implemented |
| ISO 20022 | Global | Standard messaging | Migration ongoing |
| QR Cross-border | ASEAN | QRIS linkage | Partial |

### Interoperability Frameworks

| Framework | Scope | Key Features |
|-----------|-------|--------------|
| PromptPay (Thailand) | Domestic | Universal QR, bank linkage |
| QRIS (Indonesia) | Domestic | Single QR, all providers |
| DuitNow (Malaysia) | Domestic | Universal ID, bank linkage |
| UPI (India) | Domestic + Cross-border | Real-time, bank-linked |
| FAST (Singapore) | Domestic | Real-time, 24/7 |
| FPS (Hong Kong) | Domestic | Real-time, QR code |

### Scoring Criteria

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 81-100 | High digital payment usage, comprehensive regulation, interoperable systems |
| 61-80 | Good penetration, regulatory framework, some interoperability |
| 41-60 | Moderate usage, basic regulation, limited interoperability |
| 21-40 | Low penetration, minimal regulation, no interoperability |
| 0-20 | Very limited digital payments, no framework |

### Digital Payment Barriers

1. **Regulatory Fragmentation** - Different rules across jurisdictions
2. **Interoperability Gaps** - Systems don't work together
3. **Trust Issues** - Security and fraud concerns
4. **Cost** - Transaction fees, especially cross-border
5. **Access Gaps** - Underserved populations lack access

### Policy Recommendations

1. **Develop Comprehensive Payment Framework**
   - Modern payment services regulation
   - Sandbox for innovation
   - Risk-based supervision

2. **Promote Interoperability**
   - Common standards (ISO 20022, QR codes)
   - Open banking initiatives
   - API standards

3. **Support Financial Inclusion**
   - Mobile money regulation
   - Agent banking networks
   - Digital literacy programs

4. **Enable Cross-border Payments**
   - Join regional payment initiatives
   - Harmonize AML/CFT rules
   - Reduce transaction costs

## Inter-pillar Connections

### E-commerce Regulations ↔ Competition Policy

- Platform regulations address market power
- Competition rules apply to e-commerce platforms
- Both aim for fair digital markets

### E-commerce Regulations ↔ Digital Payments

- E-commerce requires payment infrastructure
- Payment regulations affect e-commerce costs
- Consumer protection spans both areas

### Competition Policy ↔ Digital Payments

- Competition issues in payment markets
- Interoperability reduces market power
- Open banking promotes competition
