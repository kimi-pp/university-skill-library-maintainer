# process-letter-drafter

Draft professional process letters for sell-side M&A transactions. Process letters are the formal communications sent to prospective buyers at each stage of an auction — they set the rules of engagement, submission requirements, valuation expectations, and legal framework. A well-drafted process letter maximizes competitive tension, protects the seller's position, and drives deal certainty. This skill handles initial contact letters, first-round bid instructions, second-round process letters, and best-and-final instructions.

## Inputs

### Required
- **Deal name / project code:** Company or code name (required)
- **Process stage:** Initial outreach / Phase I bid instructions / Phase II process letter / Best-and-final (required)
- **Seller's counsel:** Law firm name (required for Phase II+)

### Optional
- **Target company profile:** Brief description, industry, financial summary
- **Timeline:** Key dates (bid deadline, management presentations, data room access, target signing date)
- **Valuation guidance:** Whether to provide guidance or let buyers determine value
- **Number of bidders:** Current buyer count for competitive tension calibration
- **Structure preferences:** Asset vs. stock, cash vs. stock, earnout tolerance
- **Key deal terms:** R&W insurance expectation, escrow, indemnification framework
- **Regulatory considerations:** HSR/CFIUS/industry-specific approvals required
- **Confidentiality level:** NDA already signed / NDA enclosed / additional confidentiality provisions
- **Seller advisors:** Investment bank, legal counsel, accounting advisors
- **Management meeting availability:** Dates/format for management presentations

## Methodology

### Step 1: Determine Process Stage and Letter Type

**Letter Types by Stage:**

| Stage | Letter Type | Purpose | Typical Length |
|-------|-------------|---------|---------------|
| Pre-process | Teaser / Blind Profile | Generate interest without identifying the target | 1-2 pages |
| Phase I | Initial Process Letter | NDA delivery, CIM access, first-round bid instructions | 3-5 pages |
| Phase II | Second-Round Process Letter | Detailed bid instructions, markup requirements, diligence access | 5-8 pages |
| Final Round | Best-and-Final Instructions | Final bid submission requirements, definitive agreement markup | 3-5 pages |
| Post-Selection | Exclusivity Letter | Exclusivity terms with selected bidder | 2-3 pages |

### Step 2: Draft Core Sections

**A. Opening & Context (All Letters)**
- Reference to confidentiality agreement
- Transaction overview (without revealing seller's motivation unless strategic)
- Advisor identification
- Purpose of the letter

**B. Information Access (Phase I)**
- CIM distribution (physical/electronic)
- Data room access instructions
- Additional information request process
- Restrictions on use of information
- Contact protocol (all questions through the bank, never directly to management)

**C. Bid Instructions (Phase I & II)**

**Phase I — Indication of Interest Requirements:**
1. Proposed enterprise value or range
2. Form of consideration (cash/stock/mixed)
3. Sources and certainty of financing
4. Key assumptions underlying the valuation
5. Due diligence requirements and timeline
6. Proposed transaction structure (asset vs. stock)
7. Key conditions to closing
8. Overview of buyer's organization and strategic rationale
9. Relevant transaction experience and references
10. Contact information for lead negotiator

**Phase II — Binding Offer Requirements:**
1. Definitive purchase price (no ranges — a single number)
2. Marked-up purchase agreement (against seller's draft)
3. Committed financing letters (for financial buyers)
4. Board/committee approvals obtained or expected timeline
5. Detailed integration plan (for strategic buyers)
6. Management retention/equity proposals
7. Regulatory analysis (HSR, CFIUS, industry-specific)
8. Remaining due diligence items (must be specific and limited)
9. Proposed timeline from signing to closing
10. Markup of ancillary agreements (employment agreements, non-competes, TSA)

**D. Timeline & Process Rules**

Standard process timeline template:
```
Phase I:
- CIM Distribution: [Date]
- Management Presentations: [Date Range]
- Phase I Bid Deadline: [Date, Time, Timezone]
- Notification of Phase II Advancement: [Date]

Phase II:
- Data Room Access: [Date]
- Draft Purchase Agreement Distribution: [Date]
- Site Visits / Expert Sessions: [Date Range]
- Phase II Bid Deadline: [Date, Time, Timezone]
- Buyer Selection & Exclusivity: [Date]

Signing to Close:
- Target Signing: [Date]
- Regulatory Filings: [Within X days of signing]
- Target Close: [Date]
```

**E. Legal Framework & Submission Terms**

Key legal provisions to include:
- **No obligation to sell:** Seller reserves the right to terminate the process at any time
- **No obligation to accept highest bid:** Seller may consider factors beyond price
- **Modification of process:** Seller reserves the right to modify timeline, procedures, or bid requirements
- **No binding obligation from IOI:** First-round indications are non-binding
- **Expense waiver:** Each party bears its own expenses
- **Return of materials:** All confidential materials must be returned or destroyed upon request
- **Non-solicitation of employees:** Buyers may not contact target employees without prior consent
- **No teaming:** Buyers may not form consortia without seller's prior written consent
- **Governing law:** Specified jurisdiction

**F. Submission Mechanics**
- Delivery method (email/hard copy/both)
- Number of copies
- Recipient names and addresses
- File format requirements
- Deadline (date, time, timezone — be precise)
- Late submissions policy (typically not accepted)

### Step 3: Calibrate Competitive Tension Language

**High competition (5+ active bidders):**
- Emphasize competitive nature of the process
- Strict deadlines with no extensions
- "The Company has received significant interest..."
- Require committed financing, no financing contingencies

**Moderate competition (3-4 bidders):**
- Professional tone, standard process language
- Reasonable but firm deadlines
- Focus on certainty of close and execution capability

**Limited competition (1-2 bidders):**
- Avoid language suggesting broad auction
- Focus on timeline and mutual value creation
- More flexibility on process terms
- Consider bilateral negotiation approach

### Step 4: Address Buyer-Type-Specific Considerations

**For Strategic Buyers:**
- Request synergy analysis and integration plan
- Address antitrust/regulatory considerations
- Employee retention commitments
- Technology integration approach

**For Financial Sponsors (PE):**
- Committed financing letters (not "highly confident" letters — committed)
- Management equity/rollover terms
- Fund-level authorization confirmation
- Co-invest or club deal restrictions
- Value creation plan

**For Management Buyouts:**
- Conflict of interest acknowledgment
- Independent board committee process
- Fairness opinion requirement
- Equal information access provisions

### Step 5: Industry-Specific Requirements

Certain industries require specialized diligence, regulatory, and disclosure provisions in the process letter. Include the relevant module(s) when the target operates in these sectors.

**CHEMICALS / INDUSTRIAL:**
Process letter must address:
- **Phase I Environmental Site Assessment (ESA):** Require buyers to commission or acknowledge seller-provided Phase I ESA for all owned/leased properties. Include timeline for Phase I completion (typically 4-6 weeks).
- **Phase II ESA (if triggered):** If Phase I identifies recognized environmental conditions (RECs), Phase II (subsurface investigation) may be required. Process letter should address: who bears cost, timeline extension, and whether Phase II findings can re-trade price.
- **RCRA Compliance:** Confirm target's Resource Conservation and Recovery Act compliance status (hazardous waste generator classification, permit status, corrective action requirements). Include in data room.
- **CERCLA / Superfund Exposure:** Disclose any Superfund site involvement (Potentially Responsible Party status, contribution agreements, remediation obligations, insurance coverage). Buyer will price this — better to disclose early.
- **Environmental Insurance:** Signal whether seller has environmental liability insurance (pollution legal liability) and whether policy is transferable to buyer.
- **Permit Transferability:** Address whether environmental and operating permits (air, water, waste) transfer automatically on change of control or require re-application.

**HEALTHCARE:**
- **Regulatory Licenses & Certifications:** Identify all licenses requiring regulatory approval for transfer (state health department, CMS certification, DEA registration, state pharmacy boards). Include expected timeline for change-of-control approvals.
- **Payor Contracts:** Address major payor contract assignment provisions. Medicare/Medicaid provider agreements have specific change-of-control requirements. Commercial payor contracts may have assignment restrictions.
- **Compliance History:** Disclose any compliance investigations (OIG, DOJ, state AG), corporate integrity agreements (CIAs), or False Claims Act history. Include in data room.
- **Stark Law / Anti-Kickback:** Note any physician relationships or referral arrangements requiring legal review under Stark Law and Anti-Kickback Statute.

**FINANCIAL SERVICES:**
- **Regulatory Approvals:** Identify all required regulatory approvals for change of control (OCC, Federal Reserve, state banking departments, SEC, FINRA, state insurance departments). Include expected timeline (often 90-180 days).
- **Change of Control Provisions:** Many financial services licenses have specific change-of-control notification or approval requirements. Address in timeline.
- **Capital Requirements:** Note any minimum capital or net worth requirements that buyer must satisfy post-close.
- **Customer Notification:** Some financial services regulations require customer notification of change of control. Address timing and methodology.

**DEFENSE / AEROSPACE:**
- **CFIUS Review:** If buyer is foreign-owned or controlled, the process letter MUST address CFIUS (Committee on Foreign Investment in the United States) review requirements. Include: voluntary vs. mandatory filing, timeline (45-day review + 45-day investigation), and mitigation agreement possibility.
- **ITAR Compliance:** If target handles ITAR-controlled technical data, articles, or services, foreign buyers face significant restrictions. Process letter should address export control implications and any required State Department approvals.
- **Facility Clearances:** Address whether target holds facility security clearances (FCL) and implications for buyer eligibility. Cleared facility transfers require Defense Counterintelligence and Security Agency (DCSA) approval.
- **FOCI Mitigation:** Foreign Ownership, Control, or Influence (FOCI) mitigation requirements (SSA, SCA, proxy board, VTA) must be addressed if buyer triggers FOCI concerns.

### Step 5b: R&W Insurance Signaling Language

Including R&W insurance language in the process letter signals a sophisticated, market-aware seller and can accelerate deal certainty by reducing indemnification negotiation friction.

**Standard R&W Insurance Language (include in Phase II process letter bid requirements):**

> "Bidders should assume that the Seller expects the Buyer to obtain a representations and warranties insurance policy (the 'R&W Policy') as the primary source of recovery for breaches of the Seller's representations and warranties in the definitive purchase agreement. The Seller will cooperate with the Buyer's R&W insurance underwriting process, including by participating in underwriting calls and providing access to diligence materials. Bidders should identify their preferred R&W insurance broker and confirm their intention to obtain a R&W Policy in their Phase II submission."

**When to include R&W insurance language:**
- Deal size > $25M (R&W insurance standard for middle-market+ deals)
- Multiple financial sponsor bidders (sponsors universally use R&W insurance)
- Seller wants clean exit (minimal post-close indemnification exposure)
- Private equity seller requiring "clean" exit for fund distributions

**When to omit or modify:**
- Deal size < $15M (R&W insurance may not be cost-effective)
- Distressed sale (underwriting may be difficult)
- Asset deal with limited reps (less insurable)

**R&W Insurance Impact on Process Letter Terms:**
- Indemnification cap can be reduced to $1 (or a nominal amount) for non-fundamental reps
- Escrow amount can be minimized (since R&W policy is the recourse mechanism)
- Survival period for reps can be shortened (R&W policy controls the recovery window)
- Net effect: seller retains more proceeds at close with less contingent liability

### Step 5c: Teaser Memo Cross-Reference

For initial outreach (pre-Phase I), a teaser memo (also called a "blind profile" or "teaser") typically accompanies or precedes the process letter. The teaser generates buyer interest without revealing the target's identity.

**When to include teaser memo reference:**
- Initial outreach to prospective buyers who have NOT signed an NDA
- Process letters accompanied by CIM distribution should reference the teaser as the prior step
- Cross-reference: "Following your receipt of the attached teaser memorandum and upon execution of the enclosed Confidentiality Agreement, you will receive access to the Confidential Information Memorandum."

**Teaser → Process Letter → CIM Flow:**
```
Step 1: Teaser memo sent (no NDA required, anonymous)
   → Buyer expresses interest
Step 2: NDA executed
Step 3: Phase I process letter + CIM sent (target identified)
   → Buyer submits IOI
Step 4: Phase II process letter + data room access
   → Buyer submits binding offer
```

**Process letter should note:**
- Whether a teaser has already been distributed (avoid redundant company descriptions)
- That all information in the teaser is superseded by the CIM
- Contact protocol for buyers who received the teaser but have not yet signed the NDA

### Step 6: Review & Compliance

Before finalizing:
- Cross-reference all dates for consistency
- Verify NDA terms are compatible with process letter provisions
- Check that submission requirements are achievable within the timeline
- Ensure regulatory considerations are properly scoped
- Review for inadvertent commitments or representations
- Confirm seller's counsel has reviewed legal provisions

## Quality Gates

- [ ] Correct process stage identified with appropriate level of detail
- [ ] All dates internally consistent (bid deadline after data room access, etc.)
- [ ] Bid requirements are specific and complete (not vague "provide your best offer")
- [ ] Legal provisions protect seller's flexibility (no obligation to sell, right to modify)
- [ ] Contact protocol restricts buyer-to-management direct communication
- [ ] Financing requirements appropriate for buyer type (committed letters for sponsors)
- [ ] No teaming / consortium restrictions included where appropriate
- [ ] Submission mechanics precise (date, time, timezone, format, recipient)
- [ ] Competitive tension language calibrated to actual competitive dynamics
- [ ] NDA cross-referenced and compatible with process letter terms
- [ ] Internal annotations provided for deal team context
- [ ] Letter is professional, concise, and actionable

## Professional Standards

**What separates A from B:**
- **A-grade:** Process letter calibrated to competitive dynamics. Bid requirements tailored to buyer type. Legal provisions comprehensive but not over-lawyered. Internal annotations explain strategic reasoning. Timeline accounts for regulatory considerations.
- **B-grade:** Generic template with dates filled in. Same letter sent to strategics and sponsors. Missing key legal protections. No internal annotations. Unrealistic timeline.

**Common pitfalls:**
- Setting bid deadlines too close to data room opening (buyers need time to diligence)
- Requiring committed financing letters in Phase I (too early — "highly confident" is appropriate for Phase I)
- Overly aggressive no-teaming provisions that discourage legitimate co-invest interest
- Failing to restrict direct buyer-to-management contact (buyers will try to go around the bank)
- Including valuation guidance too early (let the market tell you what it's worth first)
- Not customizing for buyer type — a strategic buyer's submission requirements differ from a sponsor's
