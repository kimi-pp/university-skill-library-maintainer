# Webnovel Planning Framework

## Overview

This skill generates a complete planning system for medium-length webnovels (targeting ~1M words). It produces:

1. **Three-Level Outline Structure**
   - Act-level story structure (3–5 major movements)
   - Arc-level breakdown (story arcs within each act)
   - Chapter-level summaries (key events, pacing, cliffhangers)

2. **World-Building System**
   - User specifies focus areas (magic system, politics, geography, history, etc.)
   - Generates documentation for each area with internal consistency checks

3. **Character Framework**
   - Character profiles with background, motivation, fears
   - Character arcs (transformation across the story)
   - Relationship web and group dynamics

4. **Development Aids**
   - Pacing calculator (estimated word count per section)
   - Cliffhanger/hook checklist for chapters
   - Thematic consistency tracker

---

## Part 1: Three-Level Outline

### Level 1: Act Structure (Story Movements)

A medium-length webnovel (1M words) typically contains **3–5 acts**, each 150k–300k words:

- **Act I: Setup** (~150k–200k) — Introduce protagonist, world, inciting incident
- **Act II: Escalation** (~250k–300k) — Complications, new challenges, character growth
- **Act III: Climax** (~200k–250k) — Major confrontation, stakes at highest
- **Act IV (Optional): Resolution/Transition** (~150k–200k) — Fallout, new status quo, setup for continuation
- **Act V (Optional): Epilogue Arc** (~50k–100k) — Character integration, hint at future

**Your task:** Define 3–5 acts with:
- **Thematic focus** (what this act explores)
- **Key turning points** (2–3 major plot beats)
- **Estimated word range**
- **Tone shift** (if any)

---

### Level 2: Story Arcs (Arc Breakdown)

Within each act, create **3–5 story arcs** (mini-story structures lasting 1–2 chapters each or spread across multiple chapters):

Each arc contains:
- **Arc name** (e.g., "The First Dungeon," "Political Betrayal")
- **Inciting incident** (what starts this arc)
- **Midpoint** (complication or revelation)
- **Climax** (arc resolution or new question)
- **Estimated chapters** (e.g., chapters 5–12)
- **Emotional/thematic weight**

---

### Level 3: Chapter Outline

For each chapter:
- **Chapter number & title**
- **POV character** (if multi-POV)
- **Opening hook** (how does it grab the reader immediately?)
- **Key events** (3–5 bullet points of what happens)
- **Character development** (how does the protagonist or key characters change?)
- **New information/revelation** (world, plot, character)
- **Closing cliffhanger** (what question/tension ends the chapter?)
- **Estimated word count** (typically 3k–5k per chapter for webnovels)
- **Connection to arc** (which arc does this chapter belong to?)

## Part 2: World-Building System

### How to Specify Focus Areas

At the start, the user specifies which world-building elements matter most. Examples:

- **Magic System:** How magic works, limitations, costs, hierarchy of practitioners
- **Political Structure:** Governments, factions, power dynamics, conflicts
- **Geography & Architecture:** Maps, cities, landmarks, transportation, climate zones
- **History & Lore:** Past events, legends, civilizations, time periods
- **Social/Caste System:** Classes, hierarchy, cultural norms, taboos
- **Economy & Resources:** Currency, trade, scarcity, wealth distribution
- **Technology Level:** Medieval, renaissance, modern, futuristic, hybrid
- **Religion & Philosophy:** Belief systems, moral frameworks, spiritual practices
- **Ecology & Flora/Fauna:** Unique creatures, plants, environmental challenges

### For Each Focus Area, Generate:

1. **Core Rules** (2–3 fundamental laws governing this system)
2. **Scope & Limitations** (what this system can and cannot do)
3. **Consequences** (how it affects character decisions, plot, society)
4. **Unique Elements** (what makes this world-building distinctive/memorable?)
5. **Integration into Plot** (where does this world-building show up in the story?)

### Consistency Check

After world-building is drafted, verify:
- Do different systems conflict with each other?
- Are there plot holes (e.g., "why doesn't the protagonist just use X?")
- Are limitations clear and consistently enforced?

## Part 3: Character Framework

For each major character (protagonist, antagonist, key supporting cast):

### Character Profile

- **Name & Role**
- **Physical description** (age, appearance, distinctive traits)
- **Background** (where they come from, key childhood events)
- **Core motivation** (what do they want at the story's start?)
- **Core fear** (what are they most afraid of?)
- **Personality archetype** (The Hero, The Shadow, The Mentor, The Trickster, etc.)
- **Skills & Weaknesses** (what are they good/bad at?)
- **Relationship to protagonist** (ally, rival, love interest, obstacle, etc.)

### Character Arc

- **Act I state** (who they are at the start)
- **Midpoint turning point** (what fundamentally challenges their worldview?)
- **Act III transformation** (who they've become by climax)
- **Growth/change metric** (how will we *see* this change? What do they do differently?)

### Relationship Web

- Map out connections between characters
- For each relationship: **dynamic** (friendly? tense? unresolved?), **conflict**, **alliance**
- Identify which relationships drive plot (e.g., love triangle, betrayal, mentorship)

## Part 4: Pacing & Development Aids

### Word Count Calculator

Given the target length (1M words) and act structure:

```
Total: 1,000,000 words
Act I:   200,000 (20%)
Act II:  300,000 (30%)
Act III: 300,000 (30%)
Act IV:  150,000 (15%)
Act V:    50,000 (5%)

Chapters (assume 3.5k avg/chapter):
Act I:   ~57 chapters
Act II:  ~86 chapters
Act III: ~86 chapters
Act IV:  ~43 chapters
Act V:   ~14 chapters
TOTAL:   ~286 chapters
```

### Chapter Quality Checklist

For each chapter:
- [ ] Opening hook is immediate (action, dialogue, or revelation within first 100 words)
- [ ] At least one piece of new information (world, plot, or character)
- [ ] Protagonist or POV character makes a decision or learns something
- [ ] Closing cliffhanger is strong enough to make reader turn the page
- [ ] Pacing matches chapter type (action scenes faster, character moments slower)
- [ ] No info-dump (world-building is woven naturally)

## Tips for Maximum Effectiveness

1. **Be specific about genre/tone** — Outline structure changes for xuyên không, võ hiệp, cyberpunk, etc.
2. **Specify which world-building matters** — Don't ask for everything; focus on what drives your plot.
3. **Know your protagonist** — Even a rough sketch helps generate better arcs.
4. **Iterate freely** — Planning docs are for YOU to revise, not carved in stone.
5. **Use the chapter outline as a writing guide** — It should be detailed enough to draft from, but loose enough to surprise you.
6. **Check consistency** — Before drafting, verify that world-building rules support your plot, not undermine it.

## Output Philosophy

All output is **markdown, human-readable, and revision-friendly**. Structure is:
- Headers for easy scanning
- Bullet points for clarity
- Italics for emphasis
- Inline links for cross-references (where relevant)
- Sections can be copy-pasted, reordered, or deleted

The goal: a living planning document that grows with your project, not a rigid template.
