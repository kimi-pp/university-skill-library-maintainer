# DTRI Trade Policy: Policies, Agreements & Cybersecurity

## Overview

This skill covers three interconnected pillars that form the foundation for digital trade readiness:

- **Pillar 1: Digital Trade Policies** - National strategies and regulatory frameworks
- **Pillar 2: Digital Trade Agreements** - Regional and bilateral commitments
- **Pillar 4: Cybersecurity** - Protection frameworks for digital transactions

These pillars establish the policy environment that enables safe and efficient cross-border digital commerce.

---

## Pillar 1: Digital Trade Policies

### What It Measures

The presence and quality of national digital trade strategies, e-commerce policies, and regulatory frameworks that govern digital commerce.

### Key Indicators

| Indicator | Description | Weight |
|-----------|-------------|--------|
| Digital Trade Strategy | Existence of comprehensive national digital trade strategy | 30% |
| E-commerce Policy | Framework for online business regulation | 25% |
| Digital Economy Policy | Broader digital transformation framework | 20% |
| Cross-border Data Flow Policy | Regulations on data localization and transfers | 15% |
| Digital Trade Facilitation | Customs and border procedures for digital goods | 10% |

### Scoring Criteria

| Score Level | Criteria |
|-------------|----------|
| 81-100 | Comprehensive strategy with implementation roadmap, dedicated agency, regular review |
| 61-80 | Strategy exists with partial implementation, clear timeline |
| 41-60 | Policy documents exist but lack integration or implementation plans |
| 21-40 | Fragmented policies across different ministries |
| 0-20 | No formal digital trade policy framework |

### Best Practices

1. **Integrated Approach**: Digital trade strategy aligned with broader economic development plans
2. **Multi-stakeholder Governance**: Involvement of private sector, academia, and civil society
3. **Evidence-based Design**: Policies grounded in data and impact assessments
4. **Regular Updates**: Mechanisms for periodic review and adaptation
5. **Coordinated Implementation**: Clear assignment of responsibilities across agencies

### Regional Examples

**High Performers:**
- Singapore: Digital Economy Framework for Action (2023) with clear KPIs
- Republic of Korea: Digital New Deal with cross-ministry coordination

**Recent Adopters:**
- Indonesia: 2021-2024 Digital Economy Roadmap
- Vietnam: Digital Economy Development Strategy to 2025

## Pillar 2: Digital Trade Agreements

### What It Measures

The extent to which economies have committed to digital trade rules through regional and bilateral agreements, and the quality of those commitments.

### Key Indicators

| Indicator | Description | Weight |
|-----------|-------------|--------|
| Digital Trade Agreement Coverage | Number of agreements with digital chapters | 35% |
| Agreement Quality | Depth of commitments on key digital trade issues | 30% |
| Regional Integration | Participation in major regional digital trade initiatives | 20% |
| WTO E-commerce Negotiations | Active participation in WTO Joint Statement Initiative | 15% |

### Major Digital Trade Agreements in Asia-Pacific

| Agreement | Members | Digital Chapter Status |
|-----------|---------|----------------------|
| CPTPP | 12 (incl. Japan, Singapore, Vietnam) | Comprehensive e-commerce chapter |
| RCEP | 15 (incl. China, ASEAN, ROK) | Limited digital provisions |
| DEPA | 4 (Singapore, Chile, NZ, ROK) | First standalone digital trade agreement |
| CETA | EU-Canada | Comprehensive digital trade chapter |
| AANZFTA | ASEAN-Australia-NZ | Limited digital provisions |

### Scoring Criteria

| Score Level | Criteria |
|-------------|----------|
| 81-100 | 5+ agreements with comprehensive digital chapters, active in WTO negotiations |
| 61-80 | 3-4 agreements with digital provisions, WTO participation |
| 41-60 | 1-2 agreements with digital provisions, limited WTO engagement |
| 21-40 | At least one agreement with minimal digital provisions |
| 0-20 | No agreements with digital trade provisions |

### Key Digital Trade Provisions

1. **Tariffs on Digital Products**
   - Commitments to maintain zero duties on electronic transmissions
   - Exclusions for digital content (audiovisual, broadcasting)

2. **Cross-border Data Flows**
   - Right to transfer data across borders
   - Exceptions for public policy, security, privacy protection
   - Prohibitions on data localization requirements

3. **Local Server Requirements**
   - Restrictions on computing facilities location
   - Technology transfer requirements

4. **Source Code Protection**
   - Prohibition on mandatory disclosure of source code
   - Exceptions for critical infrastructure and security software

5. **Digital Authentication**
   - Recognition of electronic signatures
   - Mutual recognition of digital certificates

6. **Consumer Protection**
   - Online consumer rights
   - Dispute resolution mechanisms
   - Fraud protection measures

### Policy Analysis Framework

When analyzing an economy's digital trade agreement portfolio:

1. **Coverage Assessment**: Count agreements with digital provisions
2. **Depth Analysis**: Evaluate specificity and enforceability of commitments
3. **Consistency Check**: Identify conflicting obligations across agreements
4. **Implementation Gap**: Assess domestic alignment with international commitments
5. **Negotiation Priorities**: Understand what the economy seeks in future agreements

## Pillar 4: Cybersecurity

### What It Measures

The strength of cybersecurity frameworks, institutions, and capabilities that protect digital trade transactions and infrastructure.

### Key Indicators

| Indicator | Description | Weight |
|-----------|-------------|--------|
| Cybersecurity Strategy | National cybersecurity policy framework | 25% |
| Legal Framework | Cybercrime legislation and regulations | 25% |
| Institutional Capacity | CERT/CIRT existence and capabilities | 20% |
| International Cooperation | Participation in cyber security initiatives | 15% |
| Private Sector Engagement | Public-private partnership on cyber security | 15% |

### Key Components

#### 1. National Cybersecurity Strategy

Elements of a comprehensive strategy:
- Vision, mission, and strategic objectives
- Risk assessment and threat landscape analysis
- Critical infrastructure identification and protection measures
- Incident response and recovery procedures
- Cybersecurity workforce development
- International cooperation framework

#### 2. Legal Framework

Essential legislation areas:
- Cybercrime definitions and penalties
- Data breach notification requirements
- Electronic evidence admissibility
- Jurisdictional rules for cyber offenses
- International cooperation on cyber investigations

#### 3. CERT/CIRT Structure

| CERT Type | Mandate | Typical Coverage |
|-----------|---------|------------------|
| National CERT | Country-wide coordination | All sectors |
| Sector CERT | Industry-specific focus | Finance, energy, etc. |
| Government CERT | Public sector protection | Government agencies |
| Academic CERT | Research and education | Universities |

#### 4. International Cooperation

Key initiatives:
- ITU Global Cybersecurity Agenda
- ASEAN-Japan Cybersecurity Capacity Building Centre
- APEC Cybersecurity Working Group
- Commonwealth Cybercrime Initiative

### Scoring Criteria

| Score Level | Criteria |
|-------------|----------|
| 81-100 | Comprehensive strategy, modern legislation, active CERT, multiple international partnerships |
| 61-80 | Strategy exists, legislation adequate, CERT operational, some international engagement |
| 41-60 | Basic strategy, partial legislation, CERT established but limited capacity |
| 21-40 | Draft strategy, limited legislation, no dedicated CERT |
| 0-20 | No formal cybersecurity framework |

### Regional Cybersecurity Initiatives

| Initiative | Members | Focus Area |
|------------|---------|------------|
| ASEAN CERT | 10 ASEAN members | Information sharing, capacity building |
| APCERT | 22 economies | Incident response coordination |
| APECTEL | 21 economies | Telecom security standards |
| FIRST | Global | CERT cooperation platform |

### Analysis Considerations

When evaluating cybersecurity readiness for digital trade:

1. **Trust vs. Security Balance**: Excessive restrictions can impede legitimate digital trade
2. **Resource Constraints**: Many LDCs lack capacity for comprehensive cybersecurity
3. **Private Sector Role**: Critical infrastructure often privately owned
4. **Cross-border Incidents**: Need for international cooperation mechanisms
5. **Emerging Threats**: AI-powered attacks, quantum computing risks

## Inter-pillar Connections

These three pillars are closely linked:

1. **Pillar 1 → Pillar 2**: National policies inform international negotiation positions
2. **Pillar 1 → Pillar 4**: Cybersecurity strategy is part of broader digital policy
3. **Pillar 2 → Pillar 4**: Agreements often include cybersecurity cooperation provisions

### Policy Coordination Checklist

- [ ] Digital trade strategy references cybersecurity requirements
- [ ] Trade agreement commitments align with cybersecurity legislation
- [ ] CERT/CIRT involved in digital trade policy development
- [ ] Cross-border data flow policies account for security considerations
- [ ] Trade ministries coordinate with cybersecurity agencies

## Methodology Notes

### Data Sources

- **Policy Documents**: Official government strategies and legislation
- **Treaty Databases**: WTO, UNCTAD, and government treaty collections
- **Global Cybersecurity Index**: ITU biennial assessment
- **CERT Surveys**: APCERT and FIRST membership data
- **Expert Assessments**: Regional expert panel evaluations

### Scoring Challenges

1. **Policy Quality vs. Existence**: Having a policy doesn't mean it's effective
2. **Implementation Gap**: Formal commitments may not translate to practice
3. **Rapid Change**: Cyber threats evolve faster than policy cycles
4. **Subjective Assessment**: Agreement quality involves qualitative judgment

## Analysis Guidance

### Identifying Policy Gaps

Use this framework to identify gaps in digital trade policy readiness:

1. **Policy Inventory**: List all relevant policies and agreements
2. **Coverage Assessment**: Check against RDTII indicator list
3. **Quality Evaluation**: Assess depth and enforceability
4. **Implementation Review**: Examine actual practice vs. stated policy
5. **Gap Analysis**: Compare with regional best practices

### Priority Recommendations

Based on RDTII scoring patterns:

| Score Level | Priority Actions |
|-------------|------------------|
| 0-40 | Develop basic digital trade and cybersecurity strategies |
| 41-60 | Strengthen implementation, build CERT capacity |
| 61-80 | Deepen international engagement, align agreements |
| 81-100 | Share best practices, lead regional initiatives |

### Common Improvement Pathways

1. **Starting Point**: Draft comprehensive digital trade strategy
2. **Quick Wins**: Join regional cyber security initiatives
3. **Medium-term**: Negotiate first digital trade agreement
4. **Long-term**: Establish regional leadership in digital norms
