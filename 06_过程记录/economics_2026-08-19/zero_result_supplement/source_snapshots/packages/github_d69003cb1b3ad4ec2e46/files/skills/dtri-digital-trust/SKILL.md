# DTRI Digital Trust: Data, Identity & Consumer Protection

## Overview

Digital trust is the foundation of successful digital trade. This skill covers three critical pillars that establish confidence in digital ecosystems:

- **Pillar 5: Data Governance** - Legal frameworks for data protection and cross-border flows
- **Pillar 6: Digital Identity** - Digital identification systems and authentication mechanisms
- **Pillar 7: Consumer Protection** - Legal frameworks protecting online consumers

These pillars collectively create the trust environment that enables businesses and consumers to participate confidently in digital commerce.

---

## Pillar 5: Data Governance

### What It Measures

The existence and quality of legal and regulatory frameworks governing data protection, privacy, and cross-border data flows.

### Key Indicators

| Indicator | Description | Weight |
|-----------|-------------|--------|
| Data Protection Law | Existence and comprehensiveness of data protection legislation | 30% |
| Data Protection Authority | Independence and capacity of regulatory body | 25% |
| Cross-border Data Flow Rules | Framework for international data transfers | 20% |
| Data Localization Requirements | Restrictions on where data can be stored | 15% |
| Industry-Specific Regulations | Sector-specific data rules (health, finance) | 10% |

### Data Protection Frameworks in Asia-Pacific

#### Comprehensive Data Protection Laws

| Economy | Law | Year | Key Features |
|---------|-----|------|--------------|
| Singapore | PDPA | 2012 (2020 rev) | Consent-based, limited extraterritoriality |
| Japan | APPI | 2003 (2017 rev) | Independent authority, adequacy decisions |
| Republic of Korea | PIPA | 2011 (2020 rev) | Strong individual rights, breach notification |
| Philippines | DPA | 2012 | Comprehensive privacy framework |
| Thailand | PDPA | 2019 | GDPR-inspired, breach notification |
| Indonesia | PDP Law | 2022 | GDPR-inspired, data localization for public data |
| Vietnam | Decree 13 | 2023 | Data localization requirements |
| China | PIPL | 2021 | Strict localization, cross-border assessments |
| India | DPDP Act | 2023 | Consent-based, data fiduciary model |
| Malaysia | PDPA | 2010 | Limited scope (commercial sector only) |

#### Emerging Frameworks

|---------|--------|-------------------|
| Bangladesh | Draft under consideration | 2025-2026 |
| Pakistan | Draft approved, awaiting implementation | 2025 |
| Sri Lanka | Data Protection Bill passed | 2025 implementation |
| Nepal | Draft legislation | 2025-2026 |
| Lao PDR | Draft under consideration | 2026+ |
| Cambodia | No comprehensive law yet | Uncertain |

### Cross-border Data Flow Frameworks

| Framework | Type | Participants | Status |
|-----------|------|--------------|--------|
| APEC CBPR | Voluntary certification | APEC economies | Operational, 5 certified |
| GDPR Adequacy | EU recognition | Japan, South Korea, UK | 3 countries in region |
| ASEAN Framework | Regional guidelines | ASEAN-10 | Guidelines adopted |
| RCEP Chapter | Trade agreement commitments | 15 members | Limited provisions |

### Data Localization Landscape

| Localization Level | Economies | Key Requirements |
|--------------------|-----------|------------------|
| Strict | China, Vietnam, Indonesia | Data must be stored domestically |
| Moderate | India, Russia | Sensitive data domestically stored |
| Limited | Malaysia, Thailand | Specific sectors require localization |
| None | Singapore, Japan, ROK, Australia, NZ | No general localization |

### Scoring Criteria

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 81-100 | Comprehensive data protection law, independent authority, liberal cross-border flows |
| 61-80 | Data protection law exists, authority operational, some cross-border restrictions |
| 41-60 | Draft or limited data protection law, authority developing, significant restrictions |
| 21-40 | Sector-specific rules only, no dedicated authority, heavy restrictions |
| 0-20 | No data protection framework |

### Data Governance Models

#### Model 1: Liberal with Strong Protection (Singapore, Japan, ROK)
- Strong individual rights
- Independent regulator
- Free cross-border flows with accountability
- Industry self-regulation encouraged

#### Model 2: Protective with Localization (China, Vietnam)
- Strict data localization
- Government access to data
- Limited cross-border transfers
- State-led data governance

#### Model 3: Hybrid/Developing (Indonesia, India)
- Emerging comprehensive frameworks
- Some localization requirements
- Cross-border assessments required
- Capacity building ongoing

### Key Concepts

#### Data Subject Rights

1. **Right to Access** - Know what data is collected
2. **Right to Rectification** - Correct inaccurate data
3. **Right to Erasure** - Request deletion of data
4. **Right to Portability** - Transfer data between services
5. **Right to Object** - Opt out of certain processing
6. **Right to Be Informed** - Transparency about data use

#### Legal Bases for Processing

1. **Consent** - Explicit, informed, freely given
2. **Contract** - Necessary for contract performance
3. **Legal Obligation** - Required by law
4. **Vital Interests** - Protect life/safety
5. **Public Task** - Official authority function
6. **Legitimate Interests** - Reasonable business need

#### Cross-border Transfer Mechanisms

1. **Adequacy Decision** - Destination deemed to provide equivalent protection
2. **Standard Contractual Clauses** - Model contracts approved by regulator
3. **Binding Corporate Rules** - Internal corporate data transfer rules
4. **Certification Schemes** - APEC CBPR, other approved certifications
5. **Deemed Adequate** - Specific situations deemed acceptable

## Pillar 6: Digital Identity

### What It Measures

The existence and maturity of digital identification systems that enable secure online authentication and verification.

### Key Indicators

| Indicator | Description | Weight |
|-----------|-------------|--------|
| Digital ID System | Existence of national digital ID program | 30% |
| ID Coverage | Percentage of population with digital ID | 25% |
| Authentication Standards | Framework for electronic authentication | 20% |
| Interoperability | Cross-border recognition of digital IDs | 15% |
| Private Sector Use | Extent of private sector adoption | 10% |

### Digital ID Models in Asia-Pacific

#### Model 1: Centralized National ID System

| Economy | System | Coverage | Key Features |
|---------|--------|----------|--------------|
| India | Aadhaar | 93% (1.3B people) | Biometric-based, linked to services |
| Indonesia | e-KTP | 80% | Chip-based smart card, biometrics |
| China | Resident Identity Card | 90%+ | Integrated with mobile apps |
| Bangladesh | NID | 70% | Moving toward digital ecosystem |
| Pakistan | CNIC | 70% | Moving to digital authentication |
| Vietnam | National ID | 60%+ | Digital integration ongoing |

#### Model 2: Federated/Decentralized System

| Economy | System | Coverage | Key Features |
|---------|--------|----------|--------------|
| Singapore | SingPass | 95% | Multi-factor, extensive service integration |
| Republic of Korea | i-PIN | 85% | Multiple certifying authorities |
| Estonia | e-Residency | Global | Blockchain-based, cross-border |

#### Model 3: Bank-Led Financial Identity

| Economy | System | Coverage | Key Features |
|---------|--------|----------|--------------|
| Thailand | PromptPay | 50%+ | Mobile number-based, linked to bank accounts |
| Philippines | InstaPay | 30%+ | Bank-led, mobile-first |
| Malaysia | MyKad | 85% | Smart card, integrated with banking |

#### Model 4: Mobile Operator-Led

| Economy | System | Coverage | Key Features |
|---------|--------|----------|--------------|
| Pakistan | JazzCash/Easypaisa | 20%+ | SIM-based authentication |

|------------|-------|--------|

| ID4Africa | Cross-border cooperation | Multiple pilot projects |

### Scoring Criteria

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 81-100 | Universal digital ID, strong authentication, cross-border recognition |
| 61-80 | Broad coverage, authentication standards, some interoperability |
| 41-60 | Basic digital ID system, limited coverage, developing standards |
| 21-40 | Pilot digital ID programs, low coverage, no standards |
| 0-20 | No digital ID system |

### Digital ID Design Principles

1. **Inclusivity** - Accessible to all citizens including marginalized groups
2. **User Control** - Individuals control their identity data
3. **Privacy by Design** - Minimal data collection, purpose limitation
4. **Security** - Protection against fraud and unauthorized access
5. **Interoperability** - Works across sectors and borders
6. **Usability** - Easy to use for all skill levels

## Pillar 7: Consumer Protection

### What It Measures

The legal and regulatory frameworks that protect consumers in online transactions, including e-commerce, digital payments, and data privacy.

### Key Indicators

| Indicator | Description | Weight |
|-----------|-------------|--------|
| E-commerce Law | Legal framework for online transactions | 30% |
| Consumer Rights Framework | Online consumer protections | 25% |
| Dispute Resolution | Online dispute resolution mechanisms | 20% |
| Redress Mechanisms | Compensation and refund processes | 15% |
| Enforcement | Capacity to enforce consumer protections | 10% |

### Consumer Protection Frameworks

| Economy | E-commerce Law | ODR Platform | Redress Mechanism |
|---------|----------------|--------------|-------------------|
| Singapore | ECTA 1998 | SCT (Community Disputes) | Casetrust accreditation |
| ROK | E-commerce Act | KCP Fair Trade Commission | Consumer Agency mediation |
| Japan | E-commerce Guidelines | JCCU mediation | Consumer Affairs Agency |
| Australia | ACL (applies online) | ACCC, AFCA | ASIC enforcement |
| China | E-commerce Law 2019 | Platform-internal mechanisms | CAC, SAMR enforcement |
| India | Consumer Protection Act 2019 | NCDRC, State commissions | CCPA authority |
| Thailand | E-commerce Protection Act | OCPB mediation | Consumer Protection Board |
| Indonesia | E-commerce Regulation 2020 | Consumer dispute body | BPOM enforcement |
| Philippines | E-Commerce Act 2000 | DTI dispute resolution | Consumer protection group |
| Malaysia | CP Act 1999 | KPDNHEP tribunal | Consumer claims tribunal |

### Key Consumer Rights in E-commerce

1. **Right to Information** - Clear product/service descriptions
2. **Right to Choice** - Ability to compare and select
3. **Right to Safety** - Protection from harmful products/services
4. **Right to Redress** - Complaint handling and refunds
5. **Right to Education** - Consumer awareness programs
6. **Right to Representation** - Voice in policy-making

### Common E-commerce Issues

| Issue | Regulatory Response |
|-------|---------------------|
| False/misleading advertising | Truth in advertising requirements |
| Non-delivery of goods | Delivery guarantee, refund policies |
| Defective products | Warranty, return policies |
| Unauthorized charges | Payment protection, chargeback rights |
| Data breaches | Breach notification, compensation |
| Platform liability | Platform responsibility for third-party sellers |
| Cross-border disputes | International cooperation, mutual recognition |

### Online Dispute Resolution (ODR)

| ODR Type | Description | Examples |
|-----------|-------------|----------|
| Platform Internal | Marketplace dispute processes | Amazon A-to-Z, eBay Money Back Guarantee |
| Government-Run | State-administered resolution | Singapore SCT, China 12315 |
| Industry Association | Sector-specific resolution | Banking ombudsman, telecom disputes |
| Third-party Services | Private ODR providers | Modria, SmartSettle |
| Cross-border | International frameworks | UNCITRAL ODR rules |

### Scoring Criteria

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 81-100 | Comprehensive e-commerce law, effective ODR, strong enforcement |
| 61-80 | E-commerce law exists, ODR operational, moderate enforcement |
| 41-60 | Basic consumer protections, limited ODR, weak enforcement |
| 21-40 | Fragmented protections, no formal ODR |
| 0-20 | No e-commerce consumer protections |

## Inter-pillar Connections

### Data Governance ↔ Digital Identity

- Digital identity systems require strong data protection
- Data protection laws must address ID-specific privacy issues
- Consent management critical for ID systems

### Data Governance ↔ Consumer Protection

- Data breaches are consumer protection issues
- Privacy is a consumer right
- Transparency requirements overlap

### Digital Identity ↔ Consumer Protection

- Identity fraud is a consumer harm
- Strong ID systems reduce fraud
- User control over identity data is a protection

## Policy Recommendations

### For Low-Scoring Economies (0-40)

1. **Data Governance**
   - Draft comprehensive data protection law
   - Establish data protection authority
   - Join regional data protection networks

2. **Digital Identity**
   - Develop national digital ID strategy
   - Start with civil registry digitization
   - Pilot mobile-based authentication

3. **Consumer Protection**
   - Extend consumer laws to e-commerce
   - Establish online complaint mechanisms
   - Join cross-border enforcement networks

### For Medium-Scoring Economies (41-60)

1. **Data Governance**
   - Strengthen enforcement capacity
   - Develop cross-border transfer guidelines
   - Implement breach notification requirements

2. **Digital Identity**
   - Expand digital ID coverage
   - Develop authentication standards
   - Enable private sector use cases

3. **Consumer Protection**
   - Enhance dispute resolution mechanisms
   - Strengthen platform liability rules
   - Improve cross-border cooperation

### For High-Scoring Economies (61+)

1. **Data Governance**
   - Lead regional harmonization efforts
   - Develop interoperability frameworks
   - Share best practices

2. **Digital Identity**
   - Enable cross-border recognition
   - Explore decentralized identity solutions
   - Support regional ID initiatives

3. **Consumer Protection**
   - Develop innovative ODR solutions
   - Strengthen international cooperation
   - Share enforcement resources
